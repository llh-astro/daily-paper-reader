Abstract 

We consider discrete-time non-linear stochastic dynamical systems where the driving noise is not i.i.d. but an autoregressive process (“red noise”). Under a standard dissipativity condition, we prove geometrically fast convergence to a stationary distribution in total variation. 

# 1 Introduction 

We consider a pair of processes (Yn, X n) ∈ Rd × Rd, n ∈ N. The first is an autore-gressive process 

Yn+1 − Yn = −hAY n + εn+1 , (1) where εn, n ∈ N is an i.i.d. sequence in Rd, h > 0 and A is a matrix. Yn, n ≥ 1

will represent the “red noise” driving our system. The second process is given by the recursion 

Xn+1 − Xn = hμ (Xn) + σ(Xn)Yn+1 , (2) where μ : Rd → Rd, σ : Rd → Rd×d are measurable functions. We assume through-out this paper that (Y0, X 0) are independent of (εn)n≥1.

Example 1. Consider the stochastic differential equations 

d ˆYt = −A ˆYt dt + dL t, (3) 

d ˆXt = μ( ˆXt) dt + σ( ˆXt)d ˆYt, (4) where Lt is a L´ evy-process. ˆYt is a specimen of the often-studied Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes, see [1]. In this setting, (Yn, X n) corresponds to the Euler-discretization with step size h for (3) and (4). However, (Yn, X n) is not necessarily of this form. We only assume that, observed along a given time grid, the system obeys the dynamics (2) with an autoregressive noise (1). In particular, the law of εn need not be infinitely divisible.

1arXiv:2608.09589v1 [math.PR] 10 Aug 2026 Stochastic dynamical systems like (2) appear frequently in the literature with a stationary driving noise Yn. Such Xn are called a stochastically recursive sequence ,thoroughly studied in [2] but only under some contractivity assumption on μ.In our setting, Yn is not necessarily stationary, but it is an autoregressive (hence Markovian) process, called red noise . This is a particularly often studied case, moti-vated by applications from physics and economics, see [3, 4]. More recently, interest in these models has intensified [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. When μ is not contractive, only dissipative (see Assumption 6 below), the tech-niques of [2] do not apply and literature becomes scarce. [10] studied stochastic differ-ential equations driven by fractional Brownian motion, introducing deep techniques. The methodology of [10] was translated to a discrete-time context by [11]. The main focus of the latter paper is on stationary and Gaussian Yn whose covariance function may decay slowly (that is, at a power rate, such as the increments of fractional Brow-nian motion). Such Yn fail the Markov property and intricate arguments are needed to establish convergence of Xn to a limiting law. Subsection 2.6 of [11] also treats a special case of our setting: when εn are standard Gaussian, A is constant times the identity, Yn is stationary, μ and σ satisfy continuity hypotheses, [11] establishes convergence of Xn to a limiting law at a rate O(n−p),for all p > 0.The current paper offers essential improvements with respect to [11]: non-Gaussian 

εn are allowed; our conditions on μ, σ are weaker; a more general autoregressive dy-namics is considered; Yn need not be stationary, and, most importantly, we prove optimal, exponential convergence rate of the system to its invariant distribution. We stress that the techniques of [11] apply in a much more general context than ours. Our contribution consists of proving sharper results in the specific case where Yn is an autoregressive process. Note that, in our setting, Xn fails the Markov property but the pair (Xn, Y n)

is Markovian. The textbook approach (see e.g. [12]) is verifying ψ-irreducibility and aperiodicity of (Xn, Y n) and then showing that the “Foster-Lyapunov drift condition” and “minorization on a small set” hold. Although the system (1), (2) satisfy a drift condition (see Lemma 10 below), aperiodicity and minorization seem laborious to verify. For this reason we construct the necessary couplings directly, using a slightly unusual approach. Section 2 states our assumptions and main result, Section 3 provides the proofs. Section 4 is about future research directions.

Open questions of varying generality remain. One may look at Yn which has the dynamics of a discretized ergodic diffusion. Our arguments carry over to this case, too, with suitable modifications. It would be desirable to formulate abstract ergodicity conditions on a general state space for dynamical systems driven by a Markovian process. By such tools one could study certain specific models, e.g. the waiting time in queuing models with dependent service and/or interarrival times, see e.g. [17]. These extensions are also left for future research.
