ABSTRACT 

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) embed PDE residuals into neural net-work training, but their internal representations remain opaque: it is unknown what physical features their hidden layers encode or whether those features have a local-ized causal role. We present P HYS SAE, a mechanistic interpretability framework that trains overcomplete sparse autoencoders (SAEs) on PINN penultimate-layer activations and evaluates dictionary atoms through direct causal intervention in the original frozen hidden state : hcf = h − αz kdk, bypassing the SAE decoder entirely. Across six PDE families, with 3 PINN seeds and 3 SAE seeds each—we show that (i) Our discovered SAE atoms align with independently-defined physical observables (max Pearson |r| = 0 .951 , always ≫ permutation null), (ii) the causal footprint of top-aligned atom ablation is 1.2–4.2 × more spatially concentrated canonical than PCA or ICA interventions, and (iii) top-aligned atoms outperform matched random controls on causal localization for structured physical concepts (ESF 80 advantage 0.04–0.44). Two-atom bilateral representations improve concept regression R 2 by ∆R2 = 0 .05 –0.15 over single atoms, while random pairs decrease it by up to 0.60. These results demonstrate that PINNs develop sparse, physically structured latent representations that can be identified and causally interrogated post-hoc, opening a path toward interpretability-aware scientific machine learning. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (Raissi et al., 2019) encode the residual of a PDE directly into the training loss via automatic differentiation, enabling mesh-free solution of forward and inverse prob-lems. Despite widespread adoption in fluid mechanics (Cai et al., 2021), solid mechanics (Haghighat et al., 2021), and biomedical modelling (Sahli Costabal et al., 2020), PINNs remain black boxes at the feature level. Their failure modes—spectral bias (Wang et al., 2022), gradient pathologies (Wang et al., 2021a), causality violations (Wang et al., 2024a)—are well-characterised at the loss or gradient level, but no work has asked what a trained PINN actually represents in its hidden layers. Recent mechanistic interpretability work has developed tools for extracting human-readable features from activations: linear probes (Alain & Bengio, 2017), concept activation vectors (Kim et al., 2018), and overcomplete sparse autoencoders (SAEs) (Bricken et al., 2023; Cunningham et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024). SAEs have now migrated to scientific ML, appearing on graph CFD surrogates (Hu & Liu, 2025) and neural operators (Tolooshams et al., 2025), and concept steering has been applied to physics foundation models (Fear et al., 2025). A direct inspection of PINN weight distributions by Tucny et al. (2025) found that weights “resemble Gaussian random matrices with no evident trace of the physical principles,” motivating feature-level (rather than weight-level) interpretability. PHYS SAE bridges mechanistic interpretability and scientific ML for residual-loss PINNs. Its core design choices address three challenges unique to the PINN setting: (1) concepts must be defined

2 METHOD 

PHYS SAE is a post-hoc mechanistic interpretability framework that operates entirely on a trained, frozen PINN. No retraining or architectural modification is required. The pipeline has three stages: (1) extract the PINN’s penultimate-layer activations on a dense spatio-temporal grid; (2) train a sparse autoencoder on those activations to discover a dictionary of physical atoms; (3) evaluate atoms by directly intervening in the original PINN hidden state and measuring the resulting change in the PINN output field. We describe each stage in detail below. 2.1 PROBLEM SETTING AND NOTATION 

Let Ω ⊂ Rd be a spatial domain and [0 , T ] a time interval. We consider scalar PDEs of the form 

F[u]( x, t ) = 0 on Ω × (0 , T ], (1) subject to an initial condition u(x, 0) = u0(x) and boundary condition B[u]( x, t ) = 0 on ∂Ω × [0 , T ].We write uθ : Ω × [0 , T ] → Rdout for the PINN approximation, where dout = 1 for real-valued PDEs and dout = 2 for the complex-valued Schr¨ odinger equation (split into real and imaginary parts). The PINN is a fully-connected multilayer perceptron (MLP) with L hidden layers of width dh, smooth activations, and a linear readout: 

uθ (x, t ) = Wout hL(x, t ) + bout , hℓ = σ(Wℓhℓ−1 + bℓ), h0 = [ x, t ]⊤. (2) 2Preprint—P HYS SAE: Mechanistic Interpretability with Sparse Autoencoders The key quantity for interpretability is the penultimate post-activation h ≡ hL ∈ Rdh , which we refer to throughout as the hidden state or latent representation . The output is a linear function of h,so ∆u = Wout ∆h: any change to h propagates linearly to the output, making the readout a natural causal bottleneck. 

Why the penultimate layer? The penultimate layer is the richest representation before the in-formation is projected to a scalar by the linear readout. Interpretability at this layer is therefore interpretability of the last nonlinear computation the network performs before producing its predic-tion. Earlier layers encode more distributed, less semantically organised information; the output layer is only a linear combination and carries no additional structure. 2.2 PINN T RAINING

A central design principle of P HYS SAE is that concept fields must be derived from sources indepen-dent of the PINN being interpreted. The PINN output is uθ = Wout h + bout , a linear function of h.Any concept field derived from uθ would therefore correlate with h trivially, independent of whether the PINN has learned meaningful physics. We therefore use: • Analytic reference solutions for PDEs where closed-form solutions exist (heat equation via eigenfunction expansion, linear convection via the d’Alembert formula, and the Schr¨ odinger soliton via the exact Zakharov-Shabat formula). • High-resolution numerical reference solutions for nonlinear PDEs (Burgers and Allen– Cahn): we run a pseudo-spectral RK4 solver on a fine grid ( nx = 512 ) and interpolate onto the evaluation grid. These reference solutions are computed once, cached to disk, and reused across all PINN seeds and SAE runs.

Observational alignment (§2.6) establishes that an atom’s activation pattern correlates with a physical concept, but correlation does not imply that the atom causally controls the corresponding feature in the model’s output. We therefore perform a causal intervention directly in the original PINN’s frozen hidden state, bypassing the SAE decoder entirely. 

Intervention formulation. Given atom k with unit-norm decoder direction dk ∈ Rdh and activation 

zk(x, t ) ∈ R (the SAE code at each grid point), the contribution of atom k to the original hidden state at (x, t ) is: contribution k(x, t ) = zk(x, t ) · dk ∈ Rdh . (17) The counterfactual hidden state obtained by ablating atom k at strength α is: 

hcf (x, t ; α) = h(x, t ) − α z k(x, t ) dk. (18) At α = 1 this exactly removes the atom’s contribution from h; at α ∈ (0 , 1) it partially suppresses it; at α > 1 it over-removes (amplifies the absence). We evaluate at α ∈ { 0.25 , 0.5, 1.0, 1.5} for dose-response analysis. The counterfactual output is then: 

ucf (x, t ; α) = Wout hcf (x, t ; α) + bout , (19) and the causal effect of the intervention is: 

∆u(x, t ; α) = uθ (x, t ) − ucf (x, t ; α) = α z k(x, t ) ( Wout dk). (20) This last equality shows that ∆u is linear in α and linear in zk: the causal effect of ablating atom k is exactly the product of its activation strength and its readout projection Wout dk ∈ Rdout . The SAE decoder plays no role whatsoever in this formula: the intervention operates entirely in the original PINN’s hidden-state geometry. 

Why this is stronger than SAE-internal ablation. A simpler approach would be to ablate within the SAE: set zk ← 0, decode ˆh = Wdec z, and measure ∆u = Wout (h − ˆh). This does not test whether atom k has a causal role in the original PINN ; it only tests whether removing the atom’s contribution to the SAE reconstruction changes the output. The difference matters because the SAE is a lossy approximation of h (reconstruction MSE is nonzero), and h − ˆh is partly the reconstruction error, not just atom k’s contribution. Terminology. We use alignment (= correlation/association), functional relevance (= intervention changes output), and causal evidence (= direct hidden-state intervention changes output in a structured way). We do not claim atoms are physical variables, nor that SAE “discovers the true physics.” We claim atoms are aligned with physical concepts and have a localized functional role .

On feature non-uniqueness. Low cross-seed cosine ( ≈0.35) reflects the non-uniqueness of sparse coding. 6 CONCLUSION 

We presented P HYS SAE, the first mechanistic interpretability framework for residual-loss PINNs. By training sparse autoencoders on PINN hidden activations and intervening directly in the original hidden state, we showed that PINNs develop sparse, physically structured latent representations. SAE achieves 1.2–4.2 × more concentrated causal footprints than PCA and ICA across six PDE families; top-aligned atoms beat matched random controls on localized concepts; two-atom bilateral representations consistently outperform single atoms.
