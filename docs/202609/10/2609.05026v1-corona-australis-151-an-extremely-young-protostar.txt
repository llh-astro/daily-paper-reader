ABSTRACT 

Context. Prestellar cores are the birthplace of stars and planetary systems, but they are short-lived objects, since the initial stages of dense core evolution, collapse, and the formation of a protostellar seed are fast. In an e ff ort to build a catalogue of bona-fide prestellar cores in the Solar neighbourhood, we used APEX observations to identify dynamically evolved cores among dense cores observed with Herschel . One of them, Corona Australis 151, stood out because of its centrally peaked structure, with densities above 10 7 cm −3

in the central 500 − 1000 au, and high deuteration levels (N 2D+/N2H+ ∼ 0.5), suggestive of an evolved, prestellar stage. 

Aims. Corona Australis 151 appears to be an evolved prestellar core, but the presence of broad wings in some of the detected lines and a tentative 70 μm detection with Herschel partially challenges its prestellar stage. We intend to assess its evolutionary stage. 

Methods. We analyse new ALMA data of the continuum emission and of several line tracers (including typical outflow tracers) at a resolution of ∼ 150 − 200 au, tracing the envelope. 

Results. We unveil the presence of a compact and young outflow (projected dynamical age: ∼ 500 yr), traced by SiO, CO, and H 2CO emission. The continuum emission traces an envelope structure of size ∼ 1000 au and mass 0 .33 M⊙ (assuming Tdust = 20 K). 

Conclusions. Corona Australis 151 is an extremely young protostar, possibly one of the youngest known in the Solar neighbourhood, and hence a new laboratory to study the chemical and dynamical evolution at the dawn of star formation. 

Key words. ISM: clouds — ISM: molecules — Stars: formation — ISM: individual objects: Corona Australis 151 

1. Introduction 

Prestellar cores are gravitationally bound starless cores on the verge of gravitational collapse. They contain all the ingredients to form stars and planetary systems, so their study provides criti-cal information on the initial conditions of the assembly of stellar systems like our own. They di ff er from the more general class of starless cores (dense cores not hosting young stellar objects or YSOs) due to their large central density n(H 2) > 10 5 cm −3 and centrally concentrated density distributions (Crapsi et al. 2005; Keto & Caselli 2008). Prestellar cores are rare, as their life-times are short ( ∼ 50 , 000 yr, see e.g. Tassis & Mouschovias 2007). L1544, embedded in the Taurus molecular cloud, rep-resents a prototypical example of a prestellar core, and it has been the target of several observational studies (Caselli et al. 2002; Spezzano et al. 2016, 2017; Redaelli et al. 2019, 2021; Caselli et al. 2022; Bianchi et al. 2023). In an e ff ort to build a large sample of prestellar cores in the Solar neighbourhood, in Caselli et al. (2025) we compiled a catalogue of 40 dense cores extracted from the Herschel Gould Belt Survey Archive (HGBS; André et al. 2010), by selecting high-density objects (ncentral > 3 × 10 5 cm −3 in the central 20 ′′ ) with no known asso-ciated protostellar candidates. The project used high-frequency (0 .7 − 1.3 mm) observations from the Atacama Pathfinder EX-periment (APEX; Güsten et al. 2006) to characterise the high-J emission of the well-known high-density tracers N 2H+ and N2D+ (critical densities ncrit ≳ 10 6 cm −3). Only three targets were detected in the high-frequency transitions N 2H+ 5 − 4 and N2D+ 6−5, including Corona Australis 151 (hereafter CrA 151). This source sits within region CrA-E described by Bresnahan et al. (2018), in the northern filament of the cloud, at about 5 pc east of the well-known young Coronet proto-cluster. The Gaia-based distance is d = 150 pc (Galli et al. 2020). No YSO is known in its proximity (cf. Esplin & Luhman 2022). However, Bresnahan et al. (2018) detected a point source at the 4 σ level in the Herschel /PACS 70 μm map. This could potentially indicate the presence of a very low-luminosity ob-ject (VeLLO, di Francesco et al. 2007; Tomida et al. 2010; cf. Bourke et al. 2006 in L1521F), either a very young low-mass protostar or a first hydrostatic core (FHSC, Larson 1969). The FHSC stage is a short-lived ( ∼ 10 4 yr) phase preceding the for-mation of the protostar (see simulations from Commerçon et al. 2012; Tomida et al. 2013). Simulations show that FHSCs can launch poorly collimated and slow (a few km s −1) outflows (see e.g. Machida et al. 2008), which however can play an impor-tant role in the initial evolution of the systems, removing angu-lar momentum e ffi ciently (Machida 2014). Observationally, the

search for FHSC candidates has been quite di ffi cult, with sev-eral works searching for them (Pineda et al. 2011; Pezzuto et al. 2012; Busch et al. 2020). In Redaelli et al. (2025), we modelled Herschel continuum maps and APEX line data, showing that i) CrA 151 is dense (n(H 2) > 10 7 cm −3 at scales of ∼ 1000 au); ii) it presents a very high deuteration level of N 2D+/N2H+ ∼ 50%, with several other deuterated species detected, such as HDO, CHD 2OH, D 2CO, and even ND 3; iii) the N 2H+ and N 2D+ lines present narrow linewidths, indicative of low levels of turbulence and low tem-peratures. These results are consistent with an evolved prestellar core, since deuterated molecules are very abundant under high-density, low-temperature conditions (Ceccarelli et al. 2014). A few details, however, appear to contradict the aforemen-tioned scenario. In particular, several sulphur-bearing species present in the APEX frequency coverage show line profiles with extended, high-velocity (a few km s −1 with respect to the source 

Vlsr ) wings. Furthermore, the modelling of the N 2H+ isotopo-logues unveiled no depletion of these species even in the central part of the core, unlike other similarly evolved prestellar cores (e.g. L1544, Redaelli et al. 2019). The question of whether CrA 151 is an evolved prestellar core, a FHSC, or an extremely young YSO remained unanswered. In this Letter, we report new ob-servations taken with the Atacama Large Millimeter and sub-millimeter Array (ALMA) at ∼ 200 au resolution that show the presence of a young protostar at the centre of the core. 

2. Observations 

ALMA Band 6 observations towards CrA 151 (RA(J2000) =

19 h10 m20 s.17, Dec(J2000) = −37 ◦08 m27 s.0, ICRS frame) were performed, using both the 12m-array (in configuration C43-2) and the 7m-array, in November 2024 and May 2025, as part of project ID 2024.1.00605.S (PI: D. Arzoumanian). The baselines range from 8 .9 m to 314 m and the maximum recoverable scale is ∼30 ′′ (corresponding to ∼4500 au). The bright quasar J1924-2914 was used as amplitude and bandpass calibrator, whilst J1937-3958 and J1925-3401 were used as phase calibrators for the 7m and 12m arrays, respectively. We performed a mosaic of 3 pointings with the 7m-array and 7 pointings with the 12m-array. The spectral setup consists of 5 spectral windows (SPWs): two high-resolution (channel width 61 .5 kHz, or 0 .08 km s −1) SPWs are centred on the DCO + 3 − 2 and SiO 5 − 4 transitions; the re-maining three SPWs were dedicated to continuum observations, with a bandwidth of 1 .875 GHz and a coarse channel width of 1.3 km s −1. These are centred at 219, 230, and 233 GHz. Details regarding the data reduction and imaging are sum-marised in Appendix A. The final synthesized beam sizes of the imaged data are 1 ′′ .49 ×1′′ .09 (PA = 76 .9◦) (continuum), 1 ′′ .60 ×

1′′ .19 (PA = 76 .9◦) (SiO 5 − 4), and 1 ′′ .63 × 1′′ .21 (PA = 78 .9◦)(DCO + 3 − 2). The continuum sensitivity is 0 .6 mJy beam −1,whilst the sensitivity of the high-resolution spectral windows is 11 − 12 mJy beam −1. These values refer to non-primary-beam corrected data; throughout the paper, we generally refer to data without primary-beam correction, unless otherwise stated. The continuum-dedicated SPWs cover also the CO 2 − 1line and the H 2CO 3 0,3 − 20,2 line, at a coarse velocity resolu-tion (978 kHz, or ≈ 1.3 km s −1). We imaged these transitions at a sensitivity of 3 .4 mJy beam −1 per channel. 

3. Analysis and Discussion 

Figure 1 shows the continuum emission in colorscale, which consists of a fairly compact, centrally peaked structure of ≈

1000 au in size, with a peak flux of ≈ 160 mJy beam −1. The spectra of SiO 5 − 4, extracted towards the east and southwest of the continuum peak, both present broad spectral shapes that are red-shifted and blue-shifted, respectively, with respect to the source local-standard-of-rest velocity ( Vlsr = 5.65 km s −1 based on N 2H+ and N 2D+ data; see Redaelli et al. 2025). These fea-tures clearly unveil the presence of an outflow in CrA 151. 

3.1. The continuum emission 

The mass of the CrA 151 envelope, traced by the dust continuum emission, is estimated using the standard equation: 

Menv = f d2 S 1.3mm 

k1.3mm B1.3mm (Tdust ) . (1) Here, S 1.3mm = 663 mJy is the total flux in the primary-beam corrected image, which we compute from pixels with S /N > 5(the white contour in Fig. 1), k1.3mm = 0.9 cm 2 g−1 is the dust opacity (Ossenkopf & Henning 1994), d the source’s distance, 

f = 100 the gas-to-dust mass ratio, and B1.3mm (Tdust ) the Planck function at the dust temperature Tdust . We adopt Tdust = 20 K, compatible with predictions from models of Class 0 envelopes (Shirley et al. 2002). For comparison, the brightness temperature of the continuum peak is Tb = 14 K, which can be considered a lower limit to the dust temperature. With these values, we obtain 

Menv = (0 .33 ± 0.06) M ⊙, where we assume a conservative 20% error to take into consideration the uncertainties on the flux, on 

kν, and on Tdust . This value is consistent with envelope masses of similarly young cores measured with interferometric obser-vations (Stephens et al. 2018; Maureira et al. 2020). Given the equivalent radius of the envelope of 630 au, this corresponds to an average density in the envelope of 4 × 10 7 cm −3.

3.2. The outflow properties 

To investigate the outflow properties, we compute the integrated intensities of the blue-shifted and red-shifted components only, and we show them in Fig. 1. The red lobe appears spatially con-centrated, with an extension of ≈ 600 au in the West-East direc-tion in the plane of the sky. The blue lobe is instead brighter and more extended, stretching for ≈ 900 au towards south-west and beyond the edges of the continuum emission. We can speculate that, if the continuum brightness traces the matter density dis-tribution, the blue-shifted lobe bursts through a region of lower density, hence expanding more. We estimate the projected dy-namic age of the outflow based on the length of the blue lobe (L = 900 au, based on the 5 σ contour in Fig. 1) and its veloc-ity extension ∆V = 8.5 km s −1, computed looking at the average SiO spectra in the same region (spectrum not shown here) and considering the positions in velocity where the flux reaches the zero level. With this data, we evaluate tpr dyn = L/∆V = 500 yr. This value is the projected quantity 1, which is related to the in-trinsic one through the inclination of the system with respect to the line-of-sight ( ϕ): tdyn = tpr dyn / tan( ϕ). The presence of SiO emission arising from a currently ejected outflow suggests that the outflow has a velocity ≳ 20 km s −1, as this is the minimum velocity required to release Si from dust grains (Gusdorf et al. 2008, and references therein). > 3.3. The envelope in molecular emission

The DCO + 3 − 2 emission is well extended over the whole field-of-view, hence the importance of combining the interferometric data with the single-dish ones. In the following, we discuss and model the primary-beam corrected and combined data. The line shape is a single Gaussian in the external parts of the envelope, with narrow linewidths (see Appendix C for more details). In the central 1000 − 1500 au, however, a second, fainter velocity com-ponent is visible at higher velocities ( > 6 km s −1, see Figs. C.1 and C.2). Assuming Vlsr = 5.65 km s −1, the flux dip does not ap-pear in correspondence with the source’s velocity (cf. Fig. C.2), suggesting that the second feature is an additional velocity com-ponent, and not due to e.g. self-absorption. The weaker compo-nent, in fact, presents velocities in the range 5 .9 − 6.2 km s −1.To model the DCO + spectra, we start by fitting a single Gaus-sian to all positions where Tpeak /rms > 10. Next, we calculate residuals within the velocity range [3 .9 − 8.2] km s −1 and per-form a second fit with two Gaussians for pixels where residuals exceed 2 ×rms . We then create composite images for the centroid velocity (see Fig. 3) and velocity dispersion (shown in Fig. C.4), which combine the single Gaussian results when two compo-nents cannot be fitted and the blueshifted velocity component results elsewhere. The line centroid shows a smooth gradient in the direc-tion north-west towards south-east, with values going from 5.2 km s −1 to 5 .8 km s −1. This direction is qualitatively perpen-dicular to that of the outflows, which is suggestive of core and envelope rotation. We measure a velocity gradient of 25 km s −1

pc −1, in agreement with similar measurements performed with interferometers in Class 0 envelopes (Gaudel et al. 2020). The DCO + lines are narrow: the measured velocity disper-sion is σV ≤ 0.3 km s −1. The increase seen towards the centre is associated with the appearance of the second velocity compo-nent, which –when not properly resolved– leads to line broad-ening. The median value is ⟨σV⟩ = (0 .18 ± 0.02)km s −1. For comparison, the sound speed at 20 K is CS = 0.26 km s −1. The thermal linewidth of DCO + at the same temperature is σth =

0.07 km s −1. The envelope is quiescent with subsonic motions. 

4. Conclusions 

In this Letter, we addressed the evolutionary stage of the core CrA 151. The ALMA data unambiguously reveal the presence of a molecular outflow, seen in multiple species: SiO, formalde-hyde, and CO. The presence of a compact SiO outflow strongly favours a protostellar nature over an FHSC interpretation; un-der the standard assumption that SiO requires shock velocities of ≳ 20 km s −1, the observed flow is di ffi cult to reconcile with FHSC models predicting only a few km s −1. The core bolometric temperature, evaluated from the Herschel data, is 18 .8 K. This is well below 70 K, which is considered the limit between Class 0 and Class I objects (Chen et al. 1995). Several pieces of evidence support that the protostar at the centre of CrA 151 is extremely young. We derived a projected dynamical age of 500 yr for the outflow. Correcting for the in-clination value obtained from the SiO data, we compute tdyn ≤

250 yr. The DCO + emission traces a quiescent envelope with an ordered velocity gradient, indicating that the dense gas surround-ing the protostellar object is still unperturbed by the protostellar

CrA 151 appears to be an ideal new laboratory to study young protostars and their surroundings. High-angular-resolution ( ∼ 10 au) data will be necessary to detect and pos-sibly model the disk properties and to constrain the mass of the central object. This analysis will provide, among others, the inclination of the system, which is essential to obtain reliable values for the outflow properties. Furthermore, the intermediate disk-envelope scales also deserve attention. The H 2CO and CO data show extended emission in the shape of an arc-like struc-ture. Still, we lack the spectral resolution and large-scale flux information needed to analyse this feature kinematically. This work highlights the importance of the synergy between single-dish and interferometric observations to probe the early phases of star formation.

However, in the case of the continuum (and the DCO + line, see below), this choice generated peculiar artefacts in the residuals, which change significantly in shape depending on the used scale values. In these two cases, hence, we preferred the use of ’ hogbom ’. The cubes were imaged at the original chan-nel width. The DCO + 3 − 2 line exhibits very extended emission, and the high spectral resolution of this SPW allows us to identify clear signs of missing flux due to large-scale filtering of the in-terferometer. We hence combined the interferometric data with an APEX single-dish map of the same transition. These data will be presented in a separate paper (Majerczyk et al., in prep.), but we briefly summarise their key properties here. The data were collected under project ID M9502C_115. They consist of one on-the-fly datacube with a 2 ′.5 × 2′.5 footprint, enough to en-compass the ALMA FoV completely.
