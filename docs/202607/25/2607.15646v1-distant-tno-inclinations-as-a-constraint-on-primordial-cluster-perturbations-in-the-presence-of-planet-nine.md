## 摘要
太阳几乎肯定诞生于一个恒星团中，这意味着太阳系在婴儿期受到邻近恒星一定程度的 外部扰动。然而，已发表的对最强相关恒星遭遇的估计范围很广，从相对温和的扰动到剧烈破坏性的飞越。先前，遥远的跨海王星天体（TNO）群体适中的轨道倾角分散曾被用来论证强烈的原初遭遇不太可能发生，并且外太阳系在诞生时并未被剧烈搅动。在本研究中，我们独立检验了这一约束，并探讨在第九行星存在下这一结论是否仍然成立。由于第九行星可以在数十亿年时间尺度上重塑遥远的TNO群体，当前倾角分布在多大程度上保留了原初星团激发的直接记录，这一点先验并不清楚。为解决此问题，我们比较了长期N体积分，其中遥远测试粒子初始时要么处于强烈星团扰动的构型，要么处于相对平静的构型，然后在已知巨行星、银河潮汐、经过恒星以及第九行星的影响下演化。我们发现，第九行星并未将高度激发的原初群体转变为类似观测到的低倾角遥远样本的群体。相反，模拟与观测的遥远TNO群体之间的比较表明，即使在第九行星存在下，观测到的遥远TNO也与相对温和扰动的诞生环境最为一致。如果这种相对较低的倾角分散被确认为遥远TNO群体的内在特征，而非观测偏差的假象，那么它将对原初星团扰动的严重程度构成强有力的约束。

## Abstract
The Sun was almost certainly born in a stellar cluster, implying some degree of external forcing from neighboring stars during the Solar System's infancy. Published estimates of the strongest relevant stellar encounter, however, span a broad range, from relatively gentle perturbations to violently disruptive flybys. The modest inclination dispersion of the distant trans-Neptunian population has previously been used to argue that strong primordial encounters were unlikely and that the outer Solar System was not violently stirred at birth. In this work, we independently examine this constraint and ask whether this conclusion holds in the presence of Planet Nine. Because Planet Nine can reshape the distant trans-Neptunian population over gigayear timescales, the extent to which the present-day inclination distribution preserves a direct record of primordial cluster excitation is a priori unclear. To address this question, we compare long-term N-body integrations in which distant test particles begin either in strongly cluster-perturbed configurations or in comparatively quiescent ones, and are then evolved under the influence of the known giant planets, the Galactic tide, the effects of passing stars, and Planet Nine. We find that Planet Nine does not transform a highly excited primordial population into one resembling the observed low-inclination distant sample. Instead, comparisons between the simulated and the observed distant TNO populations indicate that the observed distant TNOs are most consistent with a relatively mildly perturbed birth environment, even in the presence of Planet Nine. If this comparatively low inclination dispersion is confirmed to be an intrinsic feature of the distant TNO population, rather than an artifact of observational bias, it constitutes a robust constraint on the severity of primordial cluster perturbations.
