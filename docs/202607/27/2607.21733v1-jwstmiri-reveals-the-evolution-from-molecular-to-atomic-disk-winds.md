## 摘要
原行星盘的演化和消散——受吸积、磁致喷流和风以及光蒸发风控制——从根本上塑造了行星系统。确定这些质量损失过程如何共同演化对于约束行星形成路径至关重要。我们分析了72个倾斜（i>40°）主要是ClassII盘的历史JWST/MIRI/IFU数据，以识别和表征空间分辨的喷流和风，重点关注[NeII]和H2谱线。在66个盘中探测到H2 S(1)、S(3)、S(5)、S(7)和/或[NeII]的延展发射，显示出多样化的形态。我们开发了一个框架来识别垂直于盘的锥形H2风和高速[NeII]喷流，分别探测到46个和40个盘。所有具有[NeII]喷流的源都显示出相应的由H2（85%）或[OI]示踪的风，建立了喷流和风之间的联系。[NeII]喷流和H2风的探测率与质量吸积率正相关，与盘倾角或恒星质量无关。相反，略微分辨的低速[NeII]风更倾向于在低吸积率源中出现。在具有H2风的源中，由S(7)和S(5)示踪的热风的探测率随吸积率下降而衰减得比冷S(1)成分更快。与高分辨率[OI]6300Å光谱的比较显示，[OI] LVC和延展H2风探测更倾向于中等到高吸积率源（>~10^{-8.5} M_sun/yr），而低吸积率源仅显示[OI]和[NeII]风。这些结果表明，原子喷流和原子+分子风（与MHD盘风起源一致）在早期活跃吸积盘阶段占主导，而在较低吸积率时，喷流减弱，风主要变为原子性的。

## Abstract
The evolution and dispersal of protoplanetary disks--governed by accretion, magnetically launched jets and winds, and photoevaporative winds--fundamentally shape planetary systems. Determining how these mass-loss processes co-evolve is crucial for constraining planet formation pathways. We analyze archival JWST/MIRI/IFU data of 72 inclined (i>40deg) mostly ClassII disks to identify and characterize spatially resolved jets and winds, focusing on [NeII] and H2 lines. Extended emission in H2 S(1), S(3), S(5), S(7) and/or [NeII] is detected toward 66 disks, revealing diverse morphologies. We develop a framework to identify conical H2 winds and high-velocity [NeII] jets perpendicular to the disk, detecting them toward 46 and 40 disks, respectively. All sources with [NeII] jets exhibit a corresponding wind traced in either H2 (85%) or [OI], establishing a connection between jets and winds. The detection fractions of [NeII]-jets and H2-winds correlate positively with mass accretion rate, with no dependence on disk inclination or stellar mass. Conversely, marginally resolved low-velocity [NeII] winds are found preferentially toward lower accretors. Among sources with H2 winds, detection of hotter winds traced by S(7) and S(5) declines more rapidly with decreasing accretion rate than the colder S(1) component. Comparison with high-resolution [OI]6300\textÅ spectroscopy reveals [OI] LVC and extended H2 wind detections preferentially toward moderate-to-high accretors (>~10^{-8.5}~Msun/yr), whereas lower accretors exhibit only [OI] and [NeII] winds. Together, these results indicate that atomic jets and atomic+molecular winds, consistent with an MHD disk-wind origin, dominate during early, actively accreting disk phases, while at lower accretion rates, jets weaken and winds become predominantly atomic.
