## 摘要
围绕M星AU Microscopii的碎片盘有一系列大尺度团块，在东南侧远离星体高速运动，高于盘面。另外两个明亮特征位于盘的西北侧，低于盘面并朝向星体运动。这些团块仅在散射光中观测到，表明它们影响大小为0.2微米的小颗粒。我们提出了一种机制，通过恒星力和一个倾斜、偏心的行星与外部碎片盘相互作用，发射周期性的尘埃团块。在我们最佳匹配的模拟中，行星每轨道周期对盘施加一次脉冲，产生盘面上方尘埃的周期性增强。我们假设星风仅作用于那些超过阈值高度（我们模型中的一个自由参数）的颗粒，之后它们被加速向外。周期性粒子抛射和轨迹的行为显著依赖于行星的质量、偏心率和倾角；行星与盘之间的间距；以及星风力与恒星引力之比（β）。我们找到了与观测定性匹配的模拟结果，其中包含一颗尚未发现但观察允许的行星，质量为2倍木星质量，半长轴在3-4 au之间，偏心率为0.37，倾角为30°，一个在5-6 au之间的粒子环，以及星风高度阈值z_h = hr，其中h≈0.02。我们使用表面亮度图可视化模拟，并与AU Mic的现有观测进行比较。我们发现β≈1.8的值以与AU Mic盘中观测到的团块相当的速度径向向外加速团块，并产生了类似于观测到的特征。

## Abstract
The debris disk orbiting the M star AU Microscopii has a series of large-scale clumps that move away from the star at high velocities above the mid-plane on the southeast side. Two more bright features lie on the northwest side of the disk, localized below the mid-plane and moving toward the star. These clumps are only observed in scattered light indicating that they affect small 0.2$μ$m-sized grains. We present a mechanism for emitting periodic dust clumps by appealing to stellar forces and an inclined, eccentric planet interacting with an exterior debris disk. In our best-matching simulations, the planet exerts an impulse on the disk every orbital period, generating periodic enhancements in dust above the mid-plane. We assume that the stellar wind only acts on grains once they reach a height above the mid-planet that exceeds a threshold value (a free parameter in our model), at which point they are accelerated outward. The behavior of periodic particle ejections and trajectories depends significantly on the planet's mass, eccentricity, and inclination; separation between the planet and disk; and the ratio of stellar wind force to the star's gravitational force ($β$). We find a promising qualitative match to observations with simulations that include an as-yet-undiscovered and observationally allowed planet with mass $2 M_J$, semi-major axis between 3-4 au, eccentricity of 0.37, and inclination of 30$^\circ$, a ring of particles between 5-6 au, and a stellar wind height threshold of $z_h = hr$, where $h \approx 0.02$. We visualize our simulation with surface brightness maps to compare with existing observations of AU Mic. We find that a value of $β\approx 1.8$ accelerates the clumps radially outward at velocities that are comparable to the clumps seen in the AU Mic disk and produces features similar to those observed.
