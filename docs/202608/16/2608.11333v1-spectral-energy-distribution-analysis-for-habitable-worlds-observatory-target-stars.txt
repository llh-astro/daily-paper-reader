ABSTRACT The Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) will directly image and characterize planets potentially similar to Earth orbiting nearby stars. Accurate stellar properties, particularly effective temperatures (Teff ), angular diameters ( θ⋆), and bolometric fluxes ( Fbol ), are essential for reliable Habitable Zone (HZ) calculations, exoplanet yield predictions, and coronagraph design trade studies. We present a spectral energy distribution (SED) analysis for the 164 stars from the HWO Exoplanet Exploration Program (ExEP) list, using χ2-minimization fits of empirical spectral templates from the Pickles stellar spectral flux library to broadband photometric data. The SED fits provide direct measurements of 

Fbol . We adopt spectroscopic Teff values from the PASTEL catalog for 127 of the 164 stars, with the remainder drawn from the HWO ExEP catalog, and use these in combination with Fbol to calculate 

θ⋆ via the Stefan-Boltzmann equation. As a consistency check, we compare the Pickles template Teff 

with the adopted spectroscopic values and find agreement to within ∼3% for 65% of the sample, with a systematic tendency for the templates to yield cooler values. We identify 48 stars with discrepancies exceeding 200 K, including close binary systems where photometric contamination compromises the SED solution. We discuss the implications for HZ boundary calculations, atmospheric retrieval of directly imaged planets, and target prioritization for HWO. This SED catalog constitutes a uniform set of empirically determined Fbol values and associated stellar parameters for the HWO target sample.

INTRODUCTION A fundamental goal of exoplanet science is to ob-tain reflected-light spectra of Earth-sized planets in the habitable zones (HZs) of nearby stars, enabling the search for atmospheric biosignatures (Kasting et al. 1993; Kopparapu et al. 2013; Meadows et al. 2018; Schwieterman et al. 2018). The Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO), recommended by the Astro2020 Decadal Survey 1 as the next NASA flagship astrophysics mission, is designed to detect and spectrally character-ize at least 25 potentially Earth-like planets using an onboard coronagraph to achieve the planet-star contrast ratios ( ∼10 −10 ) required for such observations. Additional studies have addressed inner working angle constraints (Vaughan et al. 2023), radial velocity reconnaissance (Laliotis et al. 2023), dynamical viability of known plan-etary systems (Kane et al. 2024; Kane & Burt 2024), and atmospheric retrieval frameworks (Young et al. 2024). A persistent challenge is the heterogeneity of stellar property measurements in these catalogs. Spectral en-ergy distribution (SED) fitting is a well-established tech-nique for determining stellar Fbol and Teff (Pickles 1998; van Belle & von Braun 2009a; Boyajian et al. 2012a,b; von Braun et al. 2014). By fitting broadband photome-try to empirical spectral templates, SED analysis yields a direct measurement of Fbol , which, when combined with Teff or θ⋆, provides the respective other quantity via the Stefan-Boltzmann law. In this paper, we present SED fits for the 164 stars in the HWO ExEP list. We describe the data sources and methodology in Section 2, present the SED catalog and compare stellar parameters in Section 3, discuss the implications in Section 4, and summarize in Section 5.  

> 2.

METHODOLOGY 2.1. Data Sources 

The target sample was drawn from the HWO ExEP (Mamajek & Stapelfeldt 2024), publicly hosted by the NASA Exoplanet Archive (NEA; DOI: 10.26133/NEA39). The 164 stars represent the highest-priority targets for HWO direct imaging of Earth-sized planets in the HZ (Tuchow et al. 2024, 2025). For each star, we extracted distance ( d), apparent V -band mag-nitude, Teff , L⋆, and estimated θ⋆ from the ExEP cata-log. It is worth noting that the ExEP θ⋆ values are not independent measurements but are estimated from the catalog L⋆ and Teff via the Stefan-Boltzmann relation (Mamajek & Stapelfeldt 2024). For the SED fitting, we compiled broadband pho-tometry from the literature for each target star fol-lowing the procedures of von Braun et al. (2014) and von Braun & Boyajian (2017). Accurate SED fitting re-quires photometric coverage that spans the Wien peak of the stellar flux distribution, where the sensitivity to the spectral template match is greatest, through the Rayleigh-Jeans tail. For this reason, we draw photom-etry from a broad range of well-established catalogs spanning UV through mid-IR wavelengths, including Str¨ omgren uvby (Hauck & Mermilliod 1998), Johnson 

U BV (Mermilliod 1987), Geneva photometry, Tycho-2 (Høg et al. 2000), Hipparcos (van Leeuwen 2007), 2MASS (Skrutskie et al. 2006), and IRAS/WISE, sup-plemented by additional literature photometry where available (see Table 1 and associated photometry ref-erences; von Braun & Boyajian 2017). Restricting the photometric input to only a few infrared catalogs would sacrifice coverage around the Wien peak and reduce the number of degrees of freedom in the χ2 fit, yield-ing larger uncertainties on Fbol and poorer template discrimination. All photometric data are individually vetted to exclude measurements affected by saturation, source confusion, or catalog artifacts, following the pro-cedures described in von Braun & Boyajian (2017). The complete set of photometry files, documenting every measurement used for each star, is provided as supple-mentary data accompanying this paper. 2.2. SED Fitting 

The SED fitting procedure follows the methodology of von Braun & Boyajian (2017); von Braun et al. (2014); Boyajian et al. (2012a,b); van Belle & von Braun (2009a). In brief, we perform a χ2-minimization fit of the literature photometry to the library of empirical stellar spectral templates from Pickles (1998), correct-ing for filter profiles as described in Mann & von Braun (2015). The Pickles (1998) library comprises 131 flux-calibrated spectra spanning 1150–25000 ˚ A, covering a broad range of spectral types and luminosity classes. The SED fit directly yields Fbol , defined as the integral of the best-fitting template spectrum scaled to match the photometric data. This quantity does not depend on assumptions about stellar evolutionary models or bolometric corrections. A key advantage of the Pickles (1998) library is that the templates are empirical, not model-dependent. Al-ternative approaches using synthetic model grids (e.g., ATLAS9, PHOENIX/BT-Settl) could in principle pro-vide finer sampling in Teff , log g, and [Fe/H], but would introduce model-dependent systematics into the SED shape. Because Fbol is an integral quantity dominated by the broadband photometric data rather than the fine structure of the template spectrum, it is robust to the choice of template library at the percent level (von Braun & Boyajian 2017). We therefore adopt the Pickles library for all fits in this work, and note that a quantitative comparison of Fbol values derived from em-pirical versus model templates for this sample would be a valuable future exercise. 2.3. To calculate θ⋆ from Fbol via Equation 1, we re-quire an independent determination of Teff for each star. Rather than adopting the coarse Teff values associated with the discrete Pickles template grid (131 values span-ning the full spectral type range), we use high-resolution spectroscopic determinations from the PASTEL catalog (Soubiran et al. 2016). PASTEL is a regularly updated compilation of published spectroscopic determinations of Teff , log g, and [Fe/H] from the literature, and serves as the primary source for the spectroscopic parameters in the Hypatia Catalog (Hinkel et al. 2014, 2016). We cross-matched the 164 HWO ExEP stars against PASTEL using HD identifiers and, for stars cataloged under Bayer or Flamsteed designations, verified alter-nate identifiers via SIMBAD. For each matched star, we computed the error-weighted mean of all PASTEL 

Teff determinations with reported uncertainties. Mea-surements without reported uncertainties were excluded from the weighted mean but retained as consistency checks. A total of 127 stars (77% of the sample) have PASTEL matches with at least one Teff determination. The median number of reported PASTEL determina-tions per matched star is 17, and the median error-weighted uncertainty on the adopted Teff is 40 K. We note that the individual PASTEL determinations are drawn from heterogeneous literature sources employing different spectra, line lists, and analysis methods, and are not necessarily independent; the formal weighted-mean uncertainties may therefore underestimate the true systematic uncertainty on the adopted Teff for some stars. We do not impose an explicit uncertainty floor on the adopted Teff values, instead reporting the formal weighted-mean uncertainties directly. However, in light of the systematic comparisons discussed in Section 3.2, we caution that a realistic floor on the true Teff uncer-tainty for well-studied stars is of order 100 K. For the remaining 37 stars without PASTEL determinations, we adopt the Teff values from the HWO ExEP catalog; these are identified in Table 1 with a table note. The adopted 

Teff values are combined with the SED-derived Fbol to calculate θ⋆ using the methods of Mann et al. (2015); Boyajian et al. (2012a,b); von Braun et al. (2014). Adetailed discussion of the error analysis framework is provided by von Braun & Boyajian (2017).

3.2. Comparison of Stellar Parameters 

A primary objective of this work is to assess the de-gree of agreement between the SED-derived stellar pa-rameters and those compiled in the HWO ExEP list. As shown in Figure 2(a), the majority of the Teff mea-surements cluster around the one-to-one line, indicating broad consistency between the Pickles template values and the adopted spectroscopic temperatures. The me-dian absolute offset is ≈135 K, corresponding to ∼2.3% for a typical solar-type star, and 65% of the sample agrees to within 3%. We note a systematic tendency for the Pickles templates to yield cooler Teff values than the adopted spectroscopic temperatures: 76% of the tem-plate values fall below the adopted value, with a me-dian signed offset of −112 K. This bias likely reflects the coarse discretization of the Pickles template grid com-bined with metallicity and luminosity-class effects that are not fully captured by the 131 available templates. Despite this systematic, the level of agreement is suffi-cient to confirm that the SED fits are selecting physically appropriate templates for the majority of the sample. The Teff comparison shown in panel (a) of Figure 2 serves as a consistency check between two quantities de-rived by entirely different methods: the adopted spec-troscopic temperatures and the best-fitting Pickles tem-plate temperatures. It is not an independent temper-ature determination, since the template temperatures are associated with a discrete grid of 131 empirical tem-plates rather than measured for each star. We note that the Pickles Teff values are correspondingly coarser than the spectroscopic values; the comparison is most useful for identifying significant outliers rather than assessing precision offsets. A total of 48 stars (29% of the sample) exhibit 

Teff discrepancies >200 K. Among the most promi-nent are the B components of HD 131156 ( ξ Bootis) and HD 165341 (70 Ophiuchi). For HD 131156 B, the adopted Teff = 4288 ± 127 K, whereas the best-fit SED template yields Teff = 5322 ± 186 K. Sim-ilarly, HD 165341 B has an adopted temperature of 4393 ±149 K compared to the SED value of 5147 ±180 K. DISCUSSION The boundaries of the circumstellar HZ are fundamen-tally determined by L⋆ and Teff (Kasting et al. 1993; Kopparapu et al. 2013; Kane 2014; Kopparapu et al. 2014). For a solar-type star, a shift of ∼200 K in Teff at constant luminosity translates to a displacement of the HZ boundaries by several percent in orbital distance, corresponding to ∼1–3 mas at 10 pc (Kane 2014), com-parable to the anticipated inner working angle of HWO’s coronagraph (Vaughan et al. 2023). For the majority of the HWO ExEP sample, the existing catalog values are sufficiently accurate that HZ boundary calculations are not significantly affected. However, for systems where the Teff discrepancy exceeds 200 K, the propagated ef-fect can be non-trivial. For example, the conservative HZ inner boundary for HD 69830 ( d = 12 .58 pc) shifts outward by ∼2% when the SED template temperature (Teff = 5013 K) is adopted in place of the spectro-scopic value ( Teff = 5414 K), corresponding to ∼1.3 mas. Given that HD 69830 hosts three known Neptune-mass planets (Lovis et al. 2006; Tanner et al. 2015), the accu-racy of the HZ delineation for this system has concrete observational consequences. Beyond HZ boundaries, Teff uncertainties have po-tential implications for the spectral characterization of directly imaged planets. In reflected-light observa-tions, the planetary geometric albedo spectrum Ag (λ)is derived by dividing out the assumed stellar SED (Damiano & Hu 2022; Robinson & Salvador 2023). Pre-vious studies have shown that an error in Teff introduces a wavelength-dependent systematic: a 200 K offset for a solar-type star alters the assumed UV flux by ∼20–30% while affecting the near-infrared by only ∼5%, propa-gating into the retrieved albedo spectrum as an artificial tilt that can bias the inferred strengths of biosignature absorption features (Damiano et al. 2023; Young et al. 2024). While we do not model these retrieval effects di-rectly, they emphasize the potential importance of accu-rate, SED-calibrated stellar properties for HWO targets well in advance of spectral characterization observations. Several HWO ExEP stars are known exoplanet hosts (Kane et al. 2024; Christiansen et al. 2025), including HD 9826 A ( υ Andromedae), HD 75732 A (55 Can-cri), HD 95128 (47 Ursae Majoris), HD 160691 ( μ Arae), and HD 219134. For these systems, the accuracy of the stellar parameters is critical for determining whether known planets reside within the HZ (Kane & Gelino 2012; Kane et al. 2024; Kane & Burt 2024). The an-gular diameter of HD 75732 A was previously measured interferometrically by von Braun et al. (2011), and the resulting stellar parameters were used to characterize the system’s HZ boundaries (Kane et al. 2011). The 

∼368 K offset between the adopted Teff and the SED template value reported here motivates a reexamination of the catalog value in light of that interferometric mea-surement.

CONCLUSIONS We have presented a systematic SED analysis for the 164 stars in the HWO ExEP list. Using χ2-minimization fits of empirical spectral templates from the Pickles (1998) library to broadband photometric data, we de-rived Fbol for each star. These Fbol values constitute the primary independent product of this work. Com-bined with spectroscopic Teff values adopted from the PASTEL catalog (Soubiran et al. 2016) for 127 stars and from the HWO ExEP catalog for the remaining 37, they yield θ⋆ estimates that are broadly consistent with the catalog values. The Teff of the best-fitting Pickles templates agrees with the adopted spectroscopic values to within ∼3% for 65% of the sample, with a system-atic tendency for the templates to yield cooler values. A subset of 48 stars exhibit Teff discrepancies exceed-ing 200 K, most prominently the B components of close binary systems where photometric contamination from the brighter primary is the likely cause. For systems where the temperature discrepancy is significant, the propagated shift in HZ boundaries can exceed several percent in orbital distance. This catalog constitutes a uniform set of empirically determined Fbol values for the highest-priority HWO target stars. For targets with in-terferometric θ⋆ from the CHARA Array or other facili-ties, the Fbol values reported here can be combined with the measured diameters to yield fully empirical Teff de-terminations independent of the adopted spectroscopic temperature scale, although still dependent on the SED-based Fbol .The principal contribution of this paper is the uniform catalog of empirically derived Fbol values for the 164 HWO ExEP target stars, determined with a single, con-sistent methodology. The supplementary products ac-companying this work, including the complete photom-etry files for each star and the individual SED fits, are archived in the PASP supplementary material system. Together with the adopted spectroscopic temperatures and derived angular diameters, these products provide a resource for HWO mission planning, target prioriti-zation, and the interpretation of future direct-imaging observations.
