PDS 70 c and SR 12 c: Observational Constraints on Giant-Planet and Satellite Formation 

I. Mosqueira 1

> 1Department of Physics and Astronomy, San José State University, 1 Washington Square, San José, CA 95192, USA

ABSTRACT PDS 70 c and SR 12 c are the only bound planetary-mass objects with secure cold submillimeter disk detections. Together they constrain giant-planet growth and satellite formation. The PDS 70 planets exhibit remarkable parallels to the Jupiter–Saturn pair in our Solar System. Both PDS 70 planets accrete within one shared gap, which links their final masses, the material reaching each Hill sphere, and the properties of the circumplanetary disk. Planetary torques deplete this finite reservoir, causing circumplanetary supply to decline as the protoplanets open a circumstellar gap. SR 12 c separates the planetary-growth and satellite-formation timescales: gas and solids survive even though its current mass-growth timescale is (1 .9 ± 0.5) × 10 9 yr. For PDS 70 c, the 855-μm flux implies 

0.007 –0.031 M⊕ of dust at 26 K in the optically thin limit, while the optically thick limit requires a minimum coplanar radius of 0.58 –0.66 au, depending on temperature. This scale is compatible with late-stage gas inflow through a well-formed gap with specific angular momentum, rc ∼ RH/3. These observations are consistent with our satellite formation model for Jupiter and Saturn (Mosqueira & Estrada 2003a,b, submitted in 2001), in which gas-drag clearing of satellitesimals gives formation timescales of ∼ 10 6 yr for Callisto and ∼ 10 7 yr for Iapetus. The PDS 70 age of 5.4 ± 1.0 Myr lies between these values. These constraints provide strong support for a quiescent, solids-enhanced satellite-forming environment, coupled in the early stages to planetary-gap evolution.

INTRODUCTION The PDS 70 system connects a directly imaged pair of accreting giant planets to a spatially localized cir-cumplanetary continuum source while the planets re-main embedded in their natal disk. PDS 70 b lies near 22 au inside the large dust-depleted cavity (Keppler et al. 2018); PDS 70 c lies near 34.5 au, and both planets have been detected in H α emission (Haffert et al. 2019). The stellar mass is approximately 0.76 M⊙, the distance is 112.4 pc, and the system age is 5.4 ± 1.0 Myr (Müller et al. 2018; Wang et al. 2021). The system therefore can be viewed as an analogue of the Jupiter–Saturn archi-tecture in the most relevant sense here: two neighboring giant planets and their circumplanetary environments are embedded in one large-scale gap. The key motivational observation was the 855-μmALMA image of Benisty et al. (2021). At an angular

resolution of about 20 mas, a compact and detached source was found at the predicted position of PDS 70 c. The inferred source radius is smaller than approximately 1.2 au, and the optically thin dust masses obtained for the adopted grain-size distributions range from approx-imately 0.007 to 0.031 M⊕ at 26 K. The source lies well inside the planet’s Hill sphere and is spatially associated with the planet rather than the circumstellar ring. Its interpretation as a moon-forming CPD is therefore well founded. Two lines of work anticipate the physical picture ad-vanced here. Balbus & Hawley (1998) and Hawley et al. (1999) show that hydrodynamic turbulent fluctu-ations in the presence of Keplerian shear decay through Coriolis-mediated epicyclic energy exchange coupled to the turbulence cascade (see Appendix A). A second line explains the masses, compositions, and survival of the regular satellites of Jupiter and Saturn with a quies-cent, solids-enhanced circumplanetary disk (Mosqueira & Estrada 2003a,b; Estrada et al. 2009; Mosqueira et al. 2010b). The vertical distribution of millimeter grains directly constrains the turbulent stirring available to counter dust settling. In HL Tau, Pinte et al. (2016) found that the inferred thin dust layer is consistent with α ∼ 10 −4.Direct ALMA imaging of 12 edge-on disks subsequently showed significant vertical settling of large grains (Vil-lenave et al. 2020). Oph 163131 provides an especially stringent case: its millimeter-grain scale height is about an order of magnitude below that of the micron-sized grains, resulting in α ≲ 10 −5 (Villenave et al. 2022). A uniform analysis of 33 disks found vertically settled outer disks in every one of the 23 systems for which the thickness could be constrained (Villenave et al. 2025). The corresponding inferred coefficient α has typical up-per limits ≲ 10 −3; in several systems the inferred stirring is too weak for turbulence to be the principal driver of accretion. Molecular-line widths provide an independent con-straint on gas motions in the layers and radial regions traced by the observed transition. Flaherty et al. (2020) obtained upper limits of 0.08 cs in MWC 480 and 0.12 cs

in V4046 Sgr. The same analysis inferred nonthermal CO line broadening of 0.25 –0.33 cs in DM Tau. How-ever, for this detection to translate into observational support for an α disk planet-formation model, the fol-lowing hurdles would need to be cleared: first, the mea-sured broadening would have to be tied to a correlated radial–azimuthal stress; second, one would have to es-tablish that the system is in the planet-forming phase; and third, the CO-emitting region would need to be shown to overlap with the disk planet-forming region. Taken together, these observations make a robust case that weak turbulence should be regarded as the empir-ical prior in the disk regions for which settling or line-width constraints exist. (See Appendix D for an AI publication-record confirmation-bias failure mode that reversed this evidentiary burden.) Since the original observations in 2021, PDS 70 c has been recovered at multiple frequencies and epochs (Fasano et al. 2025; Domínguez-Jamett et al. 2025). The independent 0.88-mm detection of a disk around SR 12 c shows that the PDS 70 c continuum reservoir is not an isolated planetary-mass observation; the two systems lie on the extrapolated young disk–host sequence (Wu et al. 2020, 2022). The low Band 4–Band 7 spectral index has motivated both an optically thick dust-ring inter-pretation and a possible variable non-dust contribution (Domínguez-Jamett et al. 2025; Casassus et al. 2026; Shibaike et al. 2026). Molecular-line observations have also mapped the circumstellar gas in increasing detail (Law et al. 2024; Rampinelli et al. 2024). These mea-surements sharpen the radiative-transfer limits but do not yet supply a direct gas mass for the compact CPD. The central claim is broad. On the circumstel-lar side, the two PDS 70 giants share one gap; their tidal torques deplete the accessible gas, limit their final masses, change the angular momentum reaching their Hill spheres, and eventually terminate circumplanetary delivery. On the circumplanetary side, the delivery of circumstellar solids and gas constrains the compositional budget available to build moons. Section 2 establishes the observational constraints on PDS 70 c and SR 12 c, including the optically thin and optically thick continuum limits. Section 3 relates the inflow angular momentum to the CPD radial scale. Section 4 gives the torque-driven depletion of the fi-nite circumstellar reservoir in the common planetary re-gion. Section 5 converts the calculated late supply and a planar gap-fed angular-momentum distribution into a retained CPD surface-density profile while separating Hill-sphere entry from retention. Section 6 character-izes the transient stream–disk response and its decay (see Appendix A for the supporting calculation). Sec-tion 7 develops the implications for the solids-enhanced minimum-mass chronology, regular-satellite formation, and planetesimal-fragment delivery. Appendix B col-lects the adopted quantities and convenient numerical scalings.

The present detection census must be separated by tracer. PDS 70 c and SR 12 c remain the two se-cure cold 0.88-mm continuum detections around bound planetary-mass companions. Two embedded systems provide gaseous or chemical CPD candidates rather than equivalent continuum detections: localized 13 CO emis-sion in AS 209 (Bae et al. 2022) and localized HCN and C 2H emission in HD 163296 (Izquierdo et al. 2026). In both cases the central planet is inferred from disk structure or kinematics rather than directly detected. JWST has opened a complementary warm-disk census, including mid-infrared disk signatures around GQ Lup B (Cugno et al. 2024), silicate emission around YSES-1 b (Hoch et al. 2025), molecular gas and dust emis-sion around CT Cha b (Cugno & Grant 2025) and De-lorme 1 AB b (Mâlin et al. 2025), and an infrared excess consistent with a disk around TWA 27 b (Patapis et al. 2025). Those measurements trace warm micron-sized dust, infrared excess, or molecular gas; they are com-plementary evidence for circumplanetary material but are not additional points on the cold 0.88-mm scaling relation. 2.8. TRANSIENT INFLOW-DRIVEN FLUCTUATIONS While the observational evidence favors quiescent planet-forming disks, gas inflow can nevertheless drive turbulent fluctuations in a circumplanetary disk by sup-plying kinetic energy. This forcing is transient and de-cays as the inflow declines; it is not evidence for sus-tained turbulence in an unforced Keplerian disk. Ap-pendix A gives the fluctuation-energy, Reynolds-stress, ram-pressure, and decay calculations. The stream–disk interaction acts through shocks, mo-mentum loading, and velocity mismatch, but its con-version efficiency, anisotropy, and vertical deposition re-quire a three-dimensional inflow calculation. Ram pres-sure can perturb a tenuous surface layer even when the inflow supplies too little energy to stir the full disk col-umn; Figure 7 keeps those two limits separate. The plotted H α-derived planetary accretion rates are only an empirical comparison and determine neither the CPD-boundary inflow nor the mechanically coupled power. The reduced calculation instead ties the forcing to the torque-depleted common-gap supply. As that finite reservoir is exhausted, the affected layer retreats toward the surface and the fluctuations decay at every height, as shown in Figures 7 and 8 and derived in Appendix A. 

7. IMPLICATIONS FOR REGULAR-SATELLITE FORMATION 7.1. The solids-enhanced minimum-mass model and the PDS 70 comparison 

The solids-enhanced minimum-mass model, submit-ted in 2001 and subsequently published by Mosqueira & Estrada (2003a,b), describes regular-satellite formation in an extended, quiescent circumplanetary disk with in-ner and outer satellite-forming regions; later develop-ments are given by Estrada et al. (2009); Mosqueira et al. (2010b). Its solids constraint is the mass contained in the satellite-forming disk. Most of that solid mate-rial is ultimately incorporated into the regular satellites, so their masses constrain the solids inventory that the disk must have contained. The gas content is then re-duced relative to that solids inventory so that satellite survival, thermal constraints, and the observed compo-sitional structure can be satisfied. “Solids enhanced” means that the moon-forming disk has a larger solids-to-gas ratio than an unprocessed solar-composition mix-ture. 7.2. Satellite assembly, disk clearing, and the age of PDS 70 Constraints from PDS 70 c and SR 12 c 13 In the solids-enhanced minimum-mass model, the satellite-formation timescale is not set by a local accretion-rate formula. It is tied to the time required for gas drag to clear the circumplanetary disk of the satel-litesimal population available for satellite growth. For the modeled circumjovian and circum-Saturnian disks, this argument gives formation timescales of ∼ 10 6 yr for Callisto and ∼ 10 7 yr for Iapetus (Mosqueira & Estrada 2003a,b; Estrada et al. 2009; Mosqueira et al. 2010b). These values are outputs of the gas-drag clearing calcu-lation, not evaluations of a local accretion formula. The 5.4 ± 1.0 Myr age of PDS 70 lies between these two formation timescales. The secure continuum source at c and the absence of a comparably secure source at b consequently favor an interpretation in which the in-ner circumplanetary reservoir has been more substan-tially processed or depleted while the outer circumplane-tary disk and circumstellar reservoir remain active. The larger Hill-scaled region and longer local orbital periods at the outer companion provide a natural reason for its satellitesimal clearing and satellite-assembly history to extend beyond that of the inner companion, thus ac-counting for the observed dichotomy. 7.3. This is the geometry used in the ex-tended solids-enhanced minimum-mass construction: a compact inner disk and an outer satellite-forming region. The radial scale also enters the satellitesimal-clearing history. The local circumplanetary orbital period in-creases as r3/2, so an extended solids reservoir includes an outer region with longer dynamical, drag-clearing, and aggregation times. The continuum-inferred mass and scale, together with the separately model-dependent SR 12 c gas inventory, support an extended, solids-enhanced moon-forming en-vironment. They do not by themselves identify its sup-ply history; the gap-controlled inflow calculation de-veloped here applies specifically to the PDS 70 b–c common-gap architecture. A recent optically thick ring fit changes the inferred radial morphology, not this con-clusion. 7.6. Gas-to-dust uncertainty and planetesimal-fragment delivery 

The CPD gas mass is not presently known well enough to assign a reliable gas-to-dust ratio. This leaves open the planetesimal collisional model developed by Estrada & Mosqueira (2006), in which a large fraction of the cir-cumplanetary mass resides in solids and the observable dust is continually replenished by fragments generated in a giant-planet-induced collisional cascade. In that case PDS 70 c remains continuum bright because plan-etesimal delivery is still active, while PDS 70 b may have exhausted or incorporated more of its accessible solids. The observable grain population can be supplied by di-rect dust or pebble inflow, collisional fragmentation, and ablation of disk-crossing bodies, and depleted through incorporation into satellitesimals and satellites, capture by the planet, or ejection. The continuum constrains only grains that contribute appreciably to the opacity; it does not directly count kilometer-scale planetesimals or already formed moons. In the solids-enhanced minimum-mass model, abla-tion of icy and rocky planetesimal fragments crossing the circumplanetary gas is a viable mechanism for en-riching the satellite-forming disk in solids (Mosqueira & Estrada 2003a; Mosqueira et al. 2010b; Ronnet & Johansen 2020). It also supplies the leading explana-tion for the ice-rich composition of Iapetus relative to other regular satellites and outer Solar System objects (Mosqueira et al. 2010a). It is important to note that the two limiting con-tinuum interpretations lead to the same conclusion. If the emission is optically thin, it measures an instanta-neous grain mass comparable to a major regular satel-lite. If it is optically thick, it identifies a high-column region where fragmentation, drift, concentration, and satellitesimal formation can cycle mass through the ob-14 Mosqueira

CONCLUSIONS The PDS 70 system connects two forming giant plan-ets, a common large-scale gap, and a directly observed circumplanetary solids reservoir; SR 12 c supplies the second bound planetary-mass disk. Together they con-strain giant-planet growth and satellite formation with the same measurements. 1. The compact continuum source at PDS 70 c is a moon-forming circumplanetary reservoir. It is spatially associated with the accreting planet, lies inside its Hill sphere, and has been recovered at multiple ALMA frequencies and epochs. 2. In the optically thin limit, the 855-μm flux implies 

0.007 –0.031 M⊕ of dust for the discovery-analysis opacities at 26 K, comparable to Callisto. In the optically thick limit, the flux requires a minimum equivalent projected radius of 0.46 –0.52 au, or a minimum physical radius of 0.58 –0.66 au if the CPD is coplanar; the alternative values correspond to the adopted 26- and 22-K temperatures. The local dust column satisfies τ855 ≳ 1, and the obser-vational upper limit is about 1.2 au. Either limit describes a substantial moon-forming solids reser-voir. 3. PDS 70 c is part of an emerging planetary-mass disk sequence rather than an isolated continuum source. After scaling to 140 pc, its 0.88-mm flux is 0.055 ± 0.010 mJy. With the current 139 ± 5 pc distance and 16 MJ mass adopted for SR 12 c, its scaled flux is 0.125 ± 0.017 mJy and the extrap-olated young circumstellar disk relation predicts approximately 0.12 mJy. The two detections lie close to the empirical relation and within its 0.74-dex intrinsic scatter. Their inferred dust reser-voirs are also of the same order under matched optically thin assumptions. SR 12 c has nearly completed its own mass growth, yet accretion di-agnostics establish that its disk still contains gas. Its measured ˙Mp gives tgrow = (1 .9 ± 0.5) × 10 9 yr and ∆Mp/M p = (5 .2 ± 1.5) × 10 −4 over another Myr. The detected satellite-forming circumplane-tary reservoir can therefore remain after the cen-tral object is largely assembled. 4. The CPD radial scale is determined by the an-gular momentum of the inflow. For j = λΩpR2H,

rc = λ2RH/3. Inflow through the L1/L 2 regions of a well-formed gap gives rc ∼ RH/3, or 1.4–2.0 au, on the same au scale as the observations and much larger than the compact RH/48 pre-gap circular-ization radius. The observed scale is therefore con-sistent with late-stage gap-fed inflow. 5. Gas-drag clearing of the circumplanetary satellitesimal population gives solids-enhanced minimum-mass formation timescales of ∼ 10 6 yr for Callisto and ∼ 10 7 yr for Iapetus; the 

5.4 ± 1.0 Myr system age lies between them. The evidence therefore favors an interpretation in which the circumplanetary disk around chas not yet dispersed and remains in an active satellite-forming stage, while the disk around b is substantially more depleted by comparison. 6. The satellite systems cannot be separated from the two-planet gap. Gap formation limits further planetary growth, changes the specific angular mo-mentum and deposition radius of CPD inflow, and ends the circumplanetary supply. The gas between and immediately around the two giants is a finite circumstellar reservoir. Planetary tidal torques partition this gas among the inner disk, the outer disk, and the two Hill spheres. D. METHODOLOGICAL NOTE ON INHERITED BIAS IN AI-ASSISTED SCIENTIFIC REASONING This manuscript was prepared with AI editorial assistance, and the process exposed a methodological failure worth recording. When asked to summarize the observational evidence for disk turbulence, the AI assistant repeatedly reconstructed a conventional literature framing in which turbulence was treated as the default state and the observations were asked to rule it out. It did so even after the intended evidentiary burden had been stated explicitly. This was an inherited interpretive bias produced by the repeated association, in the scientific literature used to train language models, of nonthermal line widths with turbulence, turbulence with angular-momentum transport, and angular-momentum transport with an α-disk closure.
