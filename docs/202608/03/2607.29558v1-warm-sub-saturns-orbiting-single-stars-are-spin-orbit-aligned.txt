Warm Sub-Saturns Orbiting Single Stars Are Spin-Orbit Aligned 

Xian-Yu Wang 1, ∗ and Songhu Wang 1

> 1Department of Astronomy, Indiana University, 727 East 3rd Street, Bloomington, IN 47405-7105, USA

ABSTRACT In this work, we show that warm sub-Saturns orbiting single stars are predominantly aligned, in contrast to hot sub-Saturns, which are frequently misaligned, with the two populations differing at the 3.2 σ level. Because both populations are observed around cool stars, they are free from the ambiguity introduced by the Teff − λ dependence. Together with the established alignment of warm Jupiters, this demonstrates, among single-star systems, that spin-orbit misalignment arises specifically in the close-in “hot-Jupiter-analog” regime, where tidal circularization is efficient ( τe < τ age ) and high-eccentricity migration is expected to operate. We further find that the transition between aligned and misaligned sub-Saturns occurs at wider orbital separations ( afinal /R p ∼ 340) than for Jupiters ( afinal /R p ∼ 120), consistent with the expectation that the lower masses (smaller Mp/M ∗) and stronger tidal dissipation (lower Qp) of sub-Saturns allow them to be circularized into wider final orbits within their lifetimes. Taken together, these results provide the clearest direct evidence to date that, in single-star systems, spin-orbit misalignments are produced by high-eccentricity migration. If this framework is correct, spin-orbit misalignments may also emerge among hot-Jupiter analogs in other mass regimes, including hot brown dwarfs around hot stars at afinal /R p ≲ 100 and isolated hot super-Earths at afinal /R p ≲ 1000, with the corresponding transition locations shifted by the dependence of the orbital-circularization timescale on Mp/M ∗ and Qp.

1. INTRODUCTION One of the strongest current lines of evidence that spin-orbit misalignments originate from high-eccentricity migration is that hot Jupiters are frequently misaligned (K. C. Schlaufman 2010; J. N. Winn et al. 2010a; J. N. Winn & D. C. Fabrycky 2015; A. H. M. J. Triaud 2018; S. H. Albrecht et al. 2022), whereas warm Jupiters around single stars are typically aligned (M. Rice et al. 2022b; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2024; J. I. Espinoza-Retamal et al. 2025). As recently pointed out by D.-H. Wu et al. 2023 and J. Esposito et al. 2026, this con-trast is naturally expected if spin-orbit misalignments are generated by the same dynamical processes that

P. Kraft 1967; A. C. Beyer & R. J. White 2024; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2026). Consequently, while primordial spin-orbit mis-alignment has been investigated in a variety of contexts (e.g., compact multiplanet systems: S. Albrecht et al. 2013 and T. D. Morton & J. N. Winn 2014; star-disk sys-tems: C. A. Watson et al. 2011, C. L. Davies 2019, and L. I. Biddle et al. 2025), current Rossiter-McLaughlin measurements of Jupiter-mass planets alone do not yet distinguish whether the misalignments observed in hot Jupiters around hot stars reflect primordial hot-star mis-alignment or instead trace high-eccentricity migration, whose obliquity imprint may persist because tidal re-alignment is inefficient in hot stars (J. N. Winn et al. 2010a; S. Albrecht et al. 2012; S. H. Albrecht et al. 2022; E. Knudstrup et al. 2024; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2024). The stellar obliquity distribution of sub-Saturns helps clarify this picture. The discovery that sub-Saturns around cool stars can still be strongly misaligned (e.g., HAT-P-11, J. N. Winn et al. 2010b; T. Hirano et al. 2011; R. Sanchis-Ojeda & J. N. Winn 2011; D. Dem-ing et al. 2011; V. Bourrier et al. 2023; HATS-38, J. I. Espinoza-Retamal et al. 2024; GJ 436, V. Bourrier et al. 2018, 2022; GJ 3470, G. Stef` ansson et al. 2022; TOI-1842, K. Hixenbaugh et al. 2023; TOI-2374, S. W. Yee et al. 2025; WASP-107, F. Dai & J. N. Winn 2017; R. A. Rubenzahl et al. 2021; V. Bourrier et al. 2023; TOI-1135, E. Dugan et al. 2025) argues against the idea that misalignment is an outcome unique to hot stars. More-over, recent work has shown that isolated sub-Saturn systems are more likely to be misaligned, whereas those in compact multi-planet systems are typically aligned (e.g., S. Albrecht et al. 2013; S. Wang et al. 2018; G. Zhou et al. 2018; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2022; J. Lubin et al. 2023; E. Y. Zhang et al. 2025; B. T. Radzom et al. 2024, 2025; L. B. Handley et al. 2026), pointing to a dynam-ical rather than primordial origin for sub-Saturn stel-lar obliquities. However, while these clues favor a dy-namical origin, they do not by themselves demonstrate that high-eccentricity migration is the responsible mech-anism. In this work, we show that warm sub-Saturns orbit-ing single stars are predominantly aligned, in contrast to hot sub-Saturns, which are frequently misaligned. Be-cause both populations are observed around cool stars, they are free from the ambiguity introduced by the 

Teff − λ dependence. Together with the established alignment of warm Jupiters, this demonstrates, among single-star systems, that spin-orbit misalignment arises specifically in the close-in “hot-Jupiter-analog” regime, where tidal circularization is efficient ( τe < τ age ) and high-eccentricity migration is expected to operate. Goldre-ich & S. Soter 1966; P. Hut 1981; F. C. Adams & G. Laughlin 2006; B. Jackson et al. 2008). Taken together, these results provide the clearest direct evidence to date that, in single-star systems, spin-orbit misalignments are produced by high-eccentricity migration.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS 3.1. afinal /R p as a Metric for Misalignment Origin 

The conventional distinction between ‘hot’ and ‘warm’ planets in stellar obliquity studies is typically based on the scaled orbital separation, a/R ∗, with a boundary around a/R ∗ ∼ 8–10 (e.g., S. Albrecht et al. 2012; M. Rice et al. 2022b; J. J. Zanazzi et al. 2024; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2024). This criterion is motivated by tidal realign-ment theory, in which the alignment timescale depends sensitively on orbital separation, scaling approximately as ( a/R ∗)6 for cool stars with convective envelopes and (a/R ∗)17 /2 for hot stars with radiative envelopes (J.-P. Zahn 1977; S. Albrecht et al. 2012; D. Lai 2012; G. Li & J. N. Winn 2016). In this framework, a/R ∗ serves as a proxy for the efficiency of tidal realignment, and thus primarily traces the post-migration evolution of stellar obliquity. However, for sub-Saturns, due to their lower plane-tary masses, post-migration stellar obliquities are only weakly affected by tidal realignment, as the tides they raise on their host stars are much weaker than those of Jovian planets. As a result, they are more likely to retain their primordial stellar obliquities and thus more directly trace their dynamical histories. If large stellar obliquities in single-star systems are predomi-nantly generated by high-eccentricity migration (e.g., F. A. Rasio & E. B. Ford 1996; S. J. Weidenschilling & F. Marzari 1996; Y. Wu & N. Murray 2003; D. Fab-rycky & S. Tremaine 2007; S. Naoz 2016; Y. Wu & Y. Lithwick 2011; C. Petrovich 2015; M. Rice et al. 2022a; D.-H. Wu et al. 2023; Y. Kawai et al. 2025; J. Esposito et al. 2026), then the key quantity becomes whether such migration can efficiently operate. High-eccentricity mi-gration requires sufficient tidal dissipation to circularize initially eccentric orbits, with a circularization timescale 

τcirc ∝ (afinal /R p)5, where afinal = a(1 − e2) is the final circularized semi-major axis assuming approximate con-servation of orbital angular momentum (P. Goldreich & S. Soter 1966; P. Hut 1981; F. C. Adams & G. Laughlin 2006; B. Jackson et al. 2008). In this sense, afinal /R p

serves as a proxy for the efficiency of high-eccentricity migration (see Equation 1 of F. C. Adams & G. Laughlin 2006). Therefore, we adopt afinal /R p as the primary coordi-nate for empirically identifying the transition between hot and warm regimes in the observed stellar obliq-uity distribution. In this framework, systems at small

3.2. Derivation of Empirical Boundaries 

Figure 1 shows the afinal /R p − λ distribution, illus-trating a clear transition from misaligned hot planets to aligned warm planets in both the Jupiter and sub-Saturn populations. To determine the afinal /R p boundary sep-arating misaligned and aligned systems, we evaluated the p-values from two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (K-S; J. L. Hodges 1958) tests for a set of candidate cuts defined at the midpoints between adjacent unique values of afinal /R p.For each candidate cut, we compared the |λ| distribu-tions of systems with afinal /R p smaller than the cut and those with afinal /R p larger than or equal to the cut us-ing the two-sample K-S test as implemented in SciPy 

(P. Virtanen et al. 2020). We estimated the bound-ary location and its uncertainty through a bootstrap analysis. In each of 100,000 realizations, we resampled the systems with replacement and independently per-turbed both afinal /R p and |λ| according to their reported measurement uncertainties. We then repeated the two-sample K-S analysis to determine the optimal cut. The resulting distribution of optimal cuts exhibits multiple peaks, reflecting the discrete and uneven sampling of systems in afinal /R p. We adopt the highest-afinal /R p

peak, which corresponds to the outer boundary of the observed misaligned population, and derive the bound-ary value and its uncertainty from this peak. We applied the boundary derivation procedure to the Jupiter and sub-Saturn samples, obtaining afinal /R p =117 ± 9 and afinal /R p = 338 ± 27, respectively. 3.3. Alignment Significance of Warm Sub-Saturns 

To quantify the significance of the alignment ten-dency among warm sub-Saturns, we construct a refer-ence distribution under the null hypothesis that warm sub-Saturns share the same obliquity distribution as hot sub-Saturns. We randomly draw eight sky-projected obliquity measurements without replacement from the hot sub-Saturn sample, equal to the total number of warm sub-Saturn systems with RM measurements, all of which are aligned. For each realization, we count the number of systems that satisfy the misalignment crite-rion ( |λ| − 3σλ > 0 and |λ| > 10 ◦; M. Rice et al. 2022b; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2024; L. B. Handley et al. 2026). This procedure is repeated 100 ,000 times to generate the ex-pected distribution of misaligned systems under the null hypothesis. We approximate this distribution with a Gaussian characterized by mean μ = 4.01 and standard deviation σ = 1.24. The observed number of misaligned warm sub-Saturn systems is zero, placing the sample 6 Wang et al. 

(μ − 0) /σ ≃ 3.2 σ below the expectation of the null dis-tribution. We perform the same analysis for the Jupiter sam-ple and find that the alignment tendency of single-star warm Jupiters is significant at the 4.2 σ level. Across our sample, misaligned systems are concen-trated in the hot-Jupiter and hot sub-Saturn regimes. In contrast, neither more massive companions (includ-ing super-Jupiters and brown dwarfs) 5 nor small plan-ets (including sub-Neptunes, super-Earths, and smaller planets) show clear evidence of spin-orbit misalignment around single stars in the analogous short-period regime. We argue that such systems may not yet have been un-covered observationally. For massive companions, J. Rusznak et al. (2025) found that the alignment tendency holds regardless of orbital separation, and suggested that such ob-jects may lack additional nearby companions capable of triggering dynamical instability (W. Xu & S. Wang 2024). However, systems hosting multiple compact mas-sive companions are rare but do exist. For exam-ple, TIC 279401253 (V. SUMMARY We find that warm sub-Saturns orbiting single stars are predominantly aligned, in contrast to hot sub-Saturns, which are frequently misaligned, with the two populations differing at the 3.2 σ level. Because both populations are found around cool stars, they are free from the ambiguity introduced by the Teff − λ de-pendence (K. C. Schlaufman 2010; J. N. Winn et al. 2010a; S. Albrecht et al. 2012). Together with a simi-lar separation-dependent stellar obliquity transition that has previously been observed for Jupiter-mass plan-ets (M. Rice et al. 2022b; X.-Y. Wang et al. 2024), 8 Wang et al. 

this demonstrates, among single-star systems, that spin-orbit misalignment arises specifically in the close-in “hot-Jupiter-analog” regime, where tidal circularization is efficient ( τe < τ age ) and high-eccentricity migration is expected to operate. We further find that the transition between aligned and misaligned sub-Saturns occurs at wider orbital separations ( afinal /R p = 338 ± 27) than for Jupiters (afinal /R p = 117 ± 9), consistent with the expectation that the lower masses (smaller Mp/M ∗) and stronger tidal dissipation (lower Qp) of sub-Saturns allow them to be circularized into wider final orbits within their life-times (P. Goldreich & S. Soter 1966; P. Hut 1981; F. C. Adams & G. Laughlin 2006). If this framework is correct, then spin-orbit misalign-ments should also emerge among hot-Jupiter analogs in other mass regimes, including hot brown dwarfs around hot stars at afinal /R p ≲ 100 and isolated hot super-Earths at afinal /R p ≲ 1000, with corresponding but shifted transition locations reflecting their different tidal properties.
