## 摘要
我们报告了对绕富金属F8 IV-V亚巨星TOI-5646（V=11.38等；T=6136 K）运行的TESS凌星行星候选体的确认。通过HARPS-N后续径向速度测量，我们确认了TOI-5646b的行星属性。我们确定该行星的轨道周期为2.4277天，轨道偏心率与零相容。凭借45.9地球质量的质量、6.6地球半径的半径以及1935 K的平衡温度，它是海王星沙漠中迄今发现的最热的超级海王星系外行星之一。其密度（0.87 g cm^-3）低于大多数其他沙漠居民，但似乎仍然符合沙漠中的行星比山脊和热带草原中的行星更致密的趋势。母星（[Fe/H]=0.32）的金属丰度符合这一趋势：沙漠和山脊中海王星大小系外行星的宿主恒星通常比热带草原中的更富含金属。母星的log g（4.09）表明宿主是一颗演化恒星，这使得该系统颇为特殊：TOI-5646是迄今发现的在沙漠中拥有海王星大小行星的最大恒星（1.8太阳半径）。内部结构建模显示TOI-5646b高度富含金属，推断其整体金属丰度在0.7至0.95之间。大气演化模拟表明，该行星在其宿主恒星的强烈XUV辐照下经历了显著的质量损失（6%-16%）。这些结果表明，其特殊的形成历史（可能涉及早期原行星盘消散）、行星碰撞或晚期潮汐摧毁事件，可以解释其在海王星沙漠中作为低密度幸存者的当前状态。

## Abstract
We report the confirmation of the TESS transiting-planet candidate orbiting the metal-rich F8\,IV-V subgiant star TOI-5646 (V=11.38 mag; T=6136 K). The planetary nature of TOI-5646b was confirmed by means of HARPS-N follow-up radial velocity measurements. We determined that the planet has an orbital period of 2.4277 days and an orbital eccentricity compatible with zero. With a mass of 45.9 Earth masses, a radius of 6.6 Earth radii, and an equilibrium temperature of 1935 K, it is one of the hottest super-Neptune exoplanets ever discovered in the Neptune desert. Its density, 0.87 g cm^-3, is lower than that of most other desert dwellers, but still appears to follow the trend that the desert is populated by dense planets than those in the ridge and savanna. The metallicity of the parent star ([Fe/H]=0.32) follows the trend whereby the host stars of Neptune-sized exoplanets in the desert and the ridge are generally richer in metal than those in the savanna. The log{g} of the parent star (4.09) suggests that the host is an evolved star, making this a peculiar system: TOI-5646 is the largest star (1.8 Solar radii) ever discovered to host a Neptune-sized planet in the desert. Internal structure modelling revealed that TOI-5646b is highly enriched in metals, with an inferred bulk metallicity between 0.7 and 0.95. Atmospheric evolution simulations indicate that the planet has undergone significant mass loss (6-16%) through intense XUV irradiation from its host star. These results indicate a peculiar formation history (potentially involving early protoplanetary disk dispersal), a planetary collision, or a late tidal destruction event, to explain its current state as a low-density survivor in the Neptune desert.
