ABSTRACT 

Small exoplanets on close-in orbits are exposed to high levels of stellar irradiation, which can e ff ectively remove low-mass primordial H /He envelopes through atmospheric escape. Under these conditions, planets whose masses and radii are incompatible with both bare rocky interiors and extended H-dominated atmospheres are therefore expected to host high mean molecular weight atmospheres, making them promising candidates for volatile-rich hot water worlds. We present the first detailed characterisation of the low-density super-Earth TOI-2211 b (P ∼3.09 d), a recently validated transiting planet orbiting a K type star (G mag: 9.1). Combining TESS and CHEOPS photometry with radial velocity measurements from HARPS, ESPRESSO, and KPF, we precisely constrain the planet’s radius and mass to 1 .457 ± 0.047 R ⊕ and 2 .69 ± 0.56 M ⊕. We also identify an outer, non-transiting planetary candidate (P ∼15.6 d, Msin(i) = 8.53 ±1.04 M ⊕) in the radial velocity data. We investigate the internal structure of the transiting planet and assess the stability of the inferred volatile envelopes through a detailed evaporation analysis, thereby identifying TOI-2211 b as a promising hot water world candidate. TOI-2211 b therefore joins the small but growing sample of these close-in low-density super-Earths, which represent particularly interesting targets for testing current planet formation and evolution theories. Additionally, we investigate the full architecture of the planetary system using a generative model of planet formation and evolution, with the resulting posterior distributions showing a clear over-density consistent with the observed orbital period and minimal mass of the non-transiting planetary candidate identified in the radial velocity data.

1. Introduction 

Planets with radii between the ones of Earth and Neptune do not have an analogue in our Solar system, but are among the most commonly detected in our galaxy (Mayor et al. 2011; Fressin et al. 2013; Hsu et al. 2019; Kunimoto & Matthews 2020). Indeed, roughly 60% of all confirmed planets listed by the NASA exoplanet archive fall into this category. 1 The dis-tribution of these planets’ radii shows a distinct bimodality, with the smaller super-Earths separated from the larger sub-Neptunes by the so-called radius valley (Fulton et al. 2017, for orbital periods smaller than ∼100 days). tion valley was previously predicted based on theoretical mod-els by several independent groups (Owen & Wu 2013; Jin et al. 2014; Lopez & Fortney 2014), all assuming sub-Neptunes to host H /He envelopes. Since then, models based on this assump-tion have been shown to accurately reproduce the observational properties of the radius valley well (e.g. Owen & Wu 2017; Fulton & Petigura 2018; Van Eylen et al. 2018; Mordasini 2020; Ho & Van Eylen 2023; Bonfanti et al. 2024; Ho et al. 2024). In more recent years, this traditional picture of sub-Neptunes with H /He envelopes has, however, been challenged, as many interior compositions are compatible with their observed mass and radius values (Valencia et al. 2007; Seager et al. 2007; Rogers & Seager 2010; Zeng et al. 2019). For instance, planet formation models predict the existence of a large number

of water-rich planets on short orbital periods, having formed outside the water ice line and then migrated inwards (e.g. Ward 1997; Tanaka et al. 2002; Emsenhuber et al. 2021a,b, 2023). Multiple studies have shown that such water-rich sub-Neptunes can explain the observed location of the radius valley (Venturini et al. 2020a,b; Izidoro et al. 2022; Burn et al. 2024; Venturini et al. 2024; Chakrabarty et al. 2026; Shibata & Izidoro 2026). Other works also proposed that, from a formation per-spective, some of these planets should be carbon-rich soot plan-ets (Bergin et al. 2023; Lin & Seager 2025; Li et al. 2026). In addition, interior-atmosphere interactions have been pro-posed as a mechanism to alter the primordial atmospheric composition of these planets. For example, volatiles can be partitioned into the interior and later on outgassed by the cooling magma ocean, a process that may also lead to the endogenic production of water (e.g. Kite et al. 2020; Dorn & Lichtenberg 2021; Schlichting & Young 2022; Seo et al. 2024; Rigby et al. 2024; Luo et al. 2024; Bower et al. 2025; Ito et al. 2025; Werlen et al. 2025; Steinmeyer et al. 2026). Mul-tiple studies have also investigated how such interactions could shape the compositions of planetary atmospheres across the ra-dius valley, resulting in a broad range of atmospheric compo-sitions. More specifically, Heng et al. (2025) reported a gradi-ent of mean molecular weight across the radius valley, while Cherubim et al. (2025) found an oxidation gradient across the valley, with prominent populations of He- and O 2-dominated atmospheres on close-in planets. Woitke et al. (2021) derive a chemical classification for the composition of planetary atmo-spheres below 600 K, with a particular focus on the co-existence of CH 4, CO 2, and H 2O. With the advent of JWST (Gardner et al. 2006), it has now become possible to probe the atmospheric properties of some super-Earths and sub-Neptunes observationally, with the small planets observed so far spanning a broad range of bulk proper-ties and equilibrium temperatures. While many results remain tentative and further observations are needed, the first studies indicate a large diversity of atmospheric compositions, as sum-marised for example in Madhusudhan et al. (2025) or Roy et al. (2026). For several planets, the observed spectra show features consistent with molecules such as H 2O, CO 2, and CH 4. This was interpreted as evidence for a range of atmospheric com-positions, from H 2-dominated atmospheres with di ff erent en-richment levels (e.g. K2-18 b, Madhusudhan et al. 2023; TOI-270 d, Benneke et al. 2024; Holmberg & Madhusudhan 2024; TOI-421 b, Davenport et al. 2025) to steam-dominated atmo-spheres (GJ 9827 d, Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. 2024). However, other planets showcase flat, featureless spectra, which can ei-ther be explained by the presence of clouds and hazes or by a high mean molecular weight atmosphere (e.g. TOI-836 c, Wallack et al. 2024; HD 15337 c, Wallack et al. 2026). For a specific subset of planets, some of the intrinsic de-generacy of their internal structure and composition can al-ready be lifted based solely on their measured bulk den-sity. These planets are on orbits close to their host star and receive high levels of stellar irradiation, which can cause part of their atmospheres to evaporate (Owen & Wu 2017; Lopez 2017; Jin & Mordasini 2018; Kubyshkina & Vidotto 2021). H/He atmospheres are more susceptible to this at-mospheric escape due to their low mean molecular weight than heavier, volatile-enriched atmospheres (Johnstone et al. 2015; Yoshida et al. 2022, 2024; García Muñoz et al. 2024; Yoshida & Gaidos 2025; Kubyshkina et al. 2026). As a result, planets that are less dense than a bare rocky core but denser than those with a Mercury-like core and a ∼1% H /He enve-lope need to contain volatiles heavier than H /He in their en-velopes. Similar arguments have previously been made in other works, for example for π Men c (García Muñoz et al. 2020, 2021), GJ 9827 d (Piaulet-Ghorayeb et al. 2024), Kepler-138 d (Piaulet et al. 2023), and HD 219134 b (Dorn & Heng 2018; Kubyshkina et al. 2018; Ligi et al. 2019; Gupta & Schlichting 2021), as well as in Castro-González et al. (2023) for a popu-lation of low-density super-Earths. This motivates our CHEOPS (CHaracterising ExOPlanet Satellite; Benz et al. 2021; Fortier et al. 2024) guaranteed time observation (GTO) programme (Hot Water Worlds, CH_PR140068), which aims to identify new planets located in these sparsely populated regions of the mass-radius diagram. We refer to these regions as the Hot Water World triangles. In the first paper of this series, Egger et al. (2025), we intro-duced the concept behind our programme in more detail and presented the observational results for its first two targets, TOI-238 b and TOI-1685 b. In this second paper, we now present the first detailed characterisation of the recently validated transit-ing low-density super-Earth TOI-2211 b using data from TESS, CHEOPS, HARPS, ESPRESSO, and KPF, along with an addi-tional radial velocity planetary candidate orbiting TOI-2211. We present a summary of the observations used in this paper in Section 2, perform a characterisation of the host star TOI-2211 in Section 3, and model the planetary signals in the photo-metric and RV data in Section 4. In Section 5, we then use the derived planetary properties to model the internal structure and potential atmospheric evolution of TOI-2211 b. In the same sec-tion, we also investigate additional constraints on the new planet candidate identified in the radial velocity data, studying transit constraints from TESS and modelling the dynamical stability of a two-planet system. We also attempt to infer the broader archi-tecture of the planetary system using a generative model based on the Bern model of planet formation and evolution. Finally, we provide a summary of our work and draw conclusions in Sec-tion 6. 

2. Observations 

> 2.1. TESS

The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS; Ricker et al. 2015) first observed TOI-2211 in July 2020 as part of sector 27. Later on, TOI-2211 was reobserved by TESS in sectors 67, 92, and 95. A summary of these TESS observations is given in Ta-ble A.1, including the cadence of the observations, the camera and CCD TOI-2211 was observed in, and how many transits of TOI-2211 b are covered by each sector. In the following, we used the Pre-search Data Conditioning Simple Aperture Photom-etry (PDCSAP) photometry of all four available TESS sectors (Smith et al. 2012; Stumpe et al. 2012, 2014), obtained through the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST). 

> 2.2. CHEOPS

In addition to the available TESS photometry, we obtained eight visits of TOI-2211 with the CHaracterising ExOplanet Satellite (CHEOPS; Benz et al. 2021; Fortier et al. 2024) as part of the Guaranteed Time Observations (GTO) programme ‘Hot Water Worlds’ (CH_PR140068), spanning a total of 68.5 hours be-tween 2024-06-17 and 2025-08-24. The goal of these additional observations was to refine the planetary radius of TOI-2211 b, a strategy which has previously been shown to be successful due to the higher photometric precision of CHEOPS compared to

> 2.3. HARPS

TOI-2211 was additionally observed with the High Accuracy Radial Velocity Planet Searcher (HARPS; Pepe et al. 2000), which is mounted at the 3.6 m telescope at the ESO La Silla Ob-servatory in Chile. HARPS is a fibre-fed, high-resolution spec-trograph with a resolving power of R = 115 000. The observa-tions were carried out between 2024-07-07 and 2024-09-27 as part of the ESO program with ID 113.26G5 (PI: Egger). The exposure time was 1200 s for all the observations. We gath-ered 19 spectra with an average signal-to-noise at 550 nm of 41, and airmass at the beginning of the observations ranging be-tween 1.0 and 1.2. The spectra were reduced with the standard HARPS data reduction software v3.3.6 (DRS; Pepe et al. 2002; Lovis & Pepe 2007) and the RVs were computed applying the cross-correlation technique using a G9 mask. The HARPS RV time series, shown in the bottom left panel of Figure 4, has a mean RV precision of 2.4 m /s and a RV scatter of 3.4 m /s. 

> 2.4. ESPRESSO

The Echelle SPectrograph for Rocky Exoplanets and Stable Spectroscopic Observations (ESPRESSO; Pepe et al. 2014) is a ultra-stable, high-resolution, fibre-fed spectrograph mounted on the 8.2 m Very Large Telescope (VLT) at Paranal Observatory in Chile. We gathered 30 additional spectra of TOI-2211 with ESPRESSO between 2025-05-05 and 2025-07-26, as part of the ESO program with ID 115.2842 (PI: Egger). All the observa-tions had an exposure time of 600 s. The average signal-to-noise at 550 nm is 78 and the airmass at the beginning of the observa-tions ranged between 1.0 and 1.5. The spectra were reduced with the standard ESPRESSO pipeline v3.3.10 (Pepe et al. 2021) and the RVs were computed with the cross-correlation technique us-ing a G9 mask. The ESPRESSO RV time series, shown in the bottom left panel of Figure 4, has a mean RV precision of 0.6 m/s and a RV scatter of 2.5 m /s.

3. Host star characterisation 

The stellar spectroscopic parameters ( Teff , log g, [Fe /H]) were estimated using the ARES +MOOG methodology, as described in more detail in Santos et al. (2013), Sousa (2014), and Sousa et al. (2021). For this purpose, we used the latest version of ARES 3 (Sousa et al. 2007, 2015) to measure the equivalent widths (EW) of iron lines in a combined HARPS spectrum of TOI-2211. We adopted the list of FeI and FeII lines presented in Tsantaki et al. (2013), which is more suitable for stars with temperatures about 5200 K and below. A minimization procedure was applied to impose ionization and excitation equilibrium, allowing us to converge to the best set of spectroscopic parameters. For this step, we used a grid of Kurucz model atmospheres (Kurucz 1993) together with the radiative transfer code MOOG (Sneden 1973). In this analysis, we also derived a more accurate trigonomet-ric surface gravity using Gaia DR3 data, following the proce-dure described in Sousa et al. (2021). Stellar abundances were determined using the classical curve of growth method under the assumption of local thermodynamic equilibrium, employing the same codes and model atmospheres as for the stellar param-eter determination. For the derivation of chemical abundances of Mg and Si, we closely followed the methods described in Adibekyan et al. (2012, 2015). We used a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) modi-fied infrared flux method (IRFM – Blackwell & Shallis 1977; Schanche et al. 2020) to determine the stellar radius of TOI-2211. This was done by extracting synthetic photometry from a spectral energy distribution we constructed using stellar atmosphere models (Castelli & Kurucz 2003) and our spectroscopically derived stellar parameters, that we com-pared to observed broadband fluxes in the following band-passes: 2MASS J, H, and K, WISE W1 and W2, and Gaia G, GBP , and GRP (Skrutskie et al. 2006; Wright et al. 2010; Gaia Collaboration et al. 2023). From this approach we retrieved the stellar bolometric flux that we converted into the e ff ective temperature and stellar radius by using the o ff set-corrected Gaia 

parallax (Lindegren et al. 2021). The e ff ective temperature Teff , metallicity [Fe /H], and ra-dius R⋆ along with their uncertainties constituted the input set to compute the isochronal mass M⋆ and age t⋆ using two di ff erent stellar evolutionary models. In detail, we applied the isochrone placement routine (Bonfanti et al. 2015, 2016) and its capabil-ity of interpolating the input set within pre-computed grids of PARSEC 4 v1.2S (Marigo et al. 2017) isochrones and tracks to infer a first pair of estimates for M⋆ and t⋆, 0 .812 ± 0.040 M⊙

and 7 .7 ± 4.8 Gyr. A second pair of mass and age values was computed using the CLES (Code Liègeois d’Évolution Stel-laire; Scuflaire et al. 2008) code, that builds the best-fit evo-lutionary track accounting for the input observables follow-ing the Levenberg-Marquadt optimisation scheme (Salmon et al. 2021), 0 .797 ± 0.055 M⊙ and 13 .7 ± 8.0 Gyr. We analysed the periodicities of the activity indicators from the HARPS and ESPRESSO spectra to determine the stellar rotation period using the Generalised Lomb-Scargle (GLS) periodogram (Zechmeister & Kürster 2009). The periodograms of the avail-able activity indicators are shown in Figure 1. We do not find sig-nificant periodicities with false alarm probability (FAP; Baluev 2008) below 10% in the BIS, CCF Contrast, S index, H α or Ca II. The FWHM shows two periodicities with low FAP of log 10 (FAP) = −9.02 and −7.28 at 82 and 41 days, respectively. We note that the significant periodicity at 82 days is a broad, not well constrained peak. The periodogram of the Na I line also shows a significant periodicity around 41 days. The low frequency periodicities observed in many of the ac-tivity indicators’ periodograms, although of low significance, are likely to be an artefact driven by the large gap of ∼250 days between the HARPS and ESPRESSO observing seasons. Our current dataset does not allow us to resolve the stellar rotation period between 41 or 82 days because of their harmonic rela-tionship. Moreover, there is a sparse coverage of the 82 days signal, making it challenging to robustly confirm it as the stellar rotation period. We therefore adopt 41 days as a plausible stellar rotation period, which is supported by gyrochronology, for the purposes of interpreting the activity diagnostics. However, we note that the current data do not uniquely determine the rotation period; values near 66 − 82 days remain possible and additional RVs and activity monitoring would be required to distinguish between these alternatives. 

4. Modelling the planetary signals 

TOI-2211.01, a planetary candidate with an orbital period of 3.09 days, was first identified as a transiting planet candidate in December 2020 in the TESS data. A statistical validation of TOI-2211.01 was then first performed by Crossfield et al. (2025) using TRICERATOPS (Giacalone et al. 2021), classifying it as a likely planet. Later on, Lafarga et al. (2026) validated TOI-2211.01 as part of their automatic search for transiting plan-ets in TESS-SPOC full frame images with the RAVEN pipeline (Hadjigeorghiou et al. 2025). They also identified an additional transiting planet candidate at an orbital period of 6.88 days, which, however, is not validated by their pipeline.

4.2. Joint photometric and RV fit 

We use the open-source code chexoplanet 5 (e.g. Egger et al. 2024) to jointly model the available TESS and CHEOPS data, as well as the RVs from multiple instruments. To reduce the im-pact of systematics, chexoplanet samples multiple systematic and transit models simultaneously. The first part is the linear and quadratic decorrelation of CHEOPS flux with respect to various hyperparameters, both on a per-visit and global basis. Initially each individual CHEOPS light curve is fitted including decorre-lation with normalised parameters of the x and y centroid posi-tion, three aliases of the cosine and sine of the roll-angle, major residuals of the PSF fit, and time. Additionally on-board temper-ature and background flux are included, but split into high- and low-frequency signals using a spline with ∼10 min knot spacing to separate trends from jitter and noise. Bayesian model compar-

As a second step, we then determine the similarity of these decorrelation parameters across all observations using a leave-one-out comparison. Significant linear and quadratic parameters are then included in the global model. Furthermore, chexoplanet co-models shorter-frequency variations in the flux as a function of the spacecraft roll an-gle using a cubic spline (6.5 degree knot spacing). 5. Discussion 

5.1. Analysing the internal structure of TOI-2211 b 

Based on the observational bulk properties of TOI-2211 b de-rived in Section 4, we now analyse the implications this has for the composition and internal structure of this planet. As a first step, we study the location of TOI-2211 b in a mass-radius dia-gram as depicted in Figure 5, together with a number of mass-radius relations generated with the BICEPS planetary structure model (Haldemann et al. 2024). The Hot Water World triangle as introduced in Egger et al. (2025) that corresponds to the equi-librium temperature of TOI-2211 b is marked in light blue. Similarly to TOI-238 b (studied in Paper I; Egger et al. 2025), we find that at the 1 σ level, TOI-2211 b falls just above the iso-composition line for a bare rocky planetary core with-out any iron content. For a planet to lie above this line would imply that its observed mean density cannot be explained with-out at least a small volatile envelope. At the same time, the de-rived mass and radius values lie above the composition line for an Earth-like bare core by at least 2 σ. We note that from a forma-tion point of view, it is di ffi cult to come up with a scenario where a planet would only accrete silicates and no iron at all, making a purely rocky, iron-free core composition unlikely. Simultane-ously, the planet lies well below the iso-composition line for a Mercury-like core with an added H /He envelope of ∼1%. 5.2. Atmospheric evolution models for TOI-2211 b 

5.2.1. H/He-dominated atmospheres 

To assess the stability of atmospheres with di ff erent com-positions under the conditions of TOI-2211 b, we followed the procedure first introduced in Egger et al. (2025). We started by estimating the evaporation timescale of H/He-dominated atmospheres with solar metallicities using the at-mospheric evolution framework from Kubyshkina & Vidotto (2021); Kubyshkina & Fossati (2022), which relies on the Mors code (Johnstone et al. 2021; Spada et al. 2013) for the stellar input. As we are interested in the maximum evaporation time, we only considered the slow rotator scenario for TOI-2211, as-suming a stellar rotation period of P150 rot = 15 days at the age of 150 Myr (based on data from Johnstone et al. 2015). For the initial planetary mass, we adopted three values within the 1σ interval reported in Table 3 (1.96, 2.56, and 3.16 M⊕); on top of that, we considered initial atmospheric mass fractions 

f 0atm between 0.01-6.4%. We note, however, that in our models the atmosphere becomes gravitationally unstable for mass frac-tions above ∼3.5% for the given planetary parameters; therefore, these cases are not included in the results shown here. Figure 7 presents the dependence of the planetary evaporation timescale 

τesc (starting from the time of the protoplanetary disk disper-sal, adopted at 10 Myr) on the initial atmospheric mass fraction and planetary mass. We find that the evaporation timescale for H/He-dominated atmospheres on TOI-2211 b does not exceed 

∼7.65 Myr and maximizes at f 0atm values between 0.5 and 1%. The physical reasons behind this e ff ect and the model’s sen-sitivity to various free parameters were discussed in detail in Kubyshkina & Vidotto (2021). These results rule out the exis-tence of H /He-dominated atmosphere on TOI-2211 b at present day with high confidence.

5.3.1. Transit constraints from TESS 

To investigate whether the 15.6 day planet candidate TOI-2211.02 could be transiting, we performed a sensitivity analy-sis using the Transit Investigation and Recoverability Applica-tion (TIaRA; Rodel et al. 2024; Eschen et al. 2024). We use the short-cadence TESS data of the four available sectors (as out-lined in Table A.1, with QUALITY =0) and the 2-hour CDPP val-ues reported in the header of each file. We generate 1000 plan-ets uniformly distributed in log2 space ranging in period from 0.5 to 400 days and radius from 0.3 to 16 R ⊕. TIaRA com-putes the signal-to-noise ratio for each of these planets at ran-domly generated phases and determines the detectability based on the signal-to-noise, number of transits and the correspond-ing gamma-function. We show the resulting sensitivity map in Figure 9. TOI-2211 b is plotted on top of the computed sensitivities and find that as expected the planet is detectable by TESS. Since we did not detect a transit and hence obtained a radius of the outer planet candidate, we plot a vertical line at its orbital period. As done in Eschen et al. (2026), we compute predicted radii for planet compositions of pure iron as a lower limit on the radius using the planet models of Zeng et al. (2019). We find that the TESS data covers the windows in which the transit would fall and hence conclude that this planet candidate does not transit.

5.3.3. Inferring the planetary system architecture based on the Bern model of planet formation and evolution 

Given the detection limits of present day instruments, small plan-ets and planets with long orbital periods remain very challeng-ing to detect. This makes it di ffi cult to gain a full picture of the architecture of a planetary system and constrain the pres-ence, properties, and orbital configuration of potential additional planetary companions. Inferring the broader architecture of the system based on just the observed planets is a degenerate prob-lem and therefore requires a physically motivated prior, which we construct in this section based on synthetic planetary popula-tions. Our work is based on the Bern model (Alibert et al. 2005; Mordasini et al. 2009; Emsenhuber et al. 2021a,b), which is a global end-to-end model of planetary formation and evolution, built on the core-accretion paradigm. It consistently solves the coupled di ff erential equations governing the viscous evolution of the protoplanetary disc, the dynamical state of a planetesimal disc, the internal structure of the forming protoplanets, and the mutual gravitational interactions of concurrently growing em-bryos through an N-body integrator. The long-term thermody-namic evolution of each planet is computed up to 10 Gyr, includ-ing e ff ects such as cooling and contraction, atmospheric escape, radius inflation, and stellar tides. While the Bern model outputs complete populations of stable planetary systems, the computational cost of these simulations limits the size of the available samples for statistical inference. To overcome this, in a recent work, Alibert et al. (2025) trained  

> Article number, page 11 A&A proofs: manuscript no. aa61640-26

a generative model on a set of 25,000 stable planetary systems orbiting solar-type stars produced with the Bern model, enabling the rapid synthesis of additional systems that preserve the statis-tical properties of the underlying population. It was shown that it e ff ectively generated systems with physical meaning and that it could constrain properly the properties of other planets in the system given at least one detection. In the present work, we adopt the novel framework introduced by Marques & Alibert (2026), which extends the model of Alibert et al. (2025), allowing us to infer the properties of yet-to-be detected planets in the same sys-tem conditioned on the observed transiting planet(s). This gener-ative model is a character-level transformer that represents each planetary system as a sequence of discrete tokens encoding the planets’ properties: radius, orbital period, and mass. To apply this analysis to the TOI-2211 system, a sample of 10 6 synthetic systems was generated using this new framework. From these, we selected the ones containing a TOI-2211 b-like planet (allowing for a 1% uncertainty in orbital period and 10% uncertainty in radius based on the median values reported in Ta-ble 3), yielding ∼1700 systems. We removed the TOI-2211 b-like planet from the system to draw the joint distributions over the mass, radius and period for the remaining planets and added an observational filter at K = 1 m /s to exclude non-observable planets. The final distributions are shown in Fig. 11. These distributions exhibit a clear over-density consistent with the presence of a second planet in the system, whose or-bital period and inferred mass are compatible with the ones of the planet candidate identified in the RV analysis in Section 4. In particular, looking at the mass–period distribution, if we ap-ply a Gaussian fit to the predicted logarithmic mass distribution, it yields a normal distribution centred at 0 .82 ± 0.39. The min-imal mass of candidate TOI-2211.02 lies in the upper portion of this distribution but remains well within 1 σ of the median of the log-normal distribution, 6.7 M ⊕, consistent with the model prediction. Furthermore, the framework also provides a way to estimate the radius for the candidate TOI-2211.02 based on a population-level prediction of the adopted synthetic population. The analy-sis of the radius distribution is a bit more complex than the mass distribution, as it displays a bimodal structure in the logarith-mic space with two well-separated peaks. We fit this distribution with two separate Gaussian components in log( Rp/R⊕), which are centred at 0 .20 ±0.16 and 0 .84 ±0.20 in the logarithmic space and correspond to log-normal distributions with median radii of 1.6 and 6.9 R ⊕, respectively. Considering only the marginal ra-dius posterior, the candidate TOI-2211.02 has an approximately equal probability of belonging to either mode. However, condi-tioning the radius posterior on the measured minimal mass shifts the probability to ∼60% for belonging to the second mode. Fi-nally, we note that this overall analysis does not exclude the pos-sibility of additional planets in the system.

We found that there is a clear over-density in the generated posterior distributions consistent with the presence of a second planet in the system. The properties of this inferred planet are compatible with the or-bital period and minimal mass of TOI-2211.02. Additionally, the framework predicts a bimodal radius distribution with two peaks centred at approximately 1.6 and 6.9 R ⊕ for this planet candi-date.
