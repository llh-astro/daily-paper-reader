ABSTRACT 

Nearly 25 years have passed since gravitational microlensing delivered its first exoplanet discoveries. The relative proper motion between lens and source systems allows them to be resolved as they separate on the sky over many years. This enables scientists to study light coming directly from the lenses and place tight constraints on the physical properties of these planetary systems. In this paper we present an analysis of high angular resolution images of the microlensing target OGLE-2006-BLG-284 using Keck adaptive optics and HST data taken more than 15 years after the event. We clearly detect the lens system and measure its motion across the Keck and HST epochs. We measure a lens-source relative proper motion of 5 .80 ± 0.15 mas yr −1. A combination of the light curve parameters and the host star(s) brightnesses in V, I , and K passbands gives primary and secondary host star masses of M1 = 0 .43 ± 0.05 M⊙ and M2 = 0 .12 ± 0.02 M⊙, and a planetary companion mass of mp = 165 +40

M⊕. Although we are unable to determine if the planet orbits one or both stars in the system, our analysis motivates next-generation high-precision RV measurements that can distinguish these two possibilities. This is the first direct detection of flux coming from a two-star microlensing system which hosts a planet.

INTRODUCTION The gravitational microlensing technique (Paczynski 1986; Mao & Paczynski 1991) has a unique detection sensitivity to planets down to 1M ⊕ and lower (Ben-nett & Rhie 1996), that orbit their host stars at sep-arations of a few Astronomical Units (AU). The transit and radial velocity methods are insufficient at probing this parameter space. Therefore, microlensing holds an important complementary role in the characterization of exoplanetary systems. It is also important to note

that the orbital configuration of the exoplanet does not have to be highly constrained with microlensing as it does with other techniques. Nearly 300 exoplanets have been discovered using the microlensing technique; most of these planets orbit single host stars. This has enabled statistical analyses of this population to be performed (Suzuki et al. 2016, 2018; Zang et al. 2025) and tested against current planetary evolution theory (Ida & Lin 2004; Mordasini et al. 2009). Another region of exoplanet parameter space where microlensing has a unique sensitivity is for planets in stellar binary systems with star–star or planet–star sep-arations of a few AU (Gould & Loeb 1992). This is the region where microlensing is most sensitive because arXiv:2610.07133v1 [astro-ph.EP] 5 Oct 2026 2 Terry et al. 

it corresponds to the typical Einstein radius ( θE ) for microlensing events toward the Galactic bulge. Kepler (Borucki et al. 2011) has found a number of circumbi-nary planets in closer orbits around relatively tight bi-naries (Doyle et al. 2011; Welsh et al. 2012, 2015). How-ever, the prevalence of these planets is thought to be a selection effect, as short-period planets are much easier to detect with the transit method. Circumbinary plan-ets in wider orbits, like the first circumbinary planet found by microlensing (Bennett et al. 2016) and the widest orbit circumbinary planet found by Kepler (Kos-tov et al. 2016), are thought to form more easily and be more common than circumstellar planets (Thebault & Haghighipour 2015). Of the 19 published triple-lens systems to date, nine of them are reported to have planetary companions in stel-lar binary systems. With modeling of 1 planet + 2 star systems, it is very difficult to distinguish if the planet orbits both stars in the system (circumbinary) or if the planet orbits a single star in the system (circumstellar). There are several reasons why this interpretation of the orbital configuration is challenging. Firstly, from the light curve modeling alone, only the 2-dimensional in-stantaneous projected separations (i.e. a⊥) between the two stars, the planet and star 1, and the planet and star 2 are estimated. This provides just a lower limit on the instantaneous 3D separation, which itself is only loosely tied to the semi-major axes of the objects in the system. Second, in addition to the classical close/wide degeneracy (Griest & Safizadeh 1998; Dominik 1999) and the ecliptic degeneracy (Smith et al. 2003; Skowron et al. 2011), there are several triple-lens degeneracies that arise from the external-shear approximation, sign reversals on the combined parameters u0, α, ϕ and others (Song et al. 2014; Fatheddin & Sajadian 2023). Lastly, microlensing parallax and lens orbital motion are more typically unmeasured, particularly for shorter timescale events, but even if orbital motion ( ds/dt, dα/dt ) is mea-sured, these do not give a full Keplerian solution. The one published triple lens system that does have a confirmed circumbinary solution is OGLE-2007-BLG-349 (Bennett et al. 2016). The authors were able to rule out the circumstellar solution by arguing that the ratio of the separation between the planet −center of mass to the star 1 −star 2 separation is too large to be consistent with a planet orbiting one but not both of the stars. In-terestingly, they conclude that this planet does not orbit near the stability limit, where most other circumbinary planets found by Kepler reside. We also note the long duration light curve ( tE ∼ 120 days) for OGLE-2007-BLG-349 allowed the authors to measure a significant microlensing parallax effect, which resulted in modestly small uncertainties ( ∼15%) on the masses of each lens component. In this paper we re-visit the triple lens microlensing event OGLE-2006-BLG-284. In Section 2 we describe the original ground-based observations. In Section 3 we present the high angular resolution HST and Keck adap-tive optics (AO) observations and analysis. Section 4 details our direct measurement of the lens system flux and lens-source separation in the Keck and HST data. Sections 5 and 6 describe our updated light curve model-ing which includes constraints from the high-resolution imaging data, as well as our updated interpretation of the lens system properties. Finally, we discuss the over-all results and conclude the paper in Section 7.

MICROLENSING EVENT OGLE-2006-BLG-284 OGLE-2006-BLG-284, located at RA = 17:58:38.22, DEC = -29:08:12.0 and Galactic coordinates ( l, b ) = (1 .277 , −2.550) was initially alerted by the Optical Gravitational Lensing Experiment (OGLE; Udalski et al. 1993, 2015). The event was originally published in Jaroszy´ nski et al. (2010) as a stellar binary event along with other binary microlensing events detected by OGLE between the 2006 and 2008 observing seasons. Although the event was not initially alerted by the Mi-crolensing Observations in Astrophysics (MOA; Bond et al. 2001; Sumi et al. 2003) collaboration, it was re-covered as a binary lensing event in the MOA 9-year retrospective project. A joint analysis of the OGLE and MOA photometry was performed by Bennett et al. (2020) who found a best-fit triple lens model with an improvement of ∆ χ2 ∼ 567 over the previous best-fit bi-nary lens model. Their best-fit solution is a lens system with two stars and a planet. As Bennett et al. (2020) discuss, the primary diffi-culty in the interpretation of the planet as circumbinary or circumstellar is the fact that the projected separa-tion between the primary and secondary stars appears to be nearly identical to the projected separation between the planet and the primary star. The authors go on to describe prospects for distinguishing between these two OGLE-2006-BLG-284 3possibilities with future high-resolution imaging of the lens system with Keck AO and next-generation spectro-graphs on 30 m−class telescopes. Successful time-series radial velocity (RV) measurements with ∼2 km/s pre-cision or better should be able to detect the RV signal of the primary star if it is in a close orbit with the sec-ondary (e.g. a ≲ 2 AU). This would give strong evidence that the two stars are in a close, short-period orbit, and the planet must be in a larger orbit with a large line-of-sight separation (i.e. circumbinary). On the other hand, if there is no significant RV signal measured, this would favor the interpretation that the two stars are in a much wider orbit, and the planet orbits only one star (i.e. circumstellar).

HIGH ANGULAR RESOLUTION FOLLOW-UP WITH KECK & HST 3.1. 2020 −2021 Keck Analysis 

The target OGLE-2006-BLG-284 was observed with the OSIRIS imager on Keck-I in the Kprime passband (λc = 2 .12 μm, hereafter K) on August 10, 2020 and July 14, 2021. These high-resolution imaging epochs were taken approximately 14.04 and 14.97 years after the peak of the microlensing event in 2006. The 2020 Keck image quality is excellent, with an average point-spread function (PSF) full-width at half-max (FWHM) of 50 mas and Strehl Ratio (SR) of 0.46. The 2021 data is of measurably lower image quality, with an average PSF FWHM ∼ 60 mas and SR ∼ 0.26. These two pa-rameters (FWHM, SR) are generally used to understand the performance of the AO system, with smaller FWHM and larger SR values typically indicating better quality. We note there are other, sometimes more subtle, factors that can affect AO image quality that are not necessar-ily captured by FWHM or SR alone (Terry et al. 2023). The middle and right panels of Figure 1 show a zoom-in on the target(s); the diffraction-limited Airy ring can be seen around the source star in 2020. There appear to be minor systematic effects impacting the AO correction for the 2021 data which lead to lower image quality. The pixel scale for the OSIRIS imager is 9.952 mas/pixel, and all of the images were taken using the Keck-I laser guide star adaptive optics (LGSAO) system. For the 2020 data, a co-add of 25 dithered science im-ages was used for photometric calibration to images from the Vista Variables in the Via Lactea (VVV) survey (Minniti et al. 2010) following the procedure of Beaulieu et al. (2018). The images were flat-field and dark current corrected using standard methods (Lu 2022). We per-formed astrometry and photometry on the co-added im-age using SExtractor (Bertin & Arnouts 1996), and sub-sequently calibrated the Keck image to VVV by match-ing 11 bright isolated stars in the frames. This calibra-tion analysis results in uncertainties of 0.05 magnitudes. We select the 2020 dataset to perform the photometric calibration to VVV as it is the higher-quality of the two Keck datasets. We combined 12 flat-field frames, five dark frames, and 15 sky frames for calibrating our science images. As previously mentioned, a total of 25 science frames with an integration time of 60 s frame −1 were reduced using the Keck AO Imaging (KAI) Data Reduction Pipeline (Lu 2022) to correct instrumental aberrations and geo-metric distortions (Lu et al. 2008; Yelda et al. 2010; Free-man et al. 2023). Additionally, there were 23 OSIRIS 

K−band images of the target taken on July 14, 2021. Identical reduction and analysis were performed for both Keck epochs. The original motivation to re-visit the target in 2021 was because there were two lens candi-dates identified in the 2020 epoch; one to the East of the source star and one to the South-Southwest (SSW) of the source star (see middle panel of Figure 1). Both candidates seen in 2020 have separations from the source star that could be consistent with being the lens, given the estimated μrel ∼ 6.3 mas yr −1 from Bennett et al. (2020). The 2021 data allows us to marginally rule out the SSW object as the true lens, only at the 2 σ-level due to the poorer image quality in 2021. We definitively rule out this SSW object using the 2025 HST F 814 W

data, as we describe in Section 3.2. Following the methods of Bhattacharya et al. (2018) and Terry et al. (2021), we use a modified version of the DAOPHOT-II package (Stetson 1987), that we call daophot mcmc 1, to run Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling on the pixel grid encompassing the targets. Further details of the MCMC routine are given in Terry et al. (2021, 2022). Bennett et al. (2006) and Bennett et al. (2007) first conceptualized the color-dependence of the flux-weighted centroid in highly blended source and lens star pairs. Given measurably different colors, there should be an apparent difference in the location of a fitted PSF centroid as measured in two different passbands. Bhattacharya et al. (2023) confirmed the first color-dependent centroid shift measurement made by Bennett et al. (2006) by independently resolving the OGLE-2003-BLG-235 lens and source stars in Keck AO followup imaging 15 years after the microlensing magnification. We have clearly resolved and individually measured the source star and lens system in both Keck and HST datasets. However, the two stars that reside in the lens system are not individually resolved. 5.1. Investigating a Source Companion 

During our analysis efforts we noticed that the light curve modeling prefers a slightly fainter source star. As mentioned earlier, the calibrated HST V , I, and Keck 

K band source star magnitudes are marginally brighter (0 .1−0.2 mag) than the source magnitudes given by non-image-constrained light curve modeling. We attributed this to the possibility that there may be a faint com-panion to the source star that is not magnified signifi-cantly. Further, a source companion may also explain the modest discrepancy between our inferred μrel,G from the high-resolution imaging and the μrel,G reported by Bennett et al. (2020). If the source has a binary com-panion that is too widely separated to have a measur-able effect on the light curve, it could have a measurable effect on the high-precision astrometry (given a flux ra-tio ∼ 1). If the projected separation of the companion is 80 AU this would give an offset of 10 mas between the source and its companion, which is then multiplied by the flux ratio. However, as described in Section 3 and shown in Figure 3, we do not measure any color-dependent centroid shift at the position of the source system in HST. These two scenarios effectively work to counteract each other. Nevertheless, we perform additional light curve mod-eling that includes a companion to the source star. About two dozen confirmed circumbinary planets have been discovered to date, mostly from the Kepler (Borucki et al. 2011) and TESS (Ricker et al. 2015) mis-sions. Conversely, several hundred circumstellar plan-ets orbiting one star of multiple-star systems have been discovered 9. The reason for this disparity is both a se-lection effect and an astrophysical effect. Short period exoplanets are easier to detect with the transit method, and the presence of a binary companion likely interferes with circumbinary planet formation. The inner disk, which contains material that would otherwise be used to form planetesimals, is truncated for circumbinary plan-ets (Thebault & Haghighipour 2015). Although we have made a direct identification of flux coming from the OGLE-2006-BLG-284 triple lens sys-tem, we are unable to determine if the planet orbits both stars or a single star in the system. The marginal detec-tion of a color-dependent centroid shift between HST I

band and V band data may imply a large separation for the two stars in the system, thus supporting the circum-stellar interpretation for the planet. However this 1 .3σ

detection is not significant, and at best only places a

One component of the triple lens system must have a true 3D separation larger than 2 AU from the primary host star. This must be either the lower-mass secondary star or a widely or-biting planetary companion. A time-series radial velocity (RV) measurement of the lens system would provide the strongest evidence yet in support of either remaining scenario. However this measurement is effectively impossible with current-generation spectrographs on large-aperture telescopes. The K ∼ 19 .5 lens system, in very close proximity to the K ∼ 17 .4 source star, is too challenging for current-generation spectrographs on 8 − 10 m class telescopes to achieve the necessary RV precision. We expect a next-generation class of extremely large telescope (ELT) spectrographs should have the necessary combination of angular resolution, collecting area, and spectral reso-lution to attempt direct RV measurements of the lens system and distinguish between the circumstellar and circumbinary scenario. Precisions of ∼ 1 km/s on the faint target should be able to detect the RV signal of the 0.426 M⊙ primary star if it has a small semi-major axis with the companion star (circumbinary panel of Figure 7). DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION Our high resolution follow up observations of the mi-crolensing target OGLE-2006-BLG-284 have allowed us to make the first ever measurement of flux coming di-rectly from a microlens system comprising two stars and a planetary companion. We measure the total flux of the system in multiple passbands ( V , I, K), and make a pre-cise determination of the amplitude and direction of the lens-source relative proper motion μrel . The lens system flux measurements enable us to use mass-luminosity re-lations and new constraints on the light curve modeling to measure a precise mass and distance for the lens sys-tem. The lens system consists of two main sequence stars with a planetary companion in the disk at a dis-tance from Earth of DL = 4 .47 ± 0.53 kpc. The primary star has a mass of 0 .426 ± 0.046 M⊙, the secondary star has a mass of 0 .120 ± 0.015 M⊙, and the planet has a ‘sub-Jupiter’ mass of 165 +40

Using the HST V and I band data taken in 2025, we attempt the first color-dependent centroid shift mea-surement on a microlens system directly. This has been successfully demonstrated before on a blended source-lens pair (Bennett et al. 2006; Bhattacharya et al. 2023), but there have been no binary lens star pairs to attempt this measurement until now. We detect a weak ( ∼1.3σ)signal at the location of the lens, which may indicate a very wide orbit for the two lens stars and thus support the circumstellar scenario for the planetary companion.
