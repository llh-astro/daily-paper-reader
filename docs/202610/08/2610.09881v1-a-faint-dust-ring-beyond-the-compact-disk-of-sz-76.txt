Letter to the Editor 

# A faint dust ring beyond the compact disk of Sz 76 

G. Ricciardi, A. Miotello, K. Mauco, C. F. Manara, S. Facchini, Á. Kóspál, J. Alcalá, J. Eislö ff el, et al. 

(Full author list and a ffi liations details can be found after the references) 

October 8, 2026 

> ABSTRACT

We report new ALMA Band 7 high-resolution ( ∼0.05 ′′ 

) continuum and 12 CO ( J = 3–2) observations of the disk around the young low-mass star Sz 76. Visibility plane modeling have revealed a previously undetected, faint outer dust ring centered at ∼45 au surrounding the compact central disk. Including this low-surface-brightness feature increases the characteristic dust radius from Rdust,68 ≈ 7 au to 41 au. Combined with an outer gas extent of RCO,68 ≈ 77 au, the resulting gas-to-dust size ratio decreases from an extreme value of ∼11 to ∼2, setting Sz 76 in line with typical protoplanetary disks and contradicting a scenario of unimpeded inward radial drift. These results demonstrate that spatial filtering or limited surface-brightness sensitivity can severely underestimate disk sizes, highlighting the necessity of multi-scale observations to properly identify dust traps and accurately constrain disk evolution.

1. Introduction 

The high incidence of compact protoplanetary disks ( Rdust < 20 au) in millimeter continuum surveys conducted with ALMA (see Manara et al. 2023, as a review) is broadly consistent with the-oretical expectations from radial drift, which predicts that dust grains grow into pebbles and migrate toward local gas pressure maxima, leading to an inward redistribution of the solid mate-rial (Weidenschilling 1977). In disks with smooth pressure pro-files, this process concentrates dust in the inner regions, pro-ducing continuum emission that is significantly more compact than the underlying gas disk. High-angular-resolution ALMA observations have revealed that many bright and large disks host rings, gaps, and other radial substructures (e.g., Andrews 2020; Bae et al. 2023, as reviews) that have commonly been in-terpreted as manifestations of local pressure maxima that e ffi -ciently trap drifting pebbles at large radii (Pinilla et al. 2012; Dullemond et al. 2018); in addition, some also exhibit faint con-tinuum emission at large radii that had previously gone unde-tected (Ilee et al. 2022; Das et al. 2024; Curone et al. 2025). On the other hand, our understanding of compact disks, which con-stitutes a large fraction of the disk demographic, remains lim-ited (Miotello et al. 2021). It is unclear whether compact disks host analogous substructures on smaller spatial scales or whether their dust distributions are primarily shaped by unimpeded ra-dial drift. Radial drift models predict that millimeter continuum emission should be significantly less extended than molecular gas tracers, particularly CO (Facchini et al. 2017; Trapman et al. 2019). Observational surveys of bright disks generally report gas-to-dust size ratios between ∼1.5 and 3 (Barenfeld et al. 2017; Ansdell et al. 2018). While some systems require ra-dial drift to account for the observed discrepancy between gas and dust extents (e.g., Pani´ c et al. 2009; Andrews et al. 2012; Sanchis et al. 2021), in most cases, the di ff erence can be ex-plained by optical depth e ff ects, since CO emission remains detectable at lower surface densities than the dust continuum (Dutrey et al. 1998; Guilloteau & Dutrey 1998; Facchini et al. 2017). ALMA provides su ffi ciently sensitive observations of both dust and gas emission in compact disks to test whether these systems are simply scaled-down versions of their larger counterparts or instead represent disks in which radial drift has dramatically reduced the extent of the dust component. Extreme dust concentration was reported for CX Tau, where the large disparity between the CO and millimeter contin-uum radii was interpreted as a clear signature of radial drift (Facchini et al. 2019). However, the recent survey of 18 faint and compact disks in Lupus by Ricciardi et al. (2026) reached a di ff erent conclusion: all targets exhibit Rgas /Rdust < 3, with no compelling evidence for strong radial drift. In this context, CX Tau seems to be an exception more than the rule. The target of this Letter is the disk around the protostar Sz 76 ( M⋆ =0.19 M⊙, ˙Macc = 7.8 · 10 −10 M⊙yr −1) in the young Lupus I cloud (Alcalá et al. 2017; Manara et al. 2023). This disk was among the faintest, apparently compact disks, of the ALMA survey of disks in Lupus (Ansdell et al. 2018). The CO radius of this disk was measured by Sanchis et al. (2021) and compared to the dust extension to find a relatively compact disk. Recently Guerra-Alvarado et al. (2025) reported very high-angular reso-lution ( ∼0.03 ′′ 

) observations hinting at the presence of a cavity in the inner region of a compact continuum emission. In this Letter, we present ALMA Band 7 observations of Sz 76 at an angular resolution of ∼ 0.05 ′′ , providing new in-sights into the properties of its gas and dust components. Sz 76 is an intriguing system due to its historically ambiguous evolu-tionary classification. Despite retaining an infrared excess and detectable millimeter continuum emission, it has been classi-fied in the literature as a weak-line T Tauri star (WTTS) or Class III source (Wahhaj et al. 2010; López Martí et al. 2011; Alcalá et al. 2017). Thus, Sz 76 is an interesting system for in-vestigating the connection between disk dispersal and the radial evolution of solids, as it shows relatively weak accretion activ-ity alongside a still detectable circumstellar disk. Section 2 de-scribes the observations, Sect. 3 presents the analysis and results, and Sect. 4 places these results within the broader context of disk evolution.

2. Observations 

The data presented in this Letter were obtained as part of the ALMA program #2023.1.01276.S (PI: K. Mauco). The disk around Sz 76 was targeted in H 13 CN ( J = 4−3), 12 CO ( J = 3−2), and HCN ( J = 4 − 3) line emission, and with two broad contin-uum windows. In this Letter, we focus exclusively on the contin-uum emission and the 12 CO ( J = 3 − 2) line. For the continuum, we combined all line-free channels, resulting in a total e ff ec-tive bandwidth of 4.453 GHz, while the CO data were analyzed at a spectral resolution of 0.106 km s −1 before any subsequent spectral averaging. The details on the data calibration and imag-ing are reported in Appendix A. The continuum image and the intensity-weighted velocity 12 CO emission map are presented in Fig. 1.

3. Results 

3.1. Dust modeling 

To characterize the dust continuum emission, we modeled the ALMA visibilities directly in the uv-plane using the Python package GALARIO (Tazzari et al. 2018), which computes syn-thetic interferometric visibilities from an analytical surface-brightness model through a Fourier transform. The model pa-rameters were explored using a Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach by minimizing the χ2 between the observed and synthetic visibilities. Modeling the interferometric data di-rectly in the uv-plane avoids biases introduced by image decon-volution and provides robust estimates of the intrinsic disk prop-erties. The modeling was primarily aimed at determining the global disk geometry, such as the position angle (PA), inclination (i), and o ff sets in RA and Dec with respect to the phase center, together with the total continuum flux and the radius enclosing 68% of the dust emission ( R68 ,dust ). To compute the dust radial profile, we considered two di ff erent profiles: a single Gaussian component representing the bulk of the dust emission (see Fig.2, panel a); a centrally peaked Gaussian component and an addi-tional Gaussian ring (see Fig. 2, panel b). The residuals reveal that the single-Gaussian model leaves significant ring-shaped residual emission, suggesting that it does not accurately repro-duce the observed brightness distribution. In contrast, the Gaus-sian +Ring model removes these residuals completely, providing a substantially better representation of the continuum emission. The presence of the faint ring is further supported by the depro-jected visibility profile (Fig. B.2), where the ring model repro-duces the oscillatory behavior (uv-distance ≃500 k λ) of the vis-ibilities that cannot be accounted for by a single Gaussian com-ponent (Fig. B.1). 3.3. Continuum intensity profile and comparison with the literature 

Sz 76 was recently observed at very high angular resolution (∼ 0.03 ′′ 

) by Guerra-Alvarado et al. (2025) in ALMA Band 6. These observations have a synthesized beam of 0 .026 ′′ × 0.025 ′′ ,which is approximately half the size of that in our Band 7 data, an rms of 0.019 mJy beam −1 and a comparable surface-brightness sensitivity. The high angular resolution and, possibly, the lower frequency of their observations, made it possible for the innermost dust distribution to be resolved and a central depletion to be identified on scales of ∼ 5 au. However, their observations lacked short-baseline coverage, recovering only a fraction of the continuum flux measured at lower angular resolution. This suggests that a significant fraction of the extended emission was spatially filtered out. Thus, while their observations provide a more detailed view of the inner disk, they are insensitive to the faint emission at large radii revealed by our data. As shown in Fig. 3, the best fit radial profile inferred by Guerra-Alvarado et al. (2025) shown in green is strongly concentrated towards the central disk - with an inner cavity - and declines rapidly with radius. Thus, only observations combining su ffi cient angular resolution, sensitivity, and short- and long-baseline coverage can recover both compo-nents and distinguish the outer emission as a ring. 

4. Discussion and conclusions 

With the new ALMA data presented in this work, we have dis-covered a faint dusty ring at ∼ 45 au from the protostar Sz 76, beyond a compact disk of ∼ 7 au. This ring, together with the inner cavity reported by Guerra-Alvarado et al. (2025), imply that complex sub-structured dust distributions can be found also around very-low-mass protostars ( M⋆ =0.19 M ⊙) and can be signposts of planet formation (see Appendix D for more detail on exploratory planet–disk interaction modeling of Sz76). We have also shown that the inferred dust distribution of a protoplanetary disk significantly depends on the observa-tional setup (see also Ilee et al. 2022; Das et al. 2024). Adequate 

Article number, page 3 A&A proofs: manuscript no. aa62655-26corr 

surface-brightness sensitivity is required to detect faint outer emission and short baselines are necessary to recover its total flux, while long baselines are needed to resolve fine structures. The analysis on Sz 76 disk emission demonstrates the physical consequences of this observational bias. Once the faint ring at 

∼ 45 au was recovered with our Galario modeling, the measured dust radius increase from R68 ,dust = 7.1 au for the bright cen-tral component alone to R68 ,dust = 40 .98 au. The detection of this faint outer ring therefore changes not only our view of the disk morphology, but also its evolutionary interpretation: while a dust disk confined within ≤ 10 au would naturally suggest ef-ficient, unimpeded inward migration of millimeter-sized grains, the presence of an outer dust substructure instead points toward dust trapping in a pressure maximum. In this context, the simultaneous measurement of the gas and dust radii provides an important diagnostic of dust evolution. In particular, the gas-to-dust size ratio, RCO /Rdust , can help con-strain whether radial drift is a dominant process (Facchini et al. 2017; Trapman et al. 2019). In our analysis, the 12 CO ( J = 3−2) emission extends beyond the continuum, with R68 ,CO = 77 .2 au. We adopted R68 for this comparison because the CO radius is measured from the image-plane curve of growth, whose out-ermost percentiles become increasingly sensitive to low-level noise and to the determination of the total-flux plateau (see Miotello et al. 2023, as a review). In particular, R90 is con-trolled by the faintest emission at large radii and is therefore less robust for the present data. As shown in Fig. 4, consid-ering only the bright inner dust component would yield to 

R68 ,gas /R68 ,dust ≃ 11, far above the empirical threshold of ∼ 4identified by Trapman et al. (2019) as robust evidence for e ffi -cient radial drift. In a similar fashion, the R68 ,gas /R68 ,dust ratio measured by (Guerra-Alvarado et al. 2025) is above 4. Instead, once the faint outer ring is properly considered, the measured size ratio decreases to ∼ 2, placing Sz 76 among the bulk of the Lupus disk population, whose median value is R68 ,gas /R68 ,dust ≃

2.5 (Sanchis et al. 2021). This is fully consistent with the sample of radially compact disks analyzed by Ricciardi et al. (2026). As already pointed out in Sect. 3.3, the ratio obtained by the simpli-fied modeling of Sanchis et al. (2021) is within the range of our result (see blue circle in Fig. 4). Sz 76 thus provides a cautionary example for the use of gas-to-dust size ratios as diagnostics of disk evolution. Insu ffi cient sensitivity to low-surface-brightness continuum emission or an inadequate recovery of extended spatial scales can bias Rdust to-ward smaller values and artificially inflate RCO /Rdust , mimicking the observational signature of e ffi cient radial drift. More broadly, some disks currently classified as compact (and potentially in-terpreted as strongly a ff ected by radial drift) could host faint outer dust structures that remain undetected. Establishing their true radial extent therefore requires observations that combine su ffi cient angular resolution and sensitivity with both short- and long-baseline coverage.

Appendix A: Details on the observations and data reduction 

The data presented in this Letter were obtained as part of the ALMA program #2023.1.01276.S (PI: K. Mauco). The source Sz 76 was observed on 31 May 2025 in Band 7 using 48 12-m antennas, with a total on-source integration time of 780.2 s (13.0 min). The observations were designed to achieve an angular resolution of ∼0.05 ′′ 

, with a continuum sensitivity of ∼0.08 mJy beam −1. The correlator was configured with five spectral windows, comprising two high-resolution windows centered on the H 13 CN ( J = 4 − 3) and 12 CO ( J = 3 − 2) transitions, one intermediate-bandwidth window centered on HCN ( J = 4 − 3), and two broad continuum windows covering the lower and upper sidebands. In this Letter, we focus exclusively on the continuum emission and the 12 CO (J = 3 − 2) line. For the continuum we combined all line-free channels, resulting in a total e ff ective bandwidth of 4.453 GHz, while the CO data were analyzed at a spectral resolution of 0.106 km s −1 before any subsequent spectral averaging. The data were calibrated using the CASA version 6.5.4-9 (Pipeline version 2023.1.0.125). To improve the image fidelity, three rounds of phase-only self-calibration were performed on the continuum visibilities using the CASA task gaincal , with solution intervals of solint =inf, 60 s, and 10 s, respectively. The derived calibration tables were sequentially applied to the visibility data leading to an improvement of ∼ 30% in the signal-to-noise (S /N) ratio of the continuum emission. The self-calibration solutions were also applied to the 12 CO data. The self-calibrated continuum visibilities were imaged with the CASA task tclean using Briggs weighting with a robust parameter of 0.5, and a multiscale deconvolution. Cleaning was performed down to a threshold of 0.1 mJy beam −1 using the auto-multithresh masking algorithm. The 12 CO ( J = 3 − 2) data were continuum-subtracted in the visibility domain and subsequently imaged. The data cube was produced using natural weighting to maximize surface-brightness sensitivity and a multiscale deconvolution. Cleaning was performed down to a threshold of 1 mJy beam −1 using the auto-multithresh masking algorithm. The final continuum image and the intensity-weighted velocity 12 CO emission map are presented in Fig. 1.
