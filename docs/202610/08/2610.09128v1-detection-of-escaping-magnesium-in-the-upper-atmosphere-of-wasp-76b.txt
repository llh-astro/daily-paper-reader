ABSTRACT Near-ultraviolet (NUV) observations can uniquely probe low-density, high-altitude gas in ultra-hot Jupiters (UHJs) via strong resonance lines of metals. WASP-76b is a well-studied UHJ with prior detections of refractory metals and complex day–night chemistry, making it a prime target for atmo-spheric escape diagnostics in the NUV. We present HST/STIS E230M (2274 − 3119 ˚ A) transmission spectroscopy of WASP-76b. While broad- ( ∼ 200 ˚ A) and narrow-band (4 ˚ A) transmission spectra do not show spectral features, a velocity-resolved single line analysis reveals clear excess absorption in both components of the Mg ii doublet (2796 ˚ A and 2803 ˚ A, k and h lines). The Mg ii signal is localized to the blue wing (Doppler velocities [-50, -10] km s −1) and is recovered with narrower 0.5 ˚A bins; the inferred absorption exceeds both the optical continuum radius and the Roche-lobe scale, suggesting a highly extended upper atmosphere. We estimate a mass-loss rate of ˙M ∼ 10 12 .14 g s −1

from ATES. The present NUV data do not improve constraints on lower-atmosphere properties, and a comparison with upper-atmosphere forward models disfavor scenarios with extreme Mg enrichment. Together with the near-solar metallicity inferred from optical+IR spectra, this suggests that the Mg ii 

absorption can arise in an escaping upper atmosphere without requiring extreme Mg enrichment, qual-itatively consistent with hydrodynamic escape. Ongoing HST programs expanding the sample of UHJs focused on NUV observations will enable comparative tests of composition and kinematics in escaping atmospheres.

sulting low-density, high-altitude layers are best probed at ultraviolet (UV) wavelengths, which provide access to strong resonance transitions of atomic and ionized species that can trace both composition and mass loss. UV transmission spectroscopy with the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) has enabled detections of metals and ions in several close-in giant planets, including species such as C ii , Fe ii , O i, Mg i/Mg ii , and Si iii . Many of these measurements were obtained with the Space Tele-scope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS) (e.g., Vidal-Madjar et al. 2003, 2004, 2013; Sing et al. 2019; Cubillos et al. 2020; Ben-Jaffel et al. 2022; Baldwin et al. 2026) and the Cosmic Origins Spectrograph (COS) (e.g., Fossati et al. 2010; Linsky et al. 2010; Ben-Jaffel & Ballester 2013; Ballester & Ben-Jaffel 2015; Garc´ ıa Mu˜ noz et al. 2021; Ben-Jaffel et al. 2022; Dos Santos et al. 2023), demonstrating that UV lines can reveal extended atmo-spheres and provide constraints on the physical state of the escaping gas. Ultra-hot Jupiters (UHJs) are particularly favorable targets for these studies because their extreme temper-atures fundamentally alter atmospheric chemistry and opacity. At temperatures above 2,000 K, molecules ther-mally dissociate, atoms become efficiently ionized, and condensation of refractory elements is suppressed, leav-ing abundant gaseous metals (e.g., Fe and Mg) avail-able as tracers of photoionization, dynamics and escape (Visscher et al. 2010; Wakeford et al. 2017; Linssen & Oklopˇ ci´ c 2023; Lothringer et al. 2020). Observational surveys and theoretical work have consequently revealed a diverse inventory of atomic species in UHJ atmo-spheres (Lothringer et al. 2018; Parmentier et al. 2018), and predict that these planets may exhibit strong sig-natures of upper-atmosphere heating and mass loss. In this study, we present new observations of WASP-76b (West et al. 2016), complementing the existing HST transmission spectrum presented by Fu et al. (2021). WASP-76b is an UHJ orbiting an F-type star ( Vmag =9.52, Teff ∼ 6366 K) every 1.81 days at a separation of 0.033 AU (see Table 1 for all the system parame-ters). It is a benchmark planet that has been extensively studied from the ground and from space, with numer-ous atomic, ionized and molecular species reported in its atmosphere: Ba ii , Ca i, Ca ii , Co i, Cr i, Fe i, Fe ii , H i,He i, K i, Li i, Mg i, Mn i, Na i, Ni i, O i, Sr ii , V i, CO, H2O, HCN, OH, TiO and VO (see Costa Silva et al. 2024, for more details on these detections). We motivate this work with prior observations from ESPRESSO (Ehrenreich et al. 2020) and HST (Fu et al. 2021) which reveal high-altitude atomic metals and complex thermal structure on WASP-76b. Ehrenreich et al. (2020) report an asymmetrical detection of neu-tral iron (Fe i) in the optical transmission spectrum that is present on the evening (dayside) terminator but is absent on the morning terminator; this absorption is also blueshifted and stronger during egress than dur-ing ingress. This pattern in velocity and phase-resolved strength was interpreted as nightside condensation (or “iron rain”) removing gaseous Fe on the morning limb, combined with zonal winds to produce the observed Doppler shifts. Fu et al. (2021) extended the picture to 0.29 − 4.5 μm, finding a steep excess in transit depth at NUV/blue wavelengths that cannot be explained by Rayleigh scattering or hazes alone and requiring neu-tral and ionized metals (e.g. Fe, Ti, V, Ni, SiO, Ca) to fit the data. They further show that heavy met-als, H 2O and TiO seen in transmission together with muted H 2O emission but strong CO emission at 4.5 μm, are consistent with an inverted dayside T-P profile. Fu et al. (2021) advocates for the inclusion of NUV ob-servations to identify high-altitude heavy metal species, link composition to upper-atmosphere heating and ther-mal inversion and test condensation/rainout and ioniza-tion processes at the terminator. Subsequent ground-based high-resolution studies have reported additional metal detections in WASP-76b (e.g., Tabernero et al. 2021; Kesseli et al. 2022; Azevedo Silva et al. 2022), fur-ther motivating targeted NUV follow-up. Motivated by these results, we aimed to determine whether metals are present in WASP-76b’s NUV spectrum and if they are escaping. In this paper, we present a NUV transmission spec-trum of WASP-76b from HST (GO-16724, PI: L. Dos Santos). Our scientific objectives are to search for sig-natures of atmospheric escape and assess whether any outflow is consistent with hydrodynamic escape, eval-uate hydrodynamic models against the observed trans-mission spectrum, estimate the atmospheric mass-loss rate, and determine whether metals extend to the Roche lobe. The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 de-scribes the observations and data analysis; the results are shown in Section 3; Section 4 shows the modeling of the lower and upper atmosphere. A discussion and implications is provided in Section 5 and conclusions in Section 6.

1.4.5 which yielded a total of 28 sub-exposures for visit D and 35 sub-exposures for visit A. Afterwards, we used

We used the stissplice 1 software library to handle overlapping spectral orders. It first reads the 1D spec-trum data from FITS files and then identifies unique and overlapping sections of the spectrum. The library then merges these overlapping sections using a weighted averaging technique that accounts for signal-to-noise ra-tios (SNR) and data quality flags. Lastly, the unique and merged spectral components are concatenated to produce a comprehensive spliced spectrum. 2.2.2. Systematics correction 

Spectrophotometric light curves obtained with the STIS instrument on HST are significantly influenced by systematic effects related to the instrument. To han-dle systematics and light curve fitting, we follow cer-tain portions of the methodology described in Allen et al. These parameters are generated by the Engineering Data Processing Sys-tem (EDPS). Visit A is highly affected by systematic effects (see left panel of Figure 10 in Appendix A) and we do not include it on this part of the analysis whereas visit D has better quality, which is less affected by sys-tematics and is the one analyzed in this section. The raw, systematic-corrected light curves and best fitting models using the GP and linear models are shown in Figure 2. The systematics were fit using the eight de-trending vectors listed in Table 3, the derived coeffi-cients of the jitter parameters can be found in Table 8. The resulting Rp/R s for each model can be found in Ta-ble 4; we see that the values agree with each other within their respective uncertainties. Our new measurements of Rp/R s are consistent with the previously published value for WASP-76b ( Rp/R s = 0.1087 ±0.0005) from Fu et al. (2021). 3.2. Broadband Analysis

We see an increase in Rp/R s towards the shorter wavelengths for the linear model, however, with the relatively large uncertainties for each model, it is difficult to decisively conclude that there are absorbers at those wavelengths. 3.4. Single Line Analysis - Mg i, Mg ii , Fe i and Fe ii 

Due to our broadband and narrow-band searches not yielding significant detections of metal species absorp-tions – most likely because our expected signals are be-low our detection threshold given the low SNR – we carried out a single line analysis using visit A and visit D. In this approach, we compare in-transit and out-of-transit spectra specifically in the lines of interest, after transforming to the stellar rest-frame velocity scale as well as wavelength space (see Figure 5, Figure 11, Fig-ure 12, Figure 13). We start by exploring the Mg lines. Mg i has a cen-tral wavelength of 2852.965 ˚ A and the Mg ii doublet has central wavelengths at 2796.35 ˚ A and 2803.53 ˚ A. The Mg ii 2796.35 ˚ A transition is commonly denoted the k

line, and the 2803.53 ˚ A transition the h line; we adopt this notation throughout. We find no evidence for ab-sorption by Mg i (see Figure 11). In contrast, we detect and resolve both components of the Mg ii doublet. This is consistent with expectations: the first ionization po-tential (IP) of Mg is only ∼ 7.6 eV, meaning Mg is effi-ciently photoionized in the upper atmosphere of an UHJ, analogous to the behavior of carbon and other low-IP species (e.g., Garc´ ıa Mu˜ noz 2007). Mg ii is therefore the likely dominant ionization state at the altitudes probed by NUV resonance lines (see discussion in Section 5.3). The in-transit spectra show excess absorption relative to out-of-transit spectra in the blue wing of both lines, over Doppler velocities [-50, -10] km s −1 (see Figure 5; quantitative measurements are given below). The stel-lar flux approaches zero near the line center, so our data provide limited information at low velocities. We construct Mg ii light curves using the RWDDT Utils package 3, which is capable of integrating fluxes in fractional pixels and properly calculating uncertain-ties of STIS UV data using Poisson statistics. This is necessary when integrating fluxes at only a few pixels since the uncertainties are dominated by Poisson count-ing noise. The resulting light curves are shown in Fig-ure 6. Because these light curves trace resonance-line absorption in a line-dominated bandpass (and they are restricted in velocity space), we parameterize the signal using an effective radius ratio, Reff /R s, defined as the radius ratio of a gray transit model that reproduces the observed bandpass light-curve depth. DISCUSSION 5.1. Mg ii absorption: kinematics and observational sensitivity 

We detect significant excess absorption in the Mg ii 

doublet at 2796.35 ˚ A ( k) and 2803.53 ˚ A ( h) in the transmission spectrum of WASP-76b. Using the sin-gle line analysis, we measure an in-transit absorption at the blue wing of each line. We also recover consis-tent absorption using targeted 0.5 ˚ A spectrophotomet-ric bins. Parameterizing the line-band absorption depth with an effective radius ratio yields consistent values for both components across the two analyses: the single line analysis gives Reff (Mg ii , k) /R s = 0.3710 ± 0.0356 and 

Reff (Mg ii , h) /R s = 0.3849 ± 0.0326, while the targeted 0.5 ˚ A spectrophotometry yields Reff /R s ∼ 0.34 − 0.37 with larger uncertainties (Section 3.4.1). This detection indicates that Mg ii is present at high altitudes in the atmosphere of WASP-76b and can be probed with NUV spectroscopy. The interpretation of an absorption at negative ve-locities ([-50, -10] km s −1) in the stellar rest frame im-plies material with a line-of-sight motion towards the ob-server during transit. In this context, such blue-shifted absorption can arise from a combination of hydrody-namic expansion/escape (e.g., Yelle 2004; Murray-Clay et al. 2009; Owen 2019), strong winds near the termi-nator that project toward the observer (e.g., Showman & Guillot 2002; Ehrenreich et al. 2020; Seidel et al. 2025), radiative acceleration acting on resonance-line ions (e.g., Vidal-Madjar et al. 2003; Bourrier & Lecave-lier des Etangs 2013), interactions with the stellar wind (e.g., Bourrier et al. 2016; Carolan et al. 2021), and tidal deformation (e.g., Owen et al. 2023; Allart et al. 2025). While our current data does not constrain the geometry of the absorbing gas, the detection of Mg ii at velocities over [-50, -10] km s −1 suggests a fast, irradiated, and extended ionized layer in the upper atmosphere. Ionization/composition constraints from non-detections (Mg i, Fe i and Fe ii )

Combining the Mg ii detection with the lack of Mg i,qualitatively supports a highly ionized upper atmo-sphere in WASP-76b. UHJs undergo intense irradiation and photoionization is efficient at low pressures, mak-ing Mg ii a more sensitive tracer of the highest-altitude layers than neutral Mg i (e.g., Yelle 2004; Garc´ ıa Mu˜ noz 2007; Murray-Clay et al. 2009; Lothringer et al. 2018; Parmentier et al. 2018; Owen 2019). In this scenario, the non-detection of Mg i does not contradict the pres-ence of metals at high-altitude, rather, it supports that Mg predominantly ionized where the NUV resonance lines form. We do not detect absorption in the Fe i transitions nor in the Fe ii multiplets in the NUV. This could be a combination of limited sensitivity (low flux in the NUV and line blending) and atmospheric variability or spa-tial structure (e.g., Sing et al. 2019; Bourrier & Lecave-lier des Etangs 2013). More specifically, the optical Fe i

absorption in WASP-76b was reported to be phase de-pendent and asymmetric (Ehrenreich et al. 2020), po-tentially linked to condensation/rainout and strong dy-namics; if the similar behavior affects NUV iron lines, co-adding spectra across phases and visits could dilute a localized signal. The nature of a strong Mg ii detection with non-detections of Mg i and the iron lines is therefore con-sistent with a highly ionized and dynamically complex upper atmosphere where different species (and ioniza-tion stages) may trace different regions and/or velocities of the flow. We require additional NUV transits with higher SNR and phase-resolved analysis to place tighter constraints on neutral and ionized iron to test whether the Mg ii absorption exhibits ingress/egress asymmetries analogous to those seen in the optical (Ehrenreich et al. 2020). 5.4. Context of the observation and models 

A useful comparison for our NUV results is WASP-121b, which was observed with HST/STIS E230M over a comparable wavelength range and resolving power (Sing et al. 2019). That study reports a strong NUV tran-sit depth excess relative to the optical/near-IR contin-uum and detects spectrally resolved exospheric absorp-tion in both components of the Mg ii doublet (as well as Fe ii ), with Rp/R s exceeding the Roche-lobe scale in transit geometry. Recent 3D hydrodynamic simulations of WASP-121b by Wang et al. (2026) provide a phys-ical framework for interpreting such UV metal signa-tures, predicting that stellar irradiation can drive out-flows that are shaped into extended structures by orbital motion and stellar gravity. In these models, different tracers probe distinct regions of the flow and can exhibit line-of-sight velocities of order tens of km s −1, qualita-tively consistent with the high-velocity components re-ported in the transmission spectra of WASP-121b (e.g., Seidel et al. 2025). This highlights that UV observa-tions constrain not only composition but also the ge-ometry and kinematics of the upper atmosphere. For WASP-76b we also resolve both Mg ii components and infer effective radii that exceed the Roche-lobe bench-mark (Section 5.2), consistent with a highly extended ionized component. In contrast to WASP-121b, our E230M broadband transmission points show no statisti-cally significant wavelength-dependent variations at the achieved precision; the Mg ii signal in WASP-76b is most clearly detected as velocity-localized absorption in the line wings and recovered with targeted 0.5 ˚ A bins. To-gether, these results suggest that while Mg ii absorption at large effective radii is observed in multiple UHJs ac-cessible in the NUV, the detectability of a broadband NUV continuum excess and additional metal species (e.g. Fe ii ) may depend sensitively on system param-eters and data quality. Broadly placing WASP-76b alongside other hot Jupiters observed with STIS/E230M highlights both commonalities and diversity in NUV escape diagnos-tics. WASP-178b shows no clear Mg ii absorption in E230M (Lothringer et al. 2022), WASP-79b shows a non-detection on Mg ii (or any metals) (Gressier et al. 2023) while KELT-9b shows strong Mg ii and Fe ii sig-natures consistent with rapid mass-loss and substantial line broadening (Baldwin et al. 2026). In this emerg-ing E230M sample (Sing et al. 2019; Lothringer et al. 2022; Gressier et al. 2023; Baldwin et al. 2026), WASP-76b occupies an intermediate regime: we detect Mg ii in both doublet components but no Fe ii and do not mea-sure significant broadband NUV excess. Baldwin et al. (2026) proposed an empirical Mg ii Cosmic Shoreline in which the detection of escaping Mg ii is governed primar-ily by the planet’s escape velocity and the incident XUV flux: planets with high XUV irradiation and relatively low Vesc are most likely to show observable Mg ii out-flows. WASP-76b lies in the region of parameter space associated with Mg ii escape (above the non-detection 16 Ramos Rosado et al. 

of WASP-79b and below the extreme KELT-9b), so our detection provides an intermediate data point that helps refine the shoreline boundary between planets with and without Mg ii escape. From a modeling standpoint, our results emphasize that the STIS/E230M dataset constrains the upper and lower atmosphere in qualitatively different ways. The broadband E230M points carry limited information on continuum opacities because their uncertainties are large and because inter-instrument offsets absorb much of the leverage on the short-wavelength slope; consequently, adding E230M does not break the optical/IR degen-eracies in the lower-atmosphere retrieval (e.g., between metal opacity and SiO) and the inferred composition is still driven primarily by the optical+IR data. Consis-tently with this, our AIC-based model comparison shows that the broadband spectrum does not provide meaning-ful distinctions among the tested compositions (Table 6). By contrast, the spectrally resolved Mg ii measure-ment provides stronger discrimination within the same model grid because they require an extended Mg ii col-umn in the line wings. In our comparison, models with extreme Mg enhancement (10 × metallicity and 10 × so-lar Mg abundance) over-produce the absorption in the observable wings and are strongly disfavored by the Mg ii bins (∆AIC ≃ 16; p(M ) ≲ 0.01% within the tested model set), whereas models with moderate metallicities (0.1 × − 100 × solar) remain competitive (∆AIC ≲ 1–2; see Figure 7). Notably, this upper-atmosphere inference is broadly consistent with the near-solar metallicity in-ferred from optical+IR spectra (Fu et al. 2021): the Mg ii constraints do not require strong Mg enrichment or extreme bulk metallicities. While our data does not directly measure elemental abundances in the escaping flow, the fact that the Mg ii wing absorption can be re-produced without invoking extreme Mg enrichment sug-gests that a strongly Mg-fractionated outflow is not re-quired. This qualitative consistency is compatible with a hydrodynamic escape scenario in which the outflow is collisional over much of the upper atmosphere, so that heavy species can be dragged along with the bulk gas; however, ionization, condensation/rainout, and radia-tive forces can still modify the observable ionic compo-sition with altitude. While these 1D models capture the gross Mg ii absorp-tion level, interpreting the signal in terms of the spatial extent and mass loss ultimately requires modeling be-yond a gray-occultation picture. Resonance-line mea-surements are sensitive to the optical depth, covering fraction, and velocity field of the absorbing gas, and the stellar Mg ii line profile limits sensitivity near line cen-ter. Forward models that couple hydrodynamic struc-ture with ionization balance and radiative forces (and ultimately 3D geometry) are therefore needed to connect the observed Mg ii line profiles and light curves to phys-ical constraints on atmospheric escape and Roche-lobe scale structure (e.g., Bourrier & Lecavelier des Etangs 2013; Shaikhislamov et al. 2018; Carolan et al. 2021; Presa et al. 2024; Vidotto 2025). Upper-atmosphere models are also increasingly con-strained by combining multiple tracers. For WASP-76b, He i 10830 ˚ A measurements provide an upper limit (or weak/tentative constraint) on metastable helium ab-sorption and corresponding bounds on the mass-loss rate (Lamp´ on et al. 2023). Jointly interpreting He i and Mg ii is valuable because these diagnostics probe differ-ent thermochemical regimes and can help discriminate between models that reproduce a strong metal signature but over-predict the helium signal (or vice versa). In this sense, future multi-wavelength analyses that treat He i

and UV metal lines within a consistent escape frame-work will be particularly informative for constraining ˙M and the ionization structure of the outflow.  

> 6.

CONCLUSIONS We presented NUV observations of the UHJ WASP-76b obtained with HST/STIS E230M, covering 2275 −

3119 ˚ A. Using a spectrophotometric approach (broad-band ∼ 200 ˚ A bins) and a narrow-band of 4 ˚ A bins, we do not find strong evidence for metal absorption across the full wavelength range. In contrast, a single line analysis reveals significant excess absorption in the Mg ii doublet at 2796.35 ˚ A(k) and 2803.53 ˚ A ( h). The absorption is found at the blue wing over velocities [-50, -10] km s −1 and is detected in both components. Assisted by the veloc-ity localization, we also recover consistent absorption in targeted 0.5 ˚ A spectrophotometric bins centered on the wing. In both the velocity-window line light curves and the targeted 0.5 ˚ A bins, the inferred effective radii for each Mg ii component exceed the Roche-lobe scale (Reff /R s > RRL /R s), suggesting absorption from a highly extended Mg ii component consistent with Roche-lobe overflow. A robust demonstration of Roche-lobe overflow will require additional NUV transit observa-tions with improved phase sampling of the Mg ii absorp-tion (to test for early ingress/late egress) and forward modeling of the line profiles. We do not detect absorption in Mg i, Fe i, or Fe ii in the lines examined, which may reflect a combination of limited sensitivity in the NUV and a highly ionized, spatially/temporally structured upper atmosphere. We also performed preliminary broadband retrievals to test whether adding the STIS/E230M NUV points improves WASP-76b 17 constraints on SiO, motivated by the low-resolution de-generacy between metal opacity and SiO highlighted by Lothringer et al. (2022); at the achieved precision, including E230M does not significantly alter the opti-cal+IR posteriors and SiO remains only weakly con-strained. Using forward-model comparisons with the sunbather 

grid, we find that the broadband E230M spectrum does not meaningfully distinguish among the tested com-positions, with small AIC differences and broadly dis-tributed model probabilities within the model set. As a guide for the upper-atmosphere modeling, ATES esti-mates a mass-loss rate of ˙M ∼ 10 12 .14 g s −1 under our assumed high-energy irradiation proxy. In contrast, the targeted Mg ii bins provide substantially stronger dis-crimination: the extreme case of 10 × metallicity with 10 × solar Mg abundance is strongly disfavored while models without extreme Mg enhancement remain com-petitive. Taken together, the Mg ii observations favor a scenario in which the measured line-wing effective radii can be produced without requiring unusually large Mg abundances, instead pointing to high-altitude Mg ii dis-tributed over an extended path length. Together with the near-solar metallicity inferred at lower altitudes (Fu et al. 2021), the Mg ii constraints are qualitatively con-sistent with a hydrodynamic outflow that does not re-quire strong Mg enrichment and is well mixed. Finally, we emphasize that interpreting Mg ii in terms of physical extent and mass-loss rate requires sophis-ticated modeling that couples hydrodynamic structure, ionization balance, and radiative transfer; independent constraints such as those published He i 10830 ˚ A upper limits (Lamp´ on et al. 2023) provide valuable bounds on ˙M for such models. Looking forward from an observational perspective, expanding the number of UHJs with NUV constraints is essential for placing individual detections in a popula-tion context. Ongoing HST efforts (e.g., GO-18108; PI: Dos Santos) will increase the sample of UHJs observed in the UV and enable comparative tests of how irradi-ation environment and planetary parameters shape the detectability and kinematics of escaping metals. RAW DATA VISIT A AND D Visit A exhibits substantially larger visit-long and orbit-to-orbit flux variations than visit D (see Figure 10), including step-like orbit-to-orbit offsets consistent with changes in slit throughput. Visit A included an ACQ/PEAK 5 re-centering exposures, whereas visit D used an ACQ sequence without ACQ/PEAK. In particular ACQ/PEAK, re-centers the target in the slit and can introduce discontinuities in the throughput between HST orbits. Because these effects dominate the broadband continuum and are not robustly captured by our systematics model, we exclude visit A from the broadband and narrow-band analyses. We note that the velocity-resolved single line analysis is performed using relative in/out-of-transit spectra and is therefore less sensitive to absolute throughput offsets, allowing us to include visit A for line-profile comparisons. We do not recommend using acquisition strategies that can introduce orbit-to-orbit throughput offsets (e.g., ACQ/PEAK) for STIS/E230M time-series transit spectrophotometry, as these steps can dominate the broadband light curve and complicate systematics correction.
