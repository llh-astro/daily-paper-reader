# Do Better Scores Mean Better Physics? Physics-Grounded Explanations for Sim2Real Neural Operators ∗

Somyajit Chakraborty Xizhong Chen 

State Key Laboratory of Synergistic Chem-Bio Synthesis, Department of Chemical Engineering, School of Chemistry and Chemical Engineering, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai 200240, People’s Republic of China 

chksomyajit@sjtu.edu.cn , chenxizh@sjtu.edu.cn 

Abstract 

Machine-learning surrogates accelerate physical simulation, but lower predic-tion error need not coincide with lower error in physically relevant flow statis-tics. We examine this question for flow around a NACA4418 airfoil using paired computational-fluid-dynamics simulations and experimental particle-image-velocimetry measurements. A mean-preserving input intervention removes velocity fluctuations from selected regions of observed flow histories. Across four neural operators, removing fluctuations from the most energetic 10% of valid observed cells changes forecasts more than equal-area random removal. Because the masks are not matched for removed fluctuation energy, this contrast measures sensitivity, not independent evidence of physical importance. Separately, a CNO has lower velocity-field error but substantially higher two-component fluctuation-energy error than the reference on both analysis subsets. An output attenuation stress test also demonstrates disagreement between benchmark errors and domain-summed fluctu-ation energy. These single-benchmark results motivate reporting complementary physical diagnostics alongside aggregate prediction scores; they do not establish counterfactual physical correctness. 

1 Introduction 

Neural operators learn mappings between fields and are increasingly used as fast surrogates for parameterized PDEs and time-dependent physical systems [5, 6, 8]. In fluid mechanics, however, a small field error does not guarantee accurate fluctuation dynamics or coherent structures. Therefore, turbulence-oriented operator studies increasingly report flow statistics beyond pointwise errors [7, 11]. This motivates diagnostics that ask what physically changes when a score improves. The issue is especially important in Sim2Real transfer, where a surrogate is trained or adapted using simulation but ultimately judged against measurements. RealPDEBench provides broader paired simulation–measurement context [4], while the NeurIPS 2026 RealPDE Competition releases a NACA4418 paired PIV/CFD task for Sim2Real forecasting [13]. In parallel, perturbation-based XAI makes feature importance testable by altering selected inputs and measuring the model response, with matched random removal providing a useful faithfulness control [2, 3]. Beyond predictive accuracy, scientific machine learning requires evaluating whether models preserve meaningful phys-ical relationships. A physically trustworthy surrogate should not only reproduce future states, but should respond consistently when physically meaningful components of the input are modified. This

We ask two connected questions: how do forecasts respond to interventions in energetic input regions, and can improved field accuracy coexist with worse fluctuation-energy diagnostics? First, we compare mean-preserving removal of temporal velocity fluctuations in high-energy regions with equal-area random and low-energy controls. Second, we quantify this response across mask fractions and four tested architectures: a hybrid reference, FNO [6], CNO [10], and Transolver [12]. Third, we document disagreements between velocity-field and fluctuation-energy metrics. The goal is a diagnostic protocol, not a new architecture or a demonstration of causal physical importance. 

2 Data, models, and diagnostic protocol 

Dataset and models. We use the Sim-to-Real Transfer Learning task of the NeurIPS 2026 RealPDE Competition [13]. The release contains paired time-resolved PIV measurements and matched CFD simulations around a NACA4418 airfoil, with angles of attack 0◦–20 ◦, Reynolds numbers 2968– 27975, 100 paired trajectories, roughly 600 time steps per trajectory, and 64 ×128 released fields. CFD provides u, v, p ; PIV provides measured u, v . The scorer used here retains velocity after 2× spatial subsampling, giving 32 × 64 . Each sample maps 20 history frames to 20 future frames. We analyze 184 windows (historically labelled “selection”) and another 768 windows (“calibration”). These labels denote analysis bookkeeping, not independent model selection and validation: windows do not overlap, but both subsets draw from the same 23 experimental trajectories and operating-condition support. They provide within-trajectory replication rather than out-of-trajectory generalization. Our conclusions concern those 23 trajectories, not all 100 released trajectories. Statistical intervals use 5,000 percentile bootstrap replicates over complete trajectory IDs. The reference is a compact hybrid Fourier–wavelet operator with local convolution, temporal attention, source adaptation, and residual forecasting components; related Fourier–wavelet CFD-surrogate design is described in [1]. Here “reference” means the chosen comparison checkpoint, not a physics-ground-truth model. To test architecture dependence, we repeat the counterfactual on released real-finetuned FNO, CNO, and Transolver baselines under identical windows, masks, and controls. 4 Discussion and limitations 

These tests demonstrate intervention sensitivity and disagreement among velocity- and fluctuation-energy metrics, not counterfactual physical correctness. Equal-area masks remove unequal fluctuation energy, and hard boundaries may introduce spectral artifacts. The evidence covers one NACA4418 system and overlapping trajectory support; attenuation gains were tuned on both subsets. Appendix H details these limits and future controls. 5Acknowledgements

E Counterfactual and runtime provenance 

The counterfactual masks are constructed exclusively from the observed 20-frame PIV history; no future target enters mask selection. Masks are restricted to valid observed fluid cells and have equal area within each window, with ten deterministic random masks averaged for the random control. The realized 10% energetic-mask fractions are 0.09973 on selection and 0.09979 on calibration. Reference counterfactual and unperturbed predictions use the same frozen checkpoint/runtime, with window/target correspondence verified against frozen publication bundles. For the cross-model audit, FNO, CNO, and Transolver are loaded with the starting-kit normalization and output post-processing used for their real-finetuned checkpoints. Measured-channel cache parity was checked before perturbation. Mean absolute u, v discrepancies were approximately 1.7 × 10 −5 for FNO, 

2.0 × 10 −5 for CNO, and 1.4 × 10 −5 for Transolver, with maxima below 3.3 × 10 −4.

G Computational environment and intervention provenance 

Counterfactual inference and publication analyses ran on a Dell PowerEdge R730 with Ubuntu 24.04.3 LTS, two Intel Xeon E5-2682 v4 CPUs (32 physical cores/64 threads), 251 GiB RAM, and one NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB GPU. The environment used Python 3.10.20, PyTorch 2.2.2 with CUDA 12.1, NumPy 1.26.4, and cuDNN 8.9.2. The decoder-variant checkpoint (internal identifier: v2A) was evaluated with its corresponding model definition under strict state-dictionary loading. Its role is limited to a within-lineage intervention; no general claim is made about horizon-conditioned decoders. The fixed attenuation gains (gu, g v ) = (0 .975 , 0.935) were selected from a sweep that used both analysis subsets. The energy-ratio, signed-bias, spatial-correlation, pooled-TKE, and target-top-decile diagnostics were computed post hoc and were not objectives of that sweep. 

H Interpretive boundaries and tests needed for stronger physical claims 

The input intervention removes temporal velocity fluctuations while holding each masked cell’s observed temporal mean fixed. For a mask Ω, the removed fluctuation-energy mass is 

Eremoved (Ω) = X

> (x,y )∈Ω

khist (x, y ). (5) The equal-area energetic and random masks are not equal-energy controls: high-energy mask con-struction typically makes Eremoved larger in the targeted intervention. Accordingly, the reported energetic-minus-random contrasts should be read as responses to different, physically defined input modifications, not as magnitude-adjusted feature importance. An energy-matched control or an analysis normalized by removed input energy would be needed to separate these explanations; neither result is reported here. The masked histories are diagnostic inputs, not solutions of the governing equations or physically veri-fied flow interventions. Their interfaces introduce spatial discontinuities that can interact with spectral operators. Smooth transition masks, solver-generated counterfactuals, and comparison with actual experimental consequences would be required before asserting physically faithful counterfactual dynamics. This study has no counterfactual future ground truth and makes no such assertion. Finally, velocity-field error, two-component resolved fluctuation-energy error, spatial TKE correlation, regional TKE MSE, and domain-summed fluctuation-energy ratio each measure different aspects of forecast agreement with available PIV observations. A disagreement between these quantities is evidence that a scalar benchmark score provides an incomplete account of the measured flow statistics; it does not by itself determine which model would perform better for a downstream physical application. The four surrogates, shared trajectory support, and attenuation tuned on both subsets constrain wider generalization. Energy-matched and smooth-mask tests, solver-validated interventions, additional physical systems and trajectory-disjoint evaluation, and other training families (including generative approaches) are promising next steps.
