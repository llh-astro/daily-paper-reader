# Rigorous and effective numerics for Liverani-Saussol-Vaienti maps 

## Alexey Korepanov ∗ YuTong Wei † Caroline Wormell ‡

## 1 October 2026 

Abstract 

We make highly accurate numerical estimates of basic properties of the invariant measure for Liverani-Saussol-Vaienti intermittent maps, an archetypal slowly mixing dynamical system. We solve the challenge of precise and efficient estimation for a map with non-uniform expansion, where the transfer operator does not have a spectral gap. We do this using an Abel function, which “solves” the neutral dynamics, and which we can compute accurately via an asymptotic expansion. Our work covers both finite and sigma-finite invariant measure cases. In particular, we obtain the first practical estimates for the parameter far from zero, including the sigma-finite case. This opens the door to much deeper numerical study of intermittent dynamics than was previously possible. A driving motivation for this work is the upcoming proof of optimal rates of memory loss which works equally for finite and infinite invariant measure cases. No prior results on rates of memory loss for dynamical systems with infinite invariant measure exist, with a single exception of a recent paper by I.Chevyrev and A.K. on null recurrent Markov chains. 

# 1 Introduction 

Let γ > 0 and T : (0 , 1] → (0 , 1] be the Liverani-Saussol-Vaienti (LSV) intermittent map [LSV99]: 

T (x) = 

(

x(1 + 2 γ xγ ), x ∈ (0 , 1/2] 2x − 1, x ∈ (1 /2, 1] (1.1) The interest in this transformation comes from physics [PM80], where it serves as a simple model for intermittent transitions between laminar flow and turbulence. As perhaps the most basic chaotic dy-namical system with nonuniform expansion, it has been studied with great care. We have at our disposal fundamental results such as decay of correlations [Y99, G04b], invariance principles [MN05, CDKM20], concentration inequalities [CCRV09, CG12, GM14], linear response [BT16, K16, BS16], a dynamical version of renewal theory [S02, G04a, MT12, T15, T16], and much more. For each γ > 0, this system admits a unique (up to scaling) absolutely continuous finite or sigma-finite invariant measure. When γ < 1 this measure is finite, and when γ ≥ 1 it is infinite. The density of this measure, which we denote h(x), is smooth away from 0 (see [BK24, Remark 1.4]), and near 0 behaves asymptotically roughly as x−γ . In the setting of these maps, no closed forms are known for the invariant measure or most other related statistical quantities. The goal of this paper is a rigorous and accurate numerical estimation of h. This is delicate yet rela-tively straightforward for uniformly expanding maps [L01, GN14, W19, BS20], but is highly challenging for intermittent maps due to the nonuniform expansion near 0. Some papers attempted to approximate the transfer operator of the induced map (i.e. the first return map to [1 /2, 1] ), running into the issue that this operator is an infinite, slowly-decaying sum over large iterates of the map T , very slow to compute naively. See [M10, FMS11, BBD15, NT23].

Our approach takes three key steps. First, we conjugate the left branch of T to a suitable Abel function 

A (so that T n(x) = A−1(A(x) − n)), for which we have a quickly converging asymptotic expansion. Thus we can compute orbits near zero. Second, working with the analytic continuation of A, we employ the Euler-Maclaurin formula to accurately compute the action of the transfer operator (as in [VW25]). Finally, we approximate the transfer operator by a finite matrix acting on finitely many Chebyshev polynomials. In an appropriate Hardy space, the approximation is very tight, and we approximate h by the principal eigenvector of this matrix. An earlier version of some of these steps appeared in the PhD thesis of C.Wormell [W20]. The computational effort of our method grows polynomially with the number of digits of accuracy. Furthermore, the effort required using our methods does not vary massively with γ: the web app given below allows computation of properties of the map for a very wide range of γ values. This contrasts with previous results, where the effort grew exponentially with the number of digits, and reasonable estimates of statistical properties were in practice restricted to γ ≲ 14 .A special application and motivation for our work is memory loss : the speed at which the dynamical system forgets its initial state. It is measured as the rate of decay of (T n)∗(μ − ν) in total variation, where μ and ν are sufficiently regular probability measures and (T n)∗ is the push-forward operator. This is a well studied subject [AHNTV15, BBR19, KL21], yet at the moment of writing this manuscript, the literature has no results for rates of memory loss in ergodic theory with infinite invariant measure (when 

γ ≥ 1 in our case). In the simpler setting of Markov chains there is only the recent paper [CK25]. We verify numerically, for a small interval of values of γ at a time, that h′/h and h′′ /h are monotone on (0 , 1] . This is very complex and technical, but it allows a relatively simple proof of memory loss with optimal rates, independent of whether the invariant measure is finite or infinite. The memory loss itself is a subject of our follow-up paper, in preparation. Finally, we note that Abel functions have been underutilised in studying intermittent maps [P92, R99], our study suggests their great utility in understanding fine properties of the dynamical system. For example, it is an immediate byproduct of our work that the invariant densities are analytic:

The paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we introduce and approximate the Abel function; the main result is Theorem 2.1. In Section 3 we estimate the sums that appear in the expression for the induced transfer operator using the Euler-Maclaurin formula. In Section 4 we relate a finite dimensional approximation of the induced transfer operator to the true one, and approximate the invariant density 

h(x) near [1 /2, 1] by a finite sum of Chebyshev polynomials; the main result is Theorem 4.18. Section 5 contains technical bounds for our key applications: approximation of average return time to [1 /2, 1] and Lyapunov exponent. In Section 6 we extend the estimation of the invariant density to the full interval 

(0 , 1] . In Section 7 we set up the verification that h′/h and h′′ /h are monotone, the conditions needed for the proof of memory loss. The Appendix collects basic auxiliary proofs.
