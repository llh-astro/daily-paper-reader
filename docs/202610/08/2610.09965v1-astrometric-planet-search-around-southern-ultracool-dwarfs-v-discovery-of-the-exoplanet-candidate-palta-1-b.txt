ABSTRACT 

We report the discovery of PALTA-1 b, a sub-Jupiter mass exoplanet candidate orbiting the nearby early-L dwarf DENIS-P J1253108 −570924 (DE1253 −57) located at a distance of ∼17 pc. The exoplanet was discovered by monitoring the L dwarf’s as-trometric motion with FORS2 at the Very Large Telescope over more than eight years and characterising the deviations from the expected single-source motion. The estimated planet mass is between 0.54 MJup and 0.77 MJup , depending on the uncertain mass of the ultracool dwarf host. The system’s mass ratio is about 0.010, and the relative separation is estimated at 1.6–3.4 au. If confirmed, PALTA-1 b would be one of the very few known examples of a giant planet orbiting a nearby ultracool dwarf. This discovery finalises a larger astrometric planet search survey that targeted 20 nearby ultracool dwarfs and previously discovered three new ultracool binary systems. 

Key words. astrometry – technique: high angular resolution – brown dwarfs – planets and satellites: detection – binaries: general 

1. Introduction 

Low-mass main-sequence stars of spectral type M and L, that is, M and L dwarfs, are abundant in the solar neighbourhood and across the Milky Way. At their low-mass limit they, together with high-mass brown dwarfs, form a group of objects collectively referred to as ultracool dwarfs (UCDs). In 2010, we initiated an astrometric search for exoplanets around 20 nearby UCDs, a project nicknamed ‘Planets Around L-dwarfs wiTh Astrometry‘ (PALTA, Sahlmann et al. 2014). Since then, exoplanet searches around UCDs have been conducted with various observing tech-niques and have delivered fascinating results, for example, the compact TRAPPIST-1 system hosting several terrestrial planets (host-star spectral type M8 V, Gillon et al. 2016) and the system of temperate Earth-mass planets around Teegarden’s star (host-star spectral type M7 V, Zechmeister et al. 2019), and the insight that they can indeed host massive giant planets (e.g. Jung et al. 2018; Morales et al. 2019; Curiel et al. 2020). Here we report the astrometric discovery of the sub-Jupiter-mass exoplanet candidate PALTA-1 b orbiting an early-L dwarf in a ∼9 yr orbit, which is a rare example of a giant planet around a UCD and one of the first astrometric exoplanet discoveries with ground-based observations. Data acquisition and analysis are presented in Sect. 2. The system’s orbit and component masses are determined in Sect. 3, where we also summarise the overall results of the PALTA survey.

2. Analysis 

2.1. FORS2 imaging data reduction 

The UCD DENIS-P J1253108 −570924 (hereafter DE1253 −57 or PALTA-1) has a spectral type of L0.5 (Phan-Bao et al. 2008) and was observed as part of our long-term astrometric pro-gramme on the Very Large Telescope (VLT), aimed at the de-tection of exoplanets. Observations were obtained from 2011 to 2019 with the FOcal Reducer and low-dispersion Spectrograph (FORS) 2 camera (Appenzeller et al. 1998) in imaging mode with the I-band filter. We obtained 28 observational epochs with a total of about 1100 individual exposures. The charge-coupled device (CCD) images obtained were calibrated and reduced as described by Lazorenko et al. (2009, 2014). The profiles of the stellar point spread functions (PSF), which have a rather complicated structure, were approximated with a sum of three elliptical components, using for the next re-duction the most informative component centred at xi, yi and modelled by 2D Gaussians with standard deviation parameters 

σx, σy, and correlation ρ. The positions xi, yi related to the i-th exposure represent the photocentre used for astrometric re-duction (Lazorenko et al. 2014), while the other three auxiliary parameters provide useful information on whether the source is a partially resolved visual binary and are the key param-eters of the astrometry of unresolved images (AUI) analysis (Lazorenko et al. 2025). Because the PSF profiles of the CCD images are represented by simple 2D elliptic models, they are easily processed in various coordinate system transformations necessary for the image calibration and, finally, for extracting information on the possible binary geometry of stellar objects (Sect. 2.5).

2.2. Astrometric reduction 

The astrometric reduction of the observations was performed with methods proposed by Lazorenko & Lazorenko (2004) that ensure precision of about 0.1–0.2 mas per epoch, primarily by mitigating the atmospheric image motion. These methods have previously been applied successfully to the study of sev-eral ultracool binary systems (Sahlmann et al. 2013, 2015a,b, 2021; Lazorenko & Sahlmann 2018). A detailed description of the method was presented in Lazorenko et al. (2014). The astrometric reduction is performed using approximate CCD positions x0, y0 of a dense grid of reference stars and as-suming that they form a flat, non-distorted reference coordinate system. The coordinate axes are assumed to be oriented along the right ascension (RA) and declination (Dec) axes. The refer-ence areas are circular and centred on the target. The measured displacements xi − x0 and yi − y0 of photocentre positions of each star within the reference field are modelled by functions related to two independent sub-spaces: of the CCD x, y coordinates, and of time t. These displacements are fit by the model 

Fα(xi, yi) + ψα(t) + ∆ α = xi − x0

Fδ(xi, yi) + ψδ(t) + ∆ δ = yi − y0. (1) Here, the functions F(xi, yi) model the sum of geometric distor-tion and atmospheric image motion in each frame measured rel-ative to the reference frame, while the change in positions with time t due to proper motion, parallax, chromatic e ff ects, and zero points of positions are included in the function ψ(t). The terms 

∆α, ∆δ are the residuals of the fit. The functions F(xi, yi) are full bivariate polynomial func-tions of maximum power n and include ( n + 1)( n + 2) /2 free fit coe ffi cients formed on the positions of reference stars in the reference epoch. The value of n is defined as n = k/2 − 1 us-ing astrometric reduction mode k, which is an arbitrary even in-teger. The primary purpose of the reduction is to eliminate the most important low-frequency components of the image motion spectrum that are removed until the first active k mode of phase distortion (Lazorenko et al. 2009). We applied reduction with six modes k, from six to 16, which corresponds to n in the range of two to seven. In order to ensure the best balance between ref-erence frame noise and atmospheric image motion, the circular diameters of the reference frames were adjusted to increase from 70 to 194 arcsec with k increasing from six to 16. The model Eq. (1) is solved for each k separately with a least-squares fit to all measurements of reference stars in all exposures and using weights that depend on the star brightness. Therefore, the fit residuals ∆ of the solution for reference stars are orthog-onal both to the basic bivariate functions in x0, y0 space and to the time-dependent functions. The partial results (proper motion, parallax, fit residuals) obtained from individual k modes are av-eraged to yield the final results. The model takes into account some minor e ff ects, such as the relative displacement of the detector chips as described in Lazorenko et al. (2014). Also, using the absolute positions of 

Gaia DR1 (Lindegren et al. 2016) and later refined with Gaia 

DR2 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2018), we derived and then took into account the di ff erence in the pixel scale along the FORS CCD x, y axes and the inclination of the lower chip of the detec-tor relative to the upper chip (Lazorenko & Sahlmann 2017).

2.5. Verification of the brown dwarf binary system configuration 

Taking into consideration the astrometric evidence (Sect. 2) for the non-single nature of PALTA-1, we considered the possible configurations of the system that depend on the relative bright-nesses IB and IA of the secondary (B) and primary (A) com-ponents, respectively. Those can be either an ultracool dwarf

orbited by an exoplanet with IB ≪ IA, or a pair of ultracool dwarfs with 0 < IB < IA. Both configurations may result in the same photocentre orbit solution in Sect. 3, but distinguishing be-tween them is critically important for the mass estimation of the components. We used the AUI technique (Lazorenko et al. 2025) which allowed us to resolve the tight visual binary DE1756 −45 with a separation of 0.15 arcsec, much below the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of our FORS2 CCD images. We adapted this method to determine a detection limit for the pres-ence of a second component in the PSF.

The key assumption of the AUI method is that the measured PSF of field stars, despite having a rather complicated profile, can be approximated to the photon-noise level by ellipses elon-gated by optical distortions, atmospheric turbulence, and other eff ects. Due to this simple geometry of the PSF profile and by applying a sequence of coordinate system rotations and specific calibrations (Lazorenko et al. 2025), the real PSF of any single star can be mapped to an idealised circular PSF. The calibration aims at removing optical deformations and obtaining round im-ages with λ = σ, which can be achieved when averaging over a sample of reference field stars. For a single source located at the centre of the reference star group, we expect λ ≈ σ and u ≈ 1 to be as expected for a circular image. For binary objects, the presence of the secondary light source B at an angular separation R from the primary A distorts the combined image shape, leading to image elongation. Therefore, di ff erences in the calibrated idealised PSF shapes between the target object and the reference field stars can reveal a faint com-panion. For R ≤ σ there is a dependence 

u = 1 + cσ−2R2 + υ (6) between u and the angular separation R between the components of the binary (Lazorenko et al. 2025). Here, the quantity υ is a noise component, and the c term is a function of the luminosity ratio 

z = IB/IA (7) of the A and B components. For equal luminosity components we have c = 0.125 and c is decreasing towards zero for fainter and fainter companions. For intermediate cases with 0 .4 < z <

0.8, the approximate expression c = 0.1194 − 0.123(1 − z)4 is valid. The AUI method therefore consists of the conversion of the measured Gaussian parameters σx, σy, and ρ into the parameters 

λ, σ, and θ of the ellipse. This is followed by a calibration aimed at reducing the systematic distortion of the PSF, the computa-tion of the elongations u for the target object and, finally, fitting these data with Eq. (6) to obtain an estimate of the angular sepa-ration R. In this procedure, the value of z must either be known independently or estimated otherwise. The AUI method is capa-ble of resolving very tight companions in CCD images, and here we will use it to reject the binary brown dwarf hypothesis for DE1253 −57. 

2.5.2. Noise in the PSF images 

Any instantaneous PSF is slightly distorted due to unaccounted systematic optical distortions and fast anisotropic variations of the turbulent atmosphere, which both vary over the field and therefore are not fully removed by calibration. In addition, the knowledge of distortion is limited by measurement errors. These eff ects introduce random components ∆σx, ∆σy, and ∆ρ into the 2D Gaussian parameters of the target PSF whose expected true values (free of these random components) are σx,0, σy,0, and ρ0.After calibration, we expect to obtain a circular idealised PSF for a single object with σx,0 = σy,0, ρ0 = 0, and thus u = 1. The measured parameters σx = σx,0 + ∆ σx, σy = σy,0 + ∆ σy, and ρ =

ρ0 + ∆ ρ, naturally, deviate randomly from the expected values. Even a truly circular PSF would be deformed by instantaneous perturbations and becomes an ellipse with λ > σ and thus u > 1. For this reason, the measured u values are noisy, which adds some uncertainty in the interpretation of the measurements and in the classification of the object as a binary or a single star. While for a strong signal u ≫ 1 (large R) the residuals υ

in Eq. (6) are distributed approximately normally, with a stan-dard deviation ε around its expectation value of 1 + cσ−2R2, for weak elongations the distribution of υ is significantly di ff erent. Because random negative excursions are forbidden due to the implicit requirement of u > 1, in this case the noise has a one-sided distribution with negative deviations mirrored to positive values. Therefore, it can be expressed as 

υ = u0 + υ′, (8) where u0 is the (positive) expectation value of a one-sided distri-bution of υ and υ′ are the remnants of this representation. Also, we can assume that u0 ≈ ε. For single stars with actually zero 

u − 1 signal, the model Eq. (6) for the measured elongations is modified to 

u = 1 + u0 + υ′. (9) In intermediate cases, with non-zero but weak u − 1 signal when the noise dominates, it is di ffi cult to di ff erentiate between the PSF corresponding to a single star or to a binary. After calibrating the measured PSF, using five field stars of com-parable brightness within 200 pixels of PALTA-1, we computed the elongations u for the science object and for the comparison star. Figure 4 shows the distributions of these elongations u as a function of σ and FWHM. These distributions are very sim-ilar for the field star and for PALTA-1. Any signal, if present, would be weak, thus the noise of u values is better represented by Eq. We detected a small systematic excess ∆u0 when comparing the u0 value of PALTA-1 with that of the comparison star. For seeing between 0.4 and 0.6 arcsec, this excess was found to vary between 0 .001 and 0 .002 with an uncertainty of ∼ 50 %. We did not find any dependency of ∆u0 on σ as would be expected from Eq. If we assume that PALTA-1 is a binary system of two UCDs of comparable brightness, then, according to Eq. (6), the mea-sured values of u should contain a component that decreases with σ like the function shown by a dashed line in Fig. 4. This line corresponds to the signal induced in u that is compatible with the astrometric orbit parameters derived in Sect. 3 in the scenario where DE1253 −57 is an ultracool binary. It is clear that this dashed line does not match our observations, which allows us to reject the ultracool binary scenario with high confidence. By fitting the u values measured in ∼ 700 images with FWHM < 0.7 arcsec using Eq. (6) and the representation Eq. (8) for υ, we found that for a binary system with equal-luminosity components, or c = 0.125, the separation R cannot exceed 

Rmax = 0.023 arcsec at 3-sigma confidence. This is in agree-ment with Lazorenko et al. (2025), who found that the smallest detectable separation Rmax resolved with a few hundred FORS2 images is about 0.03 arcsec. In Fig. 4, the corresponding signal is u − 1 − u0 = 0.004 at 0.4 arcsec seeing. The dependence of the elongation u on seeing leads to the conclusion that PALTA-1 is either a very tight binary with R <

0.023 arcsec (a scenario rejected in Sect. 3.1.2) or a UCD with an exoplanet companion. In both cases, its PSF is indistinguishable from that of a single comparison star. Because this conclusion is of critical importance for this study, we confirm it in the follow-ing section. 

> 2.5.4. Distributions of the PSF parameters

The noise in the images a ff ects the shape of the calibrated PSF, inevitably transforming any circular image into an ellipse. When due to noise, ∆σx > ∆σy is registered in the measured PSF of a single object with σx,0 = σy,0, then the result is an elonga-tion of the PSF profile along the X axis, which corresponds to a position angle of θ = 0 of the semi-major axis. In the opposite case, the ellipse is elongated along the Y axis, corresponding to 

θ = 90 ◦. Thus, the 2D distribution of the parameters u and θ for a single star is represented by two peaks, one near θ = 0 and one at θ = 90 ◦. At a 50% occurrence rate of ∆σx > ∆σy, the number of events in each of these peaks is equal. Fluctuations in the correlation parameter ρ will smear these peaks out. How-ever, the distribution of errors in the PSF parameters can deviate from the normal distribution. As a result, the occurrence rate of 

∆σx > ∆σy may be above or below 50%, thus the number of events in the peaks are expected to be slightly unequal. Figure 5 displays the result for a simulated single object with a circular PSF, which has two peaks near 0 ◦and 90 ◦, as expected. The 2D distributions are shown for both our target and simulated data. The agreement between both distributions is good and is also seen in Fig. 5,b where we compare these distributions for PALTA-1 and for the comparison star. That means that PALTA-1 is either a single object or a binary with an undetectable faint companion, thus very small R or small z.The distributions in Fig. 5,a,b are shown for illustrative pur-poses. > 3.1.3. Orbit detectability with Gaia

PALTA-1 was observed by Gaia and the orbital motion discov-ered with FORS2 could in principle be detected with Gaia as-trometry as deviations from the standard single-source model. PALTA-1 is catalogued in Gaia DR3 (Gaia Collaboration et al. 2016, 2023b) as source Gaia DR3 6060561079334177664, with a mean magnitude of G ≈ 18 .2 mag. It has a standard six-parameter astrometric solution (Lindegren et al. 2021) whose proper motions and parallax are in broad agreement with our initial determination by Sahlmann et al. (2014). For Gaia DR3, PALTA-1 was observed by Gaia between 25 July 2014 and 28 May 2017. Assuming that our FORS2 proper motions are unbiased, a linear fit limited to the FORS2 orbit segment during that timespan should correspond to the dif-ference between the Gaia and FORS2 proper motions. This is because the orbital period is about three times longer than the 

Gaia DR3 timespan. Following this computation (cf. Fig. 7), we expect that the Gaia proper motions are more negative by 0 .172 mas yr −1 in right ascension and by 0 .293 mas yr −1

in declination. This corresponds well, at least qualitatively, to the parameters given in Table 1 but given the associated un-certainties, it should be considered marginal. Other possible non-single star indicators published in Gaia DR3, for exam-ple, RUWE =1.097 as an indicator for unresolved binarity or the ipd_gof_harmonic_amplitude =0.0095 as an indicator of par-tially resolved binarity, are inconspicuous and suggest that this source appears as being single in Gaia DR3. To confirm this, we simulated the Gaia astrometric time se-ries by using the PALTA-1 sampling and scan angles returned by the Gaia observation forecast tool ( GOST 1) and injecting the astrometric motion corresponding to our best-fit orbital model,

In summary, we found that the Gaia DR3 results are com-patible with the properties of the PALTA-1 system as determined with FORS2 and that we will have to wait for Gaia DR5 to inde-pendently confirm the astrometric orbit caused by the exoplanet candidate PALTA-1 b.

> 3.2.1. High-precision astrometry

The PALTA survey was made possible by the advances in high-precision astrometry that can be achieved with seeing-limited imaging observations with very large telescopes (Lazorenko & Lazorenko 2004; Lazorenko et al. 2009, 2014). For the first time, astrometric accuracies of 0.1 mas were achieved for a survey programme (Sahlmann et al. 2014). This exceptional performance allowed us to determine extremely pre-cise parallaxes of ultracool dwarfs (Sahlmann et al. 2014), probe the astrometric precision of Gaia DR1 (Lazorenko & Sahlmann 2017), characterise the mechanical stability of the CCD mo-saic of the FORS2 instrument camera (Lazorenko & Sahlmann 2019), and predict a performance limit of 5 μas for seeing-limited observations with extremely large telescopes (Lazorenko et al. 2024).

> 3.2.4. Extrasolar planets around ultracool dwarfs

Initially, the PALTA survey established that super-Jupiter exo-planets are rare around ultracool dwarfs. Specifically, we derived planet exclusion limits that set an upper limit of 9% on the oc-currence of giant planets with masses > 5 MJup in intermediate-separation (0.010.8 au) orbits around M8L2 dwarfs. In this work, we announce the discovery of PALTA-1 b, a sub-Jupiter exoplanet candidate with a period of ≳9 years around the L0 dwarf DE1253 −57 at a distance of ∼17 pc. 4. Conclusions 

We presented the discovery of the exoplanet candidate PALTA-1 b in a ∼9-year orbit around the L0 dwarf DE1253 −57 /PALTA-1 based on long-term astrometric monitoring with FORS2 /VLT. The astrometric orbit of the photocentre allows for two pos-sible scenarios: a binary system composed of two near-equal brightness ultracool dwarfs or a single ultracool dwarf orbited by a much fainter exoplanet. Using detailed modelling of the ob-served optical images of DE1253 −57, we could reject the former scenario, which leads to the discovery of the exoplanet candi-date PALTA-1 b. The estimated planet mass is between 0.54 MJup 

and 0.77 MJup , depending on the uncertain mass of the ultracool dwarf host. The system mass ratio is about 0.010, and the rel-ative separation is 1.6–3.4 au, which corresponds to 0.08–0.20 arcsec. PALTA-1 b is announced as a candidate primarily because of the uncertainties in the orbital solution due to the incomplete orbit coverage. Independent confirmation may prove challeng-ing because of the faintness of the ultracool dwarf host. The expected radial-velocity amplitude, for example, is ∼50 m s−1.High-contrast imaging could be another avenue towards con-firmation, but it would have to be su ffi ciently performant to detect a sub-Jupiter exoplanet at a spatial separation of 0.08– 0.20 arcsec from an L0 dwarf.
