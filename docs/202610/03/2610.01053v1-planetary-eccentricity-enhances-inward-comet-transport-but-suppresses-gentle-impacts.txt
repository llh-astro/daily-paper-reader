ABSTRACT 

Comets can supply volatile and prebiotic material to rocky planets, but survival of fragile molecules depends on atmospheric entry and impact. Compact planet chains can transfer comets inward at low velocity. We test whether modest planetary eccentricity preserves this route. We follow 64,000 comets from the region between the two outermost planets of an eight-planet chain to an Earth-mass planet on a temperate orbit around a 0 .1M ⊙ star. A second suite of simulations isolates the last transfer to the innermost planet across initial planet eccentricities 𝑒 p,0 = 0–0.05 and separations of 10 and 30 mutual Hill radii; a Δ = 12 comparison tests the influence of the near-3:2 period ratio at Δ = 10. At 𝑒 p,0 = 0.05, the fraction of comets intercepted by the outermost planet falls by 11 per cent and 6 per cent more reach the inner region. The innermost planet’s impact probability changes by less than 3 per cent, while the fraction of impacts below 15 km s −1 falls from 25 to 18 per cent. The fraction of all injected comets producing impacts below 15 km s −1 consequently decreases by 30 per cent. Measurements of the comet–planet speed before the final close encounter show that eccentricity preferentially removes the slowest approaches. Applying an impact-chemistry model gives a 31 per cent lower HCN survival-equivalent yield. Modest planetary eccentricity can therefore increase inward transport while reducing the prebiotic material delivered under chemically favourable impact conditions. 

Key words: astrobiology – celestial mechanics – comets: general – planets and satellites: dynamical evolution and stability – planets and satellites: terrestrial planets – methods: numerical 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Material formed beyond the snow line can supplement the volatile and organic inventories of rocky planets after their assembly. Dynamical models of the Solar system place water-rich planetesimals in several outer reservoirs and show that planetary growth and scattering can transport a fraction of them into the terrestrial region (Morbidelli et al. 2000; Raymond & Izidoro 2017). Cometary nuclei also retain a chemically diverse inventory, including hydrogen cyanide (HCN), simple organics and larger carbon- and nitrogen-bearing species (Mumma & Charnley 2011; Altwegg et al. 2017). These materials connect planet formation to surface environments in which prebiotic chemistry may proceed. The chemical value of an impact depends on its physical condi-tions. HCN is a feedstock for cyanosulfidic reaction networks that produce precursors of nucleotides, amino acids and lipids (Patel et al. 2015). Wet–dry cycling can concentrate delivered material and promote subsequent polymerisation (Pearce et al. 2017). Impact heating, however, destroys HCN increasingly efficiently as velocity and incidence angle rise (Todd & Öberg 2020; McDonald et al. 2025). Atmospheric passage introduces an additional size-dependent filter before an impactor reaches the surface (Anslow et al. 2025). An impact count therefore does not determine the amount of chemically useful material delivered to a planet. A chain of planets can transport small bodies from a cold outer

During scattering by one planet on a circular orbit, approximate conservation of the Tisserand parameter restricts the particle’s accessible orbits (Murray & Dermott 1999; Bonsor & Wyatt 2012). Successive encounters with neighbouring planets reset that constraint and allow the pericentre to move inward in stages. Low-mass planets are less efficient ejectors, and close spacing increases the overlap between adjacent scattering zones (Wyatt et al. 2017). Numerical calculations consequently find strong links between planet mass and spacing, inward flux, ejection and planetary accretion (Marino et al. 2018). Architectures that maximise inner-system arrival can have lower accretion efficiencies because fast passage through a tightly packed chain reduces the time available for collision. Impact velocity adds another dimension to this transport problem. A compact circular chain can pass a comet inward while preserving similar orbital velocity vectors through successive planet-to-planet transfers, producing a low-speed tail at the innermost planet (Anslow et al. 2023). This channel is especially consequential around low-mass stars. Their temperate orbits lie close to the star, so the local Keplerian speed is high; a small loss of velocity coherence can move an impact out of the narrow regime favourable to molecular survival. Compact terrestrial systems around very low-mass stars demonstrate that the required planetary multiplicity exists in nature (Gillon et al. 2017), and the regular sizes and spacings of many Kepler systems motivate an equal-mass, mutual-Hill-spaced model as a controlled dynamical laboratory (Weiss et al. 2018). Circular orbits provide the most coherent possible planet-to-planet transfer. Observed compact systems are dynamically cold, yet eccen-

3 RESULTS 3.1 Transport through the planet chain 

Table 2 shows how the fate of the injected population changes between circular and eccentric chains. In the circular systems, 34.1 per cent of comets strike P8. At 𝑒 p,0 = 0.05 this fraction falls to 30.4 per cent, an 11 per cent relative reduction. Impacts on P2–P6 rise from 25.2 to 26.5 per cent, and the surviving population at 3000 outer periods rises from 11.3 to 14.1 per cent. Ejection remains uncommon, increasing from 0.18 to 0.25 per cent; no comet strikes the star. The change at the outer edge permits more trajectories to penetrate the chain. The fraction entering the inner region increases from 3.56 to 3.78 per cent, a 6.3 per cent relative increase. Paired trajectory histories identify the origin of much of this gain: among comets that reach the inner region only in the eccentric calculation, 77 per cent strike P6–P8 in the paired circular calculation. Eccentricity weakens interception during the first stages of transfer and opens additional paths to smaller radii. The fraction striking P1 is nearly unchanged: 0.409 per cent in the circular chain and 0.400 per cent at 𝑒 p,0 = 0.05. The enhanced flux into the inner region therefore does not produce a corresponding increase in the innermost planet’s total impact rate.

4 DISCUSSION 4.1 Why inward transport and gentle delivery diverge 

The simulations separate two consequences of planetary eccentricity. At the outer edge, eccentric planets sample a wider range of radii and velocity directions during each orbit. Comets that would meet P8 in the circular geometry can pass through its encounter zone, increasing the population available to middle planets and the number that reaches the inner region. This redistribution resembles the broader result from planet-chain scattering studies that inner-system flux, planetary accretion and survival are distinct outcomes of the same transport process (Marino et al. 2018). Near P1, a gentle impact requires close alignment of the comet and planet velocity vectors. The circular compact chain repeatedly selects this geometry because successive transfers require modest changes in orbit. Planetary eccentricity adds radial motion and changes the tangential speed at a given orbital radius. It also changes which comet trajectories survive the preceding planets. The low-speed part of the Hill-entry distribution is depleted, and gravitational focusing then adds the same Earth-like escape-speed scale to both eccentricity conditions. The total P1 impact probability remains stable because the supply of inward-moving comets has increased, while their velocity distribution has become less coherent. This explains the factorisation in equation (6). Transport quantity rises, collision efficiency after arrival declines slightly, and impact quality shows the largest change. A flux measured at an inner boundary therefore cannot be converted directly into a prebiotic delivery rate without the subsequent encounter and collision velocities. 

4.2 Compact systems with modest eccentricity 

The tested eccentricities occupy the range inferred for many compact multi-planet systems. Transit-timing analyses find typical eccentrici-ties of a few per cent (Hadden & Lithwick 2017), while transit-duration studies find a broader but still dynamically cold distribution for multi-transiting systems (Van Eylen et al. 2019). In this range, eccentricity need not destabilise the chain during the delivery episode. It can still alter the relative eccentricity vectors that control close encoun-ters, consistent with the stability behaviour found for tightly packed eccentric systems (Gratia & Lissauer 2021). The effect is amplified by the orbital scale of late M-dwarf temperate zones. At 𝑣 K ≃ 50 km s −1, 𝑒 = 0.03 corresponds to 1 .5 km s −1

of epicyclic motion. The analytic circular transfer at Δ = 10 has 

𝑣 ∞ ≃ 3.3 km s −1, so an eccentric contribution of this size changes the lowest-speed encounters appreciably. The controlled grid begins to show a clear displacement near 𝑒 p,0 = 0.03, and the Δ = 12 experiment gives the same response at a second period ratio. Planet count and mutual-Hill spacing remain important predictors of inward scattering (Bonsor & Wyatt 2012; Wyatt et al. 2017). The present results add the instantaneous comet–planet eccentricity-vector difference as a local predictor of the final encounter speed. Architectures with the same spacing can deliver different low-speed fractions when their eccentric motions differ.

4.3 Consequences for prebiotic delivery 

The HCN calculation translates the dynamical shift into a chemical quantity. Equation (11) weights every P1 impact by the survival fraction predicted from its speed and angle for a 1-km-diameter comet. The circular and eccentric chains have nearly identical numbers of P1 impacts, yet 𝑌 HCN decreases from 3 .53 × 10 −4 to 2 .43 × 10 −4

surviving-comet equivalents per injected comet. The central reduction is 31 per cent and follows the loss of slow, favourable-angle events. HCN is relevant to several prebiotic pathways because it can be stored in ferrocyanide salts and later feed the synthesis of nucleobases, amino acids and lipid precursors (Patel et al. 2015; McDonald et al. 2025). The result links planetary architecture to the chemical inventory available at a rocky surface: a system that delivers more icy bodies inward can still supply less intact feedstock through impacts. Around low-mass stars, where close-in temperate planets already face high characteristic impact speeds, modest eccentricity further narrows the set of chemically favourable events. Orbital dynamics therefore contributes to the boundary conditions for habitability through the amount and state of material delivered, alongside stellar radiation, atmospheric evolution and surface geochemistry (Rimmer et al. 2018). 

4.4 Limitations and future work 

The comet population is introduced on transfer orbits between the two outermost planets. A debris belt, resonances and any planets exterior to the model determine how frequently such orbits are supplied and their distribution in Tisserand parameter, inclination and phase. A self-consistent outer reservoir is the next step for converting the conditional yields measured here into rates for individual systems. 5 CONCLUSIONS 

We investigated how modest planet eccentricity changes comet de-livery through a compact chain extending from a volatile-rich outer region to an Earth-mass planet on a temperate M-dwarf orbit. Paired end-to-end integrations measured the final yield, and controlled final-transfer calculations located the velocity mechanism. At 𝑒 p,0 = 0.05, early interception by the outermost planet decreases and the inner-region arrival probability rises by 6.3 per cent. The P1 impact probability changes by less than 3 per cent. The fraction of those impacts below 15 km s −1 falls from 25.2 to 18.0 per cent, reducing the end-to-end gentle-impact yield by 30.3 per cent in the central estimate. The slow encounters are removed at the Hill-sphere boundary.
