ABSTRACT Disintegrating rocky planets provide a rare opportunity to probe material escaping directly from intensely irradiated planetary surfaces. We present time-resolved, low-resolution ( R ≃ 600) optical spectroscopy and complementary ground-based photometry of BD+05 4868 Ab. The in-transit spectra exhibit an additional unresolved absorption feature near the O i triplet at 7771.94, 7774.17, and 7775.39 ˚ A relative to the out-of-transit baseline. The feature has an equivalent width of Wλ ≃ 1.4 ± 0.2 ˚ A, a maximum differential depth of approximately 9%, and an apparent width of 13–16 ˚ A, comparable to the instrumental resolution. We interpret the feature as candidate O i absorption from an oxygen-bearing mineral-vapor outflow. The 3 s 5S◦

lower level of the triplet lies 9.146 eV above the ground state and cannot be appreciably populated in LTE at the reported planetary temperature, requiring non-LTE excitation. A one-zone forward model in which photoionization, O +

The model favors an atomic component with a projected extent larger than the narrow condensed-dust layer and permits gas-to-dust mass-loss ratios of order a few for representative ionizing irradiation. Stellar O i line-profile variations remain the principal alternative at the present spectral resolution. We therefore identify escaping O i as the preferred physical interpretation while retaining the designation candidate O i. High-resolution, phase-resolved spectroscopy can directly test this interpretation by resolving the individual triplet components.

1. INTRODUCTION Rocky planets on ultra-short-period orbits can be heated to temperatures at which silicate material va-porizes directly from their surfaces. For sufficiently low-mass planets, this mineral vapor can escape, expand, and partially condense into dust, producing the highly variable and asymmetric transit profiles characteristic of disintegrating rocky planets (S. Rappaport et al. 2012; D. Perez-Becker & E. Chiang 2013; R. van Lieshout & S. A. Rappaport 2018; R. A. Booth et al. 2023). Systems such as KIC 12557548 b (Kepler-1520 b), KOI-2700 b, and K2-22 b demonstrate that the observed occultation is often dominated by extended dusty material rather than by a compact planetary disk (S. Rappaport et al. 2012, 2014; R. Sanchis-Ojeda et al. 2015). Photometric variability, wavelength-dependent extinction, and tail morphology have consequently provided important con-straints on grain sizes, dust dynamics, and mass loss in

these systems (M. Brogi et al. 2012; B. Croll et al. 2014; J. J. Bochinski et al. 2015; R. van Lieshout et al. 2014, 2016; A. R. Ridden-Harper et al. 2018). The escaping material is not expected to consist of dust alone. Thermochemical calculations of volatile-poor silicate vapor predict gas-phase species including Na, O 2, O, SiO, Mg, Fe, and related molecules and ions, with the composition depending on temperature, bulk composition, fractional vaporization, and condensation (L. Schaefer & B. J. Fegley 2009; Y. Miguel et al. 2011; L. Schaefer et al. 2012; E. S. Kite et al. 2016; G. C. C. Costa et al. 2017). Hydrodynamic calculations likewise show that irradiated mineral outflows can contain neu-tral atoms, ions, and free electrons while grains form farther from the source (Y. Ito & M. Ikoma 2021; R. A. Booth et al. 2023; B. Campos Estrada et al. 2024). Gas and dust may therefore represent different phases of the same escaping rock-vapor flow. Oxygen is particularly important because it is a ma-jor constituent of silicate material and is expected to remain abundant in rocky vapor. Detectable O i 7774 ˚ A absorption requires a non-LTE population mechanism, with photoionization followed by O + recombination and radiative cascade providing a natural pathway into the excited quintet system. BD+05 4868 Ab is particularly favorable for investi-gating an atomic component of a rocky-planet outflow. The planet orbits a bright K dwarf with a period of 1.271869 d and exhibits strongly variable occultations with depths of approximately 0.8–2.0% (M. Hon et al. 2025). Modeling of the system reveals both leading and trailing dust tails, characteristic grain sizes of approx-imately 1–10 μm, and a dust mass-loss rate of order ˙Mdust ∼ 2 × 10 12 g s −1. The trailing component is sub-stantially more extended than the leading tail, demon-strating that a large column of freshly supplied rocky material persists around the planet. Radial-velocity ob-servations constrain the planetary mass to Mp < 6.2 M⊕

at 5 σ; the much smaller ∼ 0.02 M⊕ value used in dust-tail simulations is a fiducial model assumption rather than a measured mass (M. Hon et al. 2025). Recent observations demonstrate that disintegrating planets can carry compositional information in both their condensed and gaseous components. JWST mid-infrared observations of K2-22 b constrain the miner-alogy of its escaping dust and reveal spectral struc-ture potentially associated with gas-phase material (N. Tusay et al. 2025). Ground-based spectroscopy has also searched for atomic species such as Na and Ca in disin-tegrating systems (E. Gaidos et al. 2019; A. R. Ridden-Harper et al. 2019). Optical atomic spectroscopy there-fore provides a complementary route to probing the gas produced alongside the dust. In this work, we present time-resolved, low-resolution optical spectroscopy and complementary ground-based photometry of BD+05 4868 Ab. We identify a transit-correlated absorption feature near the unresolved O i

7774 ˚ A triplet and adopt excited neutral oxygen as the working physical interpretation of the feature. At the 

R ≃ 600 resolution of the ALPY 600 spectrograph, the individual triplet components cannot be separated, so the present data do not by themselves provide a unique atomic identification. Nevertheless, the wavelength co-incidence, transit dependence, and expected oxygen-rich mineral vapor motivate a quantitative O i interpreta-tion. We test this interpretation with a one-zone NLTE for-ward model in which stellar photoionization supplies O +

and recombination with radiative cascade populates the 3s 5S◦ lower level. The model reproduces the observed scale of the equivalent width, line depth, and instrumen-tally broadened profile for an oxygen-bearing gas compo-nent extending beyond the narrow condensed-dust layer. We therefore treat the feature throughout this work as candidate O i absorption from an escaping mineral-vapor component, while retaining stellar and terrestrial effects as observational alternatives that require higher-resolution spectroscopy to separate definitively. The telescope operates at f / 6.8 and was equipped with a Shelyak ALPY 600 low-resolution slit spectrograph. Assignment to the in-transit and out-of-transit sam-ples was determined from orbital phase rather than from the appearance of the spectrum near 7774 ˚ A. Expo-sures obtained during the predicted passage of the ex-tended occulting material across the stellar disk were as-signed to the in-transit sample, while observations suffi-ciently separated from the predicted occultation formed the out-of-transit baseline. The classification was there-fore fixed independently of whether a particular expo-sure displayed structure near the candidate wavelength. Individual spectra were retained only when they sat-isfied a common set of full-spectrum quality crite-ria. Exposures affected by substantially reduced con-tinuum signal-to-noise, severe guiding or slit-centering losses, anomalous broadband throughput, inadequate sky-background subtraction, unreliable wavelength reg-istration, or rapid transparency variations were ex-cluded. These criteria were applied without reference to the strength of the candidate 7774 ˚ A feature. 2.1.2. Spectroscopic Reduction and Atmospheric Correction 

Raw CCD frames were reduced using the Integrated Software for Imagers and Spectrometers (ISIS; C. Buil 2020). Processing included dark-current subtraction, flat-field correction, cosmic-ray rejection, background subtraction, and extraction of the one-dimensional stel-lar spectrum. Sky background was estimated from de-tector regions adjacent to the stellar trace, and the stel-lar spectrum was extracted using an optimal-extraction procedure (K. Horne 1986). Wavelength calibration was performed using Ne–Ar comparison-lamp spectra obtained during each observ-ing night. The resulting wavelength solutions had typi-cal root-mean-square residuals of approximately 0.3 ˚ A, substantially smaller than the instrumental resolution near 7774 ˚ A. A nearby comparison star observed at similar airmass and time was used to characterize large-scale changes in instrumental throughput and atmospheric transparency. 4A smooth relative-response correction was derived while excluding narrow stellar absorption structure from the response determination. In particular, the comparison spectrum was not used as a direct divisor in the O i

7774 ˚ A region, because late-type stellar spectra can themselves contain intrinsic O i absorption. Telluric absorption was treated on an exposure-by-exposure basis using a synthetic atmospheric-transmission model following the Molecfit methodology (A. Smette et al. 2015; W. Kausch et al. 2015). The at-mospheric solution included O 2 and H 2O and was con-strained using telluric-dominated regions near the O 2 Bband around 6870 ˚ A, H 2O structure near 7200–7350 ˚ A, and the O 2 A band near 7600–7700 ˚ A. Atmospheric wa-ter vapor was allowed to vary between exposures. The 7745–7800 ˚ A interval containing the candidate feature was excluded from determination of the at-mospheric solution. The high-resolution synthetic transmission spectrum was convolved to the effective ALPY 600 line-spread function before correction of the observed spectrum. Regions of very low atmospheric transmission or poorly corrected telluric structure were excluded from the differential analysis. Additive night-sky emission was treated separately from multiplicative telluric absorption. Sky spectra measured adjacent to the stellar trace were inspected in the vicinity of the terrestrial O i 7774 ˚ A airglow transition (A. B. Christensen 1975). Exposures show-ing anomalous residual background structure were re-jected using the same full-spectrum quality criteria ap-plied elsewhere in the reduction. The telluric and sky-background procedures were applied identically to the in-transit and out-of-transit samples. 2.1.3. Wavelength Registration and Resolution Matching 

Before stacking, all accepted spectra were placed on a common wavelength grid. Residual exposure-to-exposure wavelength offsets were measured from photo-spheric absorption features distributed across the spec-trum and outside the candidate O i interval. Each spec-trum was then interpolated onto the common reference grid. The 7774 ˚ A feature itself was not used to deter-mine the wavelength shifts. Small differences in seeing, guiding, and slit illumina-tion can also change the effective line-spread function between exposures. Because such differences can gener-ate structured residuals when otherwise identical stellar spectra are divided, the effective widths of photospheric absorption features outside the candidate region were compared between the in-transit and out-of-transit sam-ples. Where required, the spectrum with the narrower ef-fective line-spread function was broadened to match the broader spectrum. RESULTS 3.1. Transit-Correlated Absorption near 7774 ˚ A

The reduced ALPY 600 spectrum of BD+05 4868 A exhibits the broad continuum shape and blended pho-tospheric absorption structure expected for the K-type primary. A representative spectrum is compared with K- and M-dwarf templates in Figure 1. The target spec-trum is substantially more consistent with the K-dwarf template, supporting the conclusion that the primary dominates the observed optical spectrum. The mean in-transit and out-of-transit spectra are shown over the full usable 3800–7800 ˚A range in Figure 2. Across most of the spectrum, the two phase-selected stacks reproduce the same broad photo-spheric structure after wavelength registration, contin-uum treatment, and matching of their effective spec-tral resolution. The principal phase-dependent differ-ence occurs near the red end of the spectrum, where the in-transit stack contains an additional absorption-like depression near 7774 ˚ A. Figure 3 shows this region in greater detail. The addi-tional absorption is present in the in-transit stack near the laboratory wavelengths of the O i triplet at 7771.94, 7774.17, and 7775.39 ˚ A, while a comparable depression is not present in the out-of-transit stack. Because the complete triplet spans only 3.45 ˚ A, substantially less than the approximately 13 ˚ A instrumental resolution established in Section 2, the individual components can-not be distinguished. The observed feature is therefore treated as a single unresolved absorption feature near the O i 7774 ˚ A triplet. The differential transmission spectrum defined in Equation 6 reaches a maximum absorption depth of ap-proximately 9% in the candidate region. Its apparent width is approximately 13–16 ˚ A, consistent with an un-resolved feature dominated by the ALPY 600 line-spread function. The present data therefore constrain the inte-grated absorption strength but not the intrinsic velocity profile or the relative strengths of the three triplet com-ponents. 6

DISCUSSION The phase-dependent absorption near 7774 ˚ A is con-sistent in wavelength, strength, and instrumental width with the unresolved O i triplet. We therefore adopt ex-cited neutral oxygen in the escaping mineral-vapor flow as the working physical interpretation of the feature. The principal questions are whether sufficient oxygen is expected in the outflow, whether the excited lower state of the 7774 ˚ A transition can be populated under the con-ditions of BD+05 4868 Ab, and whether the observed absorption can be reproduced quantitatively without re-quiring extreme gas properties. 4.1. An Oxygen-Bearing Rock-Vapor Outflow 

An oxygen-bearing gaseous component is a natural consequence of rocky material being heated to vapor-ization temperatures. Thermochemical calculations for volatile-poor silicate material predict gas-phase O 2, O, SiO, Na, Mg, Fe, and related species, with the detailed abundances determined by bulk composition, tempera-ture, pressure, and fractional vaporization (L. Schaefer & B. J. Fegley 2009; L. Schaefer et al. 2012; G. C. C. Costa et al. 2017; Y. Ito & M. Ikoma 2021). The es-caping dust and gas should therefore be regarded as dif-ferent phases of the same mineral-vapor outflow rather than as physically unrelated components. The chemical pathway connecting oxygen-bearing va-por to excited neutral oxygen was summarized in Equa-tion 1. Stellar irradiation can photodissociate O 2, pho-toionize neutral oxygen, and maintain a modest popu-lation of O + that subsequently recombines into excited states of O i. Radiative cascades through the quintet system then provide a direct route to the 3 s 5S◦

lower level of the 7774 ˚ A triplet (I. Maurer & P. A. Mazzali 2010; S. N. Nahar 2022). The high material-loss rate of BD+05 4868 Ab strengthens the connection between the gaseous and condensed components of the outflow. M. Hon et al. (2025) infer a dust mass-loss rate of order 2 × 10 12 g s −1

and show that vaporization fluxes from rock-forming material can approach those required to supply the ob-served dusty effluent. The same mineral-vapor source capable of producing the dust can therefore naturally leave a residual gaseous component, including atomic 9

We constructed a one-zone NLTE forward model con-strained by the observed Wλ ≃ 1.4 ˚ A equivalent width. Starting from an assumed gas geometry and mass-loss rate, the calculation determines a characteristic gas den-sity and evaluates the O i/O ii ionization balance. Re-combination and radiative cascade then determine the population of the 3 s 5S◦ lower state, including the effect of trapping in the 1355 ˚ A transition. The model calcu-lates the full optical depths of all three O i 7774 ˚ A com-ponents, applies the projected stellar covering fraction, and finally convolves the resulting transmission spec-trum with the ALPY 600 instrumental response for di-rect comparison with the observations. As a representative solution, we adopt a projected atomic-gas half-height of hgas = 0 .25 R⋆, corresponding in the adopted strip geometry to a stellar covering frac-tion of Cf ≃ 0.315. Here, hgas describes the projected extent of the absorbing tail rather than a hydrostatic atmospheric scale height. We further adopt a representative electron temper-ature of Te = 7500 K and an ionizing luminosity of 

LEUV /L bol = 3 × 10 −6. The electron temperature de-scribes the tenuous irradiated gas and is distinct from the ∼ 1820 K characteristic temperature associated with the planetary surface. The adopted EUV luminosity is likewise a model assumption rather than a direct mea-surement of the host star. For these parameters, reproducing the measured equivalent width requires ˙Mgas / ˙Mdust ≃ 5.1. Combined with the dust mass-loss estimate ˙Mdust ∼ 2 × 10 12 g s −1

from M. Hon et al. (2025), the representative solution corresponds to ˙Mgas ∼ 1 × 10 13 g s −1. This value is a model-dependent requirement of the fiducial solution rather than a direct measurement of the gas mass-loss rate. The corresponding total oxygen column along the modeled line of sight is of order 2 × 10 20 cm −2, whereas only N (3 s 5S◦) ≃ 2.1 × 10 13 cm −2 is required in the excited lower level of the optical triplet. Thus, only ap-proximately 10 −7 of the total oxygen column—roughly one oxygen atom in 10 7—must occupy the 3 s 5S◦ state at a given time. The representative ionization balance remains predominantly neutral, with only of order sev-eral percent of the oxygen present as O ii . The required absorption therefore does not demand either a highly ionized oxygen tail or a large excited-state fraction. The weak 1355 ˚ A transition is itself optically thick in the representative solution, with τ1355 of order 10 2.Equation 11 then gives an escape probability of order 

βesc ∼ 10 −2. After convolution with the R ≃ 600 ALPY 600 in-strumental response, the same model produces an ap-parent differential depth of approximately 9.7% and a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of approximately 13.5 ˚ A, in close agreement with the observed scale of the unresolved feature. The FWHM is the total wavelength width of the absorption profile measured at one-half of its maximum depth. Its value is very close to the approx-imately 13 ˚ A instrumental resolution near 7774 ˚ A, show-ing that the modeled feature would appear essentially unresolved in the present spectra. BD+05 4868 Ab extends a small group of systems in which catastrophic rocky-planet mass loss can be stud-ied directly. Kepler-1520 b, KOI-2700 b, and K2-22 b ex-hibit the same broad phenomenology of variable transit depths and asymmetric occultations produced by escap-ing dusty material (S. Rappaport et al. 2012, 2014; R. Sanchis-Ojeda et al. 2015). Optical searches for atomic species such as Na i and Ca ii in these systems have generally resulted in upper limits rather than secure de-tections (E. Gaidos et al. 2019; A. R. Ridden-Harper et al. 2019). Infrared observations provide a complementary view of the condensed phase. JWST/MIRI spectroscopy of K2-22 b disfavors a featureless iron-dominated dust spectrum and is broadly consistent with silicate-rich ma-terial, with additional structure potentially associated with gaseous species (N. Tusay et al. 2025). Such obser-vations probe mineral and molecular signatures, whereas the 7774 ˚ A feature considered here probes a specific ex-cited state of atomic oxygen. If the O i interpretation is confirmed, BD+05 4868 Ab would therefore connect the condensed and atomic phases of a disintegrating rocky-planet outflow: silicate vapor supplies both the dust observed photometrically and the oxygen-bearing gas traced spectroscopically. The comparatively bright K-dwarf host and percent-level occultations make this system particularly favor-able for such phase-resolved spectroscopy (M. Hon et al. 2025). We do not require BD+05 4868 Ab to occupy a unique evolutionary stage relative to the other disintegrating systems. Differences in bulk composition, irradiation, planetary mass, condensation efficiency, gas-to-dust ra-tio, and stellar environment can all alter the observable 13 gas and dust signatures. The significance of the present candidate is instead that it provides a direct route toward linking atomic spectroscopy with the mineral-vapor process responsible for rocky planet disintegra-tion. 4.5. High-Resolution Test of the O i Interpretation 

The most direct test of the interpretation is phase-resolved high-resolution spectroscopy of the 7774 ˚Atriplet. At the ALPY 600 resolution, all three O i com-ponents and the stellar photospheric line are blended. Resolving the triplet would allow their relative depths, velocity structure, and orbital-phase dependence to be tested independently. An immediate archival opportunity is provided by the seven NEID spectra obtained by M. Hon et al. (2025) with the WIYN 3.5 m telescope. NEID covers the full triplet at R ∼ 120 ,000, corresponding near 7774 ˚ A to approximately 0 .065 ˚ A or 2 .5 km s −1 resolution. The individual components, separated by 2.23 and 1.22 ˚ A, are therefore fully resolved. The individual exposures rather than a phase-averaged stellar stack are required for this test. A plane-tary signal should vary with orbital phase, whereas pho-tospheric absorption remains tied to the stellar spec-trum, telluric absorption remains fixed in the terrestrial frame before barycentric correction, and airglow appears as narrow additive emission. A convincing confirmation would be the recovery of phase-correlated absorption in multiple components of the triplet with a common ve-locity behavior inconsistent with stellar center-to-limb variation or terrestrial contamination. Conversely, sufficiently sensitive high-resolution spec-tra showing no phase-dependent triplet absorption would place a strong limit on the excited O i column and challenge the planetary interpretation of the low-resolution feature. Dedicated spectroscopy obtained continuously before, during, and after a dust-tail tran-sit, together with simultaneous photometry, would pro-vide the strongest test because the dust-production rate of BD+05 4868 Ab varies substantially from epoch to epoch.

CONCLUSION We have presented time-resolved, low-resolution optical spectroscopy and complementary ground-based photometry of the disintegrating rocky planet BD+05 4868 Ab. The observations reveal a transit-correlated absorption feature near the unresolved O i

7774 ˚ A triplet. We interpret this feature as candidate absorption from neutral oxygen in an escaping mineral-vapor component and test that interpretation with a one-zone NLTE forward model. Our principal conclu-sions are as follows. 1. The in-transit spectrum contains additional ab-sorption near the O i triplet at 7771.94, 7774.17, and 7775.39 ˚ A relative to the out-of-transit base-line. At the ALPY 600 resolving power of R ≃

600, the triplet is unresolved within an approxi-mately 13 ˚ A instrumental profile. The differential feature has an equivalent width of approximately 

Wλ ≃ 1.4±0.2 ˚ A, a maximum differential depth of order 9%, and an apparent width of approximately 13–16 ˚ A. 2. An oxygen-bearing gaseous component is a natu-ral consequence of the rock-vapor outflow expected from BD+05 4868 Ab. Vaporization of silicate and oxide material can supply O 2, O, SiO, Mg, Fe, and other refractory species, linking the candidate atomic absorption to the same escaping material that ultimately produces the observed dust tail. 3. The O i 7774 ˚ A triplet requires non-LTE excitation under the conditions of this system. The 3 s 5S◦

> 2

lower level lies 9.146 eV above the ground state and cannot be appreciably populated thermally at the reported planetary temperature. Photoioniza-tion followed by O + recombination and radiative cascade provides a natural pathway into the ex-cited quintet system. 4. The NLTE forward model quantitatively repro-duces the observed absorption. For a representa-tive model adopting hgas = 0 .25 R⋆, Te = 7500 K, and LEUV /L bol = 3 × 10 −6, the projected geome-try gives Cf ≃ 0.315, and reproducing Wλ ≃ 1.4 ˚ Arequires ˙Mgas / ˙Mdust ≃ 5.1. After convolution with the R ≃ 600 ALPY 600 instrumental re-sponse, the same model produces an apparent differential depth of approximately 9.7% and a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of approx-imately 13.5 ˚ A, in close agreement with the ob-served scale of the unresolved feature. This width is comparable to the approximately 13 ˚ A instru-mental resolution near 7774 ˚ A, so the modeled fea-ture would remain unresolved in the present spec-tra. The agreement in equivalent width, depth, and instrumentally broadened width demonstrates that an oxygen-bearing NLTE outflow can quan-titatively reproduce the observed absorption. 5. The wavelength coincidence, transit dependence, rock-vapor chemistry, and quantitative NLTE 14 agreement together make escaping O i our pre-ferred interpretation of the 7774 ˚ A feature. Be-cause the present spectra do not resolve the triplet from the intrinsic stellar O i line, we retain the des-ignation “candidate O i.” Stellar center-to-limb and line-profile variations remain the most impor-tant alternative. High-resolution, phase-resolved spectroscopy capable of resolving multiple triplet components provides a direct test of the interpre-tation. The existing R ∼ 120 ,000 NEID spectra of BD+05 4868 A provide a particularly valuable oppor-tunity for such a test. Confirmation of phase-dependent absorption in multiple O i triplet components would es-tablish an atomic-gas counterpart to the dusty effluent and open a complementary route to studying the com-position and physical evolution of disintegrating rocky planets. BD+05 4868 Ab is therefore a promising system for connecting the mineral vapor, atomic gas, and con-densed dust phases of catastrophic rocky-planet mass loss.
