## 摘要
Ariel 的 Tier 3 观测策略为在光谱和时间域中探索行星与恒星可变性提供了独特机会。然而，若没有合适的工具包，宿主恒星的时间可变性可能会使不同历元获得的观测的联合分析复杂化。我们研究了恒星可变性对 Ariel Tier 3 观测的影响，并建立了三种物理复杂度递增的缓解方法。利用对一个受不同黑子和光斑构型污染的有利系统的模拟重复观测，我们比较了一种朴素的、未经修正的叠加方法、通过逐步去除受污染最强的光学波长来单独分析红外区域，以及使用恒星-行星联合反演校正单次观测。我们发现，在强恒星可变性下，叠加不一致的观测可能在反演得到的行星性质中产生显著偏差，尽管单次活动校正仍能相当好地恢复行星参数。应用渐进式光学数据剔除可在一定程度上显著提高反演准确度，无需任何反演内校正，且仅造成适度的精度损失。在叠加前校正单次观测会产生更一致的光谱和通常更小的不确定性，但需要更大的计算投入。这些结果表明，在解释重复的 Ariel 观测时应考虑恒星可变性，但不必因此排除对活跃系统的大气表征。一套灵活的缓解方法，辅以预备性和同期恒星监测，可使恒星可变性的处理适应各个目标，同时保持最大化 Ariel 科学回报所需的效率。

## Abstract
Ariel's Tier 3 observing strategy offers a unique opportunity to explore planetary and stellar variability in the spectral and time domain. However, without the correct toolkit, temporal variability of the host star may complicate combined analysesof observations obtained at different epochs. We investigate the impact of stellar variability on Ariel Tier 3 observations and establish three different mitigation approaches of increasing physical complexity. Using simulated repeated observations of a favourable system contaminated by different spot and facula configurations, we compare a naive, unmodified stacking approach, separate analysis of the infrared region through progressive removal of the most strongly contaminated optical wavelengths, and the correction of individual visits using combined star-planet retrievals. We find that stacking inconsistent visits can produce substantial biases in the retrieved planetary properties under strong stellar variability, although a single activity correction can still recover the planetary parameters reasonably well. Applying progressive optical data cuts substantially improves the accuracy of retrievals up to a point, without requiring any in-retrieval correction and causing only a modest loss of precision. Correcting individual visits prior to stacking produces more consistent spectra and generally smaller uncertainties, but requires greater computational effort. These results demonstrate that stellar variability should be considered when interpreting repeated Ariel observations, but need not preclude atmospheric characterisation of active systems. A flexible suite of mitigation approaches, supported by preparatory and contemporaneous stellar monitoring, can allow the treatment of stellar variability to be adapted to individual targets while maintaining the efficiency required to maximise Ariel's scientific return.
